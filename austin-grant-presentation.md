## THE CRISIS — AUSTIN BY THE NUMBERS

### Housing
- **$435,000** median home price — only **2 of 75 ZIP codes** are affordable to a median-income family.
- A **48,000-unit shortage** for households earning less than 30% of Area Median Income (the regional household income figure used by federal housing programs to define affordability).
- More than **85%** of extremely low-income renters pay more than half their income on rent.
- Ranked **third nationally** for the worst home-value declines — market instability hurts every household, not just the one selling.
- Austin added **120,000 housing units** between 2015 and 2024 but still cannot keep up with demand for affordable housing.
- City development fees increased **$15,000 to $25,000** per home since 2020.

### Homelessness
- **3,238 people** counted in the 2025 federally required Point-in-Time count (up **36%** from 2023).
- An estimated **6,235 to 6,358** people experience homelessness on any given day.
- **68%** became homeless in Austin — these are neighbors, not transplants.
- **44%** are experiencing homelessness for the first time.
- **13%** are military veterans.
- Youth homelessness has **nearly quadrupled**: 247 in 2020 to **934** in 2024.
- **911 people experiencing homelessness** are in the Travis County Jail — 36% of the jail population.
- The city budget for homelessness response is **$30.3 million** — the official 10-year plan calls for it to **double to $60 million-plus** and requires **$350 million** in total investment.

### Workforce
- A family needs about **$95,000 a year** to afford Austin's median home. The actual median household income is **$80,954**.
- Without education or a credential beyond high school, Central Texans have only a **12% chance** of earning a living wage.
- The healthcare workforce is short thousands of unfilled positions across Central Texas.
- The **Interstate 35 corridor** carries more than **$10 billion** in active construction creating tens of thousands of jobs — but workers need both training **and** housing nearby.

### Health equity
- **Black homeownership** in Austin is about 30% versus 70% for white households — a 40-point gap.
- Hispanic families face about **2.3 times** the housing cost burden of white families.
- **46% of renters** across the metro pay more than 30% of their income for housing.
- Housing instability is the single largest non-medical factor that shapes health outcomes (what public-health researchers call a "social determinant of health").

## THE SOLUTION — THRIVEUP'S 20-PLATFORM ECOSYSTEM

### "We bring solutions to anyone by meeting them where they are with our comprehensive solutions and suite of tools. No problem remains a problem."

### Why ThriveUp is built for Austin

**The problem with how most services work today:**
Most organizations offer one thing — a job-training program, *or* a health clinic, *or* a housing referral. The person being served falls through the gaps between disconnected services. A veteran who needs mental-health support, job training, and housing has to do three separate intakes, sit on three separate waiting lists, and work with three systems that do not talk to each other.

**ThriveUp's answer:**
One connected ecosystem. **20 artificial-intelligence-powered platforms** working together, sharing data in real time, with Dr. Terry Flood's published methodologies at the core. One front door. Comprehensive wraparound support. No dead ends. Always a safety net.

## PFLUGERVILLE — THE UNTAPPED OPPORTUNITY

### Why Pflugerville matters
- **330 affordable housing units** are coming online (the Branchview Apartments, in 2027) — but with **zero matching social-support infrastructure**.
- Pflugerville is a federal Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) entitlement city — it receives federal housing dollars directly.
- The Pflugerville Community Development Corporation (PCDC) has more than **$150,000 in workforce-matching grants** available.
- Population is growing faster than community services.
- The **Downtown East** civic center (29 acres) opens in 2026 — perfect timing for service integration.

### ThriveUp fills the gap
The city is investing more than $80 million in physical infrastructure (water, roads, buildings) but is investing nothing in human-services infrastructure. ThriveUp brings:
- Workforce pathways for new residents.
- Health navigation for families in the new affordable units.
- Veteran services (Pflugerville has a significant military population near Fort Cavazos).
- Youth support through the Integrated Supports for Thriving Youth and WholeMind Learning platforms.
- Business development for local entrepreneurs through the Minority Center of Excellence.
