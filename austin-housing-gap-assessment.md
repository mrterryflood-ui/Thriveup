## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Austin–Pflugerville area faces a serious shortage of homes that working families, veterans, and people in historically underserved neighborhoods can afford. Median home prices sit at $429,000–$435,000 in Austin (only 2 of 75 ZIP codes are affordable to a median-income household) and around $390,000 in Pflugerville. The documented shortage is more than 48,000 homes for households earning less than 30% of Area Median Income (AMI is the regional household income figure that federal housing programs use to define affordability — for the Austin metro, 30% AMI is roughly $26,000 a year for a family of four). ThriveUp Academy's network of 19 connected community-support platforms is positioned to address housing stability through workforce training, health-care integration, and wraparound family services. Five active grant opportunities line up directly with this work.

## PART 1: AUSTIN HOUSING CRISIS — BY THE NUMBERS

### Why this matters
When working families cannot find a home they can afford near their jobs, schools, or family, the consequences ripple through the whole region: longer commutes, more strain on emergency rooms, higher demand on schools that absorb relocated children, and rising rates of homelessness. These are everyone's costs, not just the costs to the families directly affected.

### Market snapshot (2025–2026)
| Measure | Austin | Pflugerville |
|---|---|---|
| Median home price | $429,000–$435,000 | $390,000 |
| ZIP codes affordable to a median-income family | 2 of 75 | Limited |
| Shortage of homes for households below 30% Area Median Income | More than 48,000 | Part of the regional shortage |
| Homelessness (2024 federally required Point-in-Time count by the Ending Community Homelessness Coalition) | 3,785 people on a single night | Contributes to the regional total |
| Renters paying more than 30% of income on rent (cost-burdened) | 46% of all renters | About 40% of all renters |
| Market condition | Cooling but still unaffordable | Buyer's market (6.7 months of housing supply) |

### Who is most affected
- **Veterans:** About 45,000 veterans live in the Austin metro. Veteran homelessness rose 12% regionally. The federal Department of Housing and Urban Development's HUD-VASH housing voucher program (HUD-Veterans Affairs Supportive Housing) currently has waitlists of 18 months.
- **Black and Hispanic households:** The homeownership rate for Black households in Austin is about 30%, compared with about 70% for white households — a 40-point gap. Hispanic families face roughly 2.3 times the housing cost burden of white families.
- **Working families:** A family needs roughly $95,000 a year to afford the median Austin home. The actual median household income is $80,954.
- **Youth aging out of foster care:** More than 250 young people age out of the foster care system in Travis County each year, most without a housing plan.
- **People living with disabilities:** About 14% of Austin's residents live with a disability. Accessible, affordable units are nearly nonexistent.

## PART 2: PFLUGERVILLE — GROWTH WITHOUT THE COMMUNITY SUPPORTS

### Housing development pipeline
| Project | Units | Type | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branchview Apartments | 330 | Affordable, financed by Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC, the main federal tool for funding affordable rental housing) | Under construction; opens 2027 |
| Merle on Howard | 1,000+ | Market-rate apartments | Under construction |
| Carmel Community | 500 | Market-rate single-family homes | Final phase; sales spring 2026 |
| Limmer Square | Mixed-use | Market-rate mixed-use | Approved April 2025 |
| Downtown East (civic center) | Not housing | New City Hall and recreation campus | First phase opens by 2026 |

### Infrastructure investment
- More than $80 million in water and wastewater projects (2025–2026): a $53 million Colorado River raw-water line, a $27 million water-treatment-plant expansion, and $17 million in sewer-line work.
- **The gap:** Pflugerville is making major investments in physical infrastructure (pipes, roads, buildings), but virtually no investment in the community-support side — no workforce training tied to the new affordable units, no veteran services, no integrated health-and-housing supports, and no wraparound services for the families who will move into the 330 affordable apartments coming online.

### The opportunity
Pflugerville is building housing without building the community supports that make housing succeed. ThriveUp Academy fills that gap by bringing the human-services side of infrastructure: workforce pathways, health navigation, veteran support, and family stability services.

## PART 3: THE INTERSTATE 35 CORRIDOR — THE ECONOMIC ENGINE

The Interstate 35 expansion from Dallas through Austin to San Antonio is the largest highway construction program in Texas history.

| Segment | Investment | Timeline | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital Express Central (Austin) | $4.5 billion | 2024–2033 | Active construction |
| Capital Express North (Austin) | $606 million | 2023–2027 | Active |
| Capital Express South (Austin) | Multi-billion | Planning | Upcoming |
| North Expansion Central (San Antonio) | $1.5 billion+ | 2022–2027 | 60% complete |
| North Expansion South (San Antonio) | $700 million+ | Active | Phase 1 |
| Dallas–Fort Worth segments | $3 billion+ | Various | Active |

### What this means for workers and small businesses
- Tens of thousands of construction jobs along the corridor.
- Families displaced by construction need relocation help.
- Construction workers need housing near worksites.
- Small and minority-owned businesses are eligible for subcontracts through the federal Disadvantaged Business Enterprise (DBE) program (currently paused — see the Opportunity Pipeline brief for details).
- ThriveUp's Veteran-Owned Small Business (VOSB) certification (held through The Collaborative Advocate platform) makes it eligible for federal set-aside contracts.

- **The housing connection:** After mental-health diagnosis, housing instability is the strongest predictor of veteran suicide. Mission Transition, Shield Atlas, and SafeReport together create a safety net.
- **Austin data:** More than 45,000 veterans in the Austin metro; veteran homelessness up 12%.
