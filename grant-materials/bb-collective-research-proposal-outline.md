## 1. RESEARCH PROPOSAL

### Problem Statement

Black and Brown communities in Austin, Manor, and Pflugerville face compounding structural violence across multiple domains simultaneously — housing instability, healthcare access barriers, justice system involvement, employment discrimination, educational inequity, and mental health service deserts. These forms of structural violence are interconnected, yet the systems designed to address them operate in silos, creating fragmented service delivery that perpetuates harm.

Current interventions typically target single domains (housing OR employment OR mental health), failing to account for the cumulative, intersecting nature of structural violence. This fragmentation is itself a form of structural violence — communities must navigate dozens of disconnected systems while in crisis.

### Research Questions

1. **Primary:** How does access to an integrated, multi-domain community technology ecosystem affect exposure to and experiences of structural violence among Black and Brown residents in Central Texas?

2. **Secondary:**
   - What pathways do community members use when navigating interconnected services through a unified platform vs. fragmented traditional referral systems?
   - How do community-identified indicators of structural violence differ from institutionally-defined metrics?
   - What role does technology-mediated warm handoff (cross-platform referral with outcome tracking) play in reducing service fragmentation?
   - How do implementation science frameworks (RE-AIM, CFIR) apply to evaluating community technology interventions in structural violence contexts?

### Theoretical Framework

**Intersectionality + Structural Violence Theory + Implementation Science**

- **Structural Violence (Galtung/Farmer):** Violence built into social structures that prevents people from meeting basic needs — applied to analyze how system fragmentation compounds harm
- **Intersectionality (Crenshaw):** Multiple identity dimensions interact to create unique experiences of structural violence
- **RE-AIM Framework (Glasgow):** Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance — applied to evaluate the ecosystem intervention
- **CFIR (Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research):** Inner/outer setting, intervention characteristics, individual characteristics, implementation process — applied to understand adoption barriers and facilitators
- **Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR):** Community members as co-researchers, not subjects

### Methodology

**Design:** Mixed-methods community-engaged research

**Qualitative Component:**
- Semi-structured interviews with 25-30 community members across Austin, Manor, and Pflugerville who interact with ecosystem platforms
- Focus groups (4-6 groups of 6-8 participants) organized by community and primary service need
- Community mapping exercises to identify structural violence hotspots and service deserts
- Photovoice documentation by community participants

**Quantitative Component:**
- Analysis of de-identified ecosystem platform data: referral patterns, service utilization, cross-platform navigation paths, outcome measurements
- Pre/post surveys measuring perceived structural violence exposure (adapted from existing validated instruments)
- Social Determinants of Health (SDOH) data integration from CDC PLACES, Census ACS, and SAMHSA treatment locator
- Platform engagement metrics correlated with self-reported well-being indicators

**Community Engagement Strategy:**
- Community Advisory Board (CAB) of 8-10 members from target neighborhoods
- CAB involvement in research design, instrument development, data interpretation, and dissemination
- Community members trained as co-researchers for data collection
- Findings presented back to communities before academic publication
- All materials available in English and Spanish

### Innovation

This study is innovative because it:
1. **Examines technology as structural intervention** — not just a service delivery tool, but a mechanism for disrupting structural violence by connecting previously siloed systems
2. **Uses the ecosystem itself as a research instrument** — the 24-platform integrated system generates natural data about how communities navigate structural violence
3. **Applies implementation science to violence prevention** — RE-AIM and CFIR are rarely used in structural violence research
4. **Centers community definition of violence** — rather than imposing institutional metrics, this study surfaces how communities experience and name structural violence

### Potential Impact on Violence Prevention Practice and Policy

- **Practice:** Evidence base for integrated technology approaches to violence prevention; model for replication in other communities
- **Policy:** Data to support funding integrated multi-domain interventions rather than single-issue programs; evidence for technology investment in violence prevention infrastructure
- **Scholarship:** Contribution to structural violence literature incorporating technology-mediated intervention and implementation science frameworks
