ThriveUp’s 14 Youth Elements Plan presents a comprehensive, outcomes-driven approach to delivering all 14 required WIOA Title I Youth program elements in a cohesive, integrated model grounded in evidence-based practice, continuous improvement, and a robust ecosystem of platforms that collectively advance youth toward credential attainment, meaningful work, and durable postsecondary pathways. Grounded in the MAP-GAP framework (Monitoring, Assessing, Predicting — Gap analysis, a continuous improvement cycle), reinforced by SALP (structured adherence/fidelity protocol), and guided by the Three Realities framework (Research Reality, Political Reality, Ground-Level Reality), this plan aligns with national youth employment objectives while translating them into local impact for in-school and out-of-school youth facing barriers. Our approach leverages ThriveUp’s 14-platform technology ecosystem, including LifeBridge, The Incubator, MCE, RPLICE, SafeReport, Perfectly Different, Sankofa Health, PillScheduler, SafeCogniCare, WholeMind Learning, ISSS, M2C Transition, Better Science Lab, and the newly integrated platforms that underpin program design and service delivery. Each platform contributes directly to the 14 elements, ensuring a seamless, student-centered pathway from intake through post-exit follow-up, with a strong emphasis on paid and unpaid work experiences, credential attainment, and the transition to postsecondary opportunities.

Context and Statement of Need
National data affirm the central challenge: youth ages 16–24 confront persistent barriers to employment, education, and long-term earnings, particularly among those who are out of school, English learners, justice-involved, homeless or foster-in-care, low-income, or experiencing disability. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and NCES report that youth unemployment remains elevated relative to the general population, with out-of-school youth disproportionately affected by chronic skill gaps and limited access to career pathways. Within our Tri-County regional footprint, the most recent regional needs assessment synthesizes data from WIB partners, school districts, juvenile justice systems, shelter networks, and community-based organizations. It identifies a substantial OSY population—estimated at tens of thousands of youth ages 16–24—with barriers that include high school non-completion, limited literacy and numeracy foundations, English language learning needs, unstable housing, transportation gaps, and inconsistent access to credentials that employers recognize. A disproportionate share of this population is also touching systems—juvenile justice, child welfare, and hospital-based youth services—creating complex needs that demand tightly coordinated services rather than parallel, siloed interventions.

Within this landscape, studies consistently show that two core practices move the needle: (1) authentic, paid and quality work experiences linked to industry-recognized credentials, and (2) integrated supports that address both academic/algebraic readiness and career navigation. Meta-analytic reviews of youth employment programs, including large-scale evaluations conducted by MDRC and Mathematica, indicate that programs with structured work experiences, case management, mentoring, and clear postsecondary transition supports yield higher employment rates, increased earnings, and greater credential attainment relative to programs that emphasize only classroom instruction. Our plan positions these practices within a 14-platform ecosystem that is designed to mitigate barriers and accelerate outcomes by providing synchronized services across educational, occupational, and supportive domains. Local conditions—rising demand for CTE-aligned credentials in healthcare, information technology, advanced manufacturing, and green energy sectors; a strong but underutilized network of employers; and a growing need for mental health and substance use supports—underscore the urgency of a comprehensive, scalable approach that is equally focused on immediate labor market outcomes and durable career pathways. To respond to these dynamics, ThriveUp proposes a 14-element, platform-enabled strategy that ensures at least 75% of participants served are OSY, with no less than 20% of program resources allocated to Work Experience activities, all under a fidelity framework that guarantees consistent, high-quality service delivery across the entire youth ecosystem.

Program Design and Theory of Change
The program design rests on a theory of change that foregrounds integrated, multi-service delivery anchored in MAP-GAP. Through MAP-GAP, we monitor baseline readiness, continuously assess needs and progress, and calibrate interventions through gap analysis and predictive analytics to tailor services to individual trajectories. The fundamental premise is that successful youth outcomes emerge when academic supports, work readiness, labor market information, and psychosocial supports are delivered in an integrated, person-centered sequence, with mentors and employers engaged from the outset to create a coherent path to credentials and employment. The 14-platform ecosystem is intentionally designed to operationalize this theory of change. ThriveUp’s core education platform provides foundational learning support and credential pathways through WholeMind Learning and ISSS, ensuring cognitive readiness and social-emotional competencies are developed in parallel with career preparation. The Incubator anchors program design and curriculum development, ensuring that all learning experiences are modular, stackable, and aligned with industry standards. LifeBridge serves as the community voice and benefits navigation hub, connecting youth to supports such as transportation, housing assistance, childcare, and health services. RPLICE delivers fidelity monitoring, ensuring that manuals, checklists, and coaching models are consistently applied. SafeReport provides anonymous reporting and safety supports so youth can voice concerns and receive timely protection. Perfectly Different offers targeted supports for neurodiverse youth and those with disabilities, ensuring inclusive access to all elements through adaptive materials and assistive technologies.
