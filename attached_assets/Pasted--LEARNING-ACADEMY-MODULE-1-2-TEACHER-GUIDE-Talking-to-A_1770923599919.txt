## MODULE OVERVIEW

### Learning Objectives
By end of module, students will:
1. Apply 5 W's framework to create clear AI prompts
2. Explain "Garbage In, Garbage Out" principle with examples
3. Improve prompts by adding context and details iteratively
4. Demonstrate polite communication with AI consistently
5. Score "Proficient" or higher on module assessment

### Standards Alignment
- **CSTA:** 1A-AP-08, 1B-AP-11
- **ISTE:** 4.1.c, 6.2.d
- **CCSS ELA:** SL.3.3-5.3, W.3.2-5.2
- **CASEL:** Self-Management, Responsible Decision-Making

## LESSON 1: HOW TO TALK TO AI - THE 5 W'S

### At a Glance
**Time:** 20 minutes  
**Workbook:** Pages 8-9  
**Key Concept:** Using Who, What, When, Where, Why to create clear prompts

### Minute-by-Minute Lesson Plan

**MINUTES 0-3: Warm-Up**
- "Simon Says" with increasingly specific instructions
- Debrief: Which instruction was easiest to follow? Why?

**MINUTES 3-11: Direct Instruction**
- Introduce AI as "super-smart 12-year-old friend" metaphor
- Present 5 W's framework using anchor chart
- Demonstrate each W with before/after examples:
  - WHO: "presidents" → "presidents for 4th graders"
  - WHAT: "help with math" → "multiply two-digit numbers"
  - WHEN: "technology" → "technology in 2020s"
  - WHERE: "weather" → "weather in Austin, Texas"
  - WHY: "photosynthesis" → "for children's book"

**MINUTES 11-17: Guided Practice**
- Display vague prompts on board
- Class improves together using 5 W's
- Create "5 W's Make Better Prompts" anchor chart
- Examples:
  - "Tell me about animals" → improve together
  - "Help with homework" → improve together

**MINUTES 17-19: Independent Practice**
- Students choose ONE vague prompt from workbook p.9
- Improve using at least 3 W's
- Write before/after versions

**MINUTES 19-20: Closure**
- Quick review: "When talking to AI, include..."
- Students call out the 5 W's
- Give "AI Detective" badges

### Teacher Tips
✅ **What Works:**
- Use exaggerated clarity vs. vagueness for humor
- Let students share real-life examples of unclear instructions
- Celebrate ALL improvements, even small ones

⚠️ **Watch For:**
- Students trying to use ALL 5 W's every time (they don't need to!)
- Confusion between "When" and "Where"
- Students who need sentence frames for writing

### Differentiation Quick Guide
- **Below Level:** Provide sentence frames, work in pairs
- **On Level:** Standard instruction
- **Above Level:** Challenge to use all 5 W's in one natural sentence
- **ELL:** Visual W cards, bilingual examples, extra processing time
- **IEP:** Break into single W at a time, oral responses OK

### Assessment
- **Formative:** Observe participation, check workbook p.9
- **Look For:** Can student identify vague vs. specific? Can they add at least 2-3 W's?

## LESSON 2: GARBAGE IN, GARBAGE OUT

### At a Glance
**Time:** 20 minutes  
**Workbook:** Pages 10-11  
**Key Concept:** Quality of input determines quality of output

### Minute-by-Minute Lesson Plan

**MINUTES 0-3: Warm-Up**
- "Recipe Following" demonstration
- Show vague vs. clear sandwich-making instructions
- Act out confusion with vague directions (make it fun!)

**MINUTES 3-10: Direct Instruction**
- Introduce GIGO principle with visual diagram
- Explain: Garbage (vague) → Garbage (unhelpful)
- Explain: Gold (clear) → Gold (helpful)
- Real examples on board:
  - Garbage: "How do I do math?" → vague AI response
  - Gold: "How do I add fractions with different denominators for 4th grade?" → helpful response
- Key principle: "AI isn't magic—it only knows what YOU tell it!"

**MINUTES 10-17: Guided Practice**
- "Garbage or Gold" sorting game
- Display prompt cards one at a time
- Class votes: Garbage or Gold?
- For each Garbage prompt, improve it together
- Examples from workbook p.11:
  1. "Tell me about space" - GARBAGE → improve
  2. "Explain clouds for 3rd grade science project" - GOLD ✓
  3. "Help me" - GARBAGE → improve
  (Continue through all 10 prompts)

**MINUTES 17-19: Independent Practice**
- Students complete workbook p.11 sorting activity
- Pick ONE garbage prompt and turn it to gold

**MINUTES 19-20: Closure**
- Class chant: "Garbage in, garbage out! Gold in, gold out!"
- Post new rule: "Give clear, specific prompts!"

### Teacher Tips
✅ **What Works:**
- Physical sorting (garbage bin vs. gold trophy cutouts)
- Let students defend their "garbage" or "gold" choices
- Use humor—make terrible prompts funny!

⚠️ **Watch For:**
- Students being too harsh calling things "garbage"
- Confusion about what makes a prompt "good enough"
- Students who need more examples

### Digital Integration
💻 **If using interactive tool:**
- Project "Garbage or Gold Game" on smartboard
- Students take turns dragging prompts
- Check answers together as class

### Assessment
- **Formative:** Sorting accuracy, improvement attempts
- **Look For:** Can student explain WHY a prompt is garbage/gold?

## LESSON 3: ADDING CONTEXT & DETAILS

### At a Glance
**Time:** 20 minutes  
**Workbook:** Pages 12-13  
**Key Concept:** Iteration and context improve prompts

### Minute-by-Minute Lesson Plan

**MINUTES 0-4: Warm-Up**
- "20 Questions" game (modified)
- Teacher thinks of object
- Students ask questions
- Debrief: Each question gave more CONTEXT!

**MINUTES 4-11: Direct Instruction**
- Define context with relatable example:
  - "It's cold" vs. "Cold in December in Minnesota, need heavy coat"
- Five types of helpful context:
  1. Your background ("I'm a 4th grader")
  2. Your goal ("for school project")
  3. Constraints ("under 5 sentences")
  4. Examples ("like Charlotte's Web")
  5. Preferences ("make it funny")
- Show iterative improvement:
  - V1: "Tell me about dogs"
  - V2: "Tell me about dogs for 4th grader"
  - V3: "Tell me what dogs eat for 4th grader"
  - V4: "Explain what dogs eat for 4th grade science report in 3-5 sentences"
- Key insight: "You don't need perfect first try—keep improving!"

**MINUTES 11-18: Guided Practice**
- "Pump Up the Prompt" activity
- Start with: "Tell me about weather"
- Add WHO, WHAT, WHY step-by-step as class
- Create visual showing progression
- Do 2-3 examples together

**MINUTES 18-19: Independent Practice**
- Workbook p.13: Students improve "Tell me about weather"
- Write 3 versions, each adding more details

**MINUTES 19-20: Closure**
- Share: 1-2 students read their progression
- Reminder: "Prompts are like rough drafts!"

### Teacher Tips
✅ **What Works:**
- Use cooking metaphor: "Adding ingredients makes it better!"
- Let students suggest what to add next
- Celebrate creativity in context

⚠️ **Watch For:**
- Students adding irrelevant details
- Perfectionism (it doesn't have to be perfect!)
- Students who rush and don't actually improve

### Extension Activity
🌟 **For early finishers:**
- Take a classmate's V1 prompt and improve it
- Write 5 different improvements focusing on different W's

### Assessment
- **Formative:** Three versions show improvement?
- **Look For:** Are they adding meaningful context, not just words?

## LESSON 4: POLITE COMMUNICATION WITH AI

### At a Glance
**Time:** 20 minutes  
**Workbook:** Pages 18-19  
**Key Concept:** Why manners matter (habit-building, not AI feelings)

### Minute-by-Minute Lesson Plan

**MINUTES 0-3: Warm-Up**
- "Manners Check-In" discussion
- When do you use polite words?
- Why do we use them?

**MINUTES 3-10: Direct Instruction**
- Address directly: "Does AI have feelings?" NO!
- Three reasons to be polite anyway:
  1. **Building Good Habits:** Practice becomes automatic
  2. **Better Results:** Polite = clearer communication
  3. **Respect Creators:** Honor the people who made AI
- Polite Prompt Formula:
  - Optional greeting: "Hi!"
  - Polite request: "Could you please..."
  - Clear need: (use 5 W's!)
  - Gratitude: "Thank you!"
- Show before/after:
  - Rude: "Do my homework"
  - Polite: "Hi! Could you help me understand this math problem? I'm working on fractions. Thank you!"
- Important note about cheating:
  - ❌ "Write my essay"
  - ✅ "Help me brainstorm ideas for my essay"

**MINUTES 10-17: Guided Practice**
- Role-play: Polite vs. Rude Requests
- Pairs take turns being "AI" and "student"
- First: demonstrate RUDE request
- Discuss: What made it rude? How did it feel?
- Then: demonstrate POLITE request
- Discuss: What changed? Better feeling?
- Do 2-3 scenarios as class
- Create "Class AI Manners Guide" anchor chart

**MINUTES 17-19: Independent Practice**
- Workbook p.18: Rewrite ONE rude prompt politely
- Options:
  - "Tell me about Texas"
  - "Find stuff about dogs"
  - "Do my math"
  - "Give me answers"

**MINUTES 19-20: Closure**
- Class promise: "I promise to practice good manners with AI and people!"
- Post "AI Manners Guide" in classroom

### Teacher Tips
✅ **What Works:**
- Role-play with exaggerated rudeness (funny, not mean)
- Connect to real-world manners practice
- Emphasize: It's about WHO YOU ARE, not how AI feels

⚠️ **Watch For:**
- Debates about "AI doesn't care, so why bother?"
- Students being rude in role-plays and staying in character
- Missing the habit-building point

### Family Connection
📧 **Send home this week:**
- "Polite Tech Challenge" tracking sheet
- Discussion: How does your family use polite language with tech?

### Assessment
- **Formative:** Role-play quality, rewritten prompts
- **Look For:** Understanding WHY manners matter beyond feelings

## MODULE ASSESSMENT (End of Week 2)

### Prompt Expert Challenge (Workbook Pages 20-21)

**Time:** 25-30 minutes (can extend beyond normal 20min lesson)

**Format:**
Students choose one scenario and complete 5-part process:
1. First draft prompt
2. Self-analysis (what's missing?)
3. Improved version
4. Final polished version
5. Reflection on improvements

**Scenarios:**
- Learn about Texas Revolution for history
- Find book similar to favorite
- Understand fraction multiplication
- Plan science experiment

**Rubric (4 levels: Beginning/Developing/Proficient/Advanced)**

Score on:
- Use of 5 W's (0-4 points)
- Specificity (0-4 points)
- Context & Details (0-4 points)
- Polite Communication (0-4 points)
- Understanding GIGO (0-4 points)
- Reflection Quality (0-4 points)

**Total: 24 points possible**
- Beginning: 6-9 pts
- Developing: 10-13 pts
- Proficient: 14-17 pts
- Advanced: 18-24 pts

**Target:** 85% of students achieve Proficient or higher

## DIFFERENTIATION STRATEGIES

### Below Grade Level
- Provide sentence frames for all writing
- Use visual 5 W's cards with pictures
- Partner with stronger readers/writers
- Reduce number of required examples
- Oral responses accepted
- Pre-teach vocabulary with visuals
- Work in small teacher-led group

### On Grade Level
- Standard instruction as outlined
- Mix individual and collaborative work
- Choice in how to demonstrate learning
- Balance scaffolding with independence

### Above Grade Level
- Extension questions about AI limitations
- Research real AI mistakes and explain
- Create teaching materials for younger students
- Design jobs that use AI strengths/avoid weaknesses
- Analyze multiple ways to ask same question
- Introduce "prompt engineering" as career

### English Language Learners
- Visual vocabulary cards (bilingual when possible)
- Sentence frames with blanks
- Partner with bilingual buddies
- Allow responses in home language with visual proof
- Extra processing time before answering
- Concrete examples over abstract
- Gestures and TPR during instruction
- Pre-teach key vocabulary with images

### Students with IEPs
- Extended time for all activities
- Break multi-step directions into single steps
- Movement breaks between activities
- Alternative response modes (oral, typed, illustrated)
- Reduced visual clutter on worksheets
- High-contrast materials
- Advance organizer showing lesson flow
- Check-ins during independent work
- Simplified language in instructions
- Provide worked examples as models

## TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

### "Students don't see the point of being polite to AI"
✅ **Solution:** Focus on habit-building narrative. "Everything you practice becomes automatic. We want your polite habits to be so strong they come out naturally with everyone—AI and people!"

### "Some students want to use ALL 5 W's every time"
✅ **Solution:** Emphasize "only use the W's that help!" Show examples where some W's don't apply.

### "Workbook activities taking too long"
✅ **Solution:** Reduce number of examples required. Focus on quality over quantity. Allow oral responses instead of written for some students.

### "Students struggling with what 'context' means"
✅ **Solution:** Use food metaphor: "Context is like adding toppings to pizza—it makes it exactly what you want!" Give many concrete examples from their lives.

### "Digital tools not working / no devices"
✅ **Solution:** All activities have paper-based versions. Use physical sorting games, verbal examples, board demonstrations. Digital is enhancement, not requirement!
