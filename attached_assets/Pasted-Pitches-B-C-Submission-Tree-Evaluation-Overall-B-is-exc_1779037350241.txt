Pitch B — Foster-Youth Transition Engine
What’s working.
Maya is as strong as Marcus. The specificity — no Social Security card, transcript behind a portal she no longer had the password to — is earned and precise. It doesn’t editorialize; it just describes the system failure accurately. Keep it exactly as written.
“We built the Transition Engine around a simple principle: reintegration only works when the navigation layer, the workforce pathway, and the community supports are reachable from the same door” is the best single sentence in Pitch B. That’s the thesis.
The four-domain risk scoring (housing, food, mental-health, documents) plus the printable case plan is concrete enough for Archie (MD, systems thinker) to evaluate clinically. That’s the right level of detail for this funder profile.
One flag: Jim Currier attribution.
“A warm-intro discussion with the National Foster Youth Institute alumni network is in progress (Jim Currier, MSW)” — parenthetical name-drops create a specific problem. If Smart Family Fund knows Jim Currier, you’ve made a claim they can verify before you’re ready. If they don’t, it reads as credential-stuffing a name they can’t evaluate. Either way, the parenthetical does more risk than work.
Fix: “A warm-intro discussion with the National Foster Youth Institute alumni network is in progress.” Drop the name. The institution is the credential; the name is exposure.
Minor: “no-login intake wizard” is accurate but “wizard” is a UX term that may read colloquially. Consider “no-login intake guide” or “no-login intake flow.” Low priority.

Pitch C — TCAF as Community Infrastructure
This is the strongest document in the package. The ecosystem-as-infrastructure framing — “each platform is a door, not a destination” — is the cleanest expression of TCAF’s theory of change across all three pitches. That line should live somewhere permanent.
The cross-platform handoff scenarios are well-constructed: Trade Sims learner → Whole-Person Health on benefits cliff; Spanish-monolingual newcomer → LifeBridge → workforce/healthcare/civic. These aren’t hypotheticals, they read as architecture. That’s the right register for a funder who wrote $250K to Recidiviz.
One structural note. The anchor pilots section presents two bullets with very different levels of readiness — Trade Sims (200-learner cohort launching) versus Transition Engine (two-state pilot in development, warm intro in progress). A careful reader will notice the asymmetry. You don’t need to hide it, but you should frame it deliberately: “Two anchor pilots at different stages of deployment — one cohort-launching, one infrastructure-stage — give the portfolio both near-term outcome data and longer-horizon system-building.” That turns asymmetry into portfolio strategy.
The closing paragraph is your best closing across all three pitches. “Meeting people where they are is not soft work, it’s the hard infrastructure work no one else is building” — that’s the sentence that stays with a reader. It also directly answers the implicit question every funder asks: why hasn’t this been done?
