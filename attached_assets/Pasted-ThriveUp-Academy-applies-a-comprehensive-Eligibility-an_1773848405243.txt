ThriveUp Academy applies a comprehensive Eligibility and Outreach Plan designed to meet WIOA Title I Youth requirements while advancing our organization’s unique mission to empower youth through a multi-platform ecosystem that integrates education, real-work experience, health equity, and community navigation. The plan is anchored in a rigorous understanding of the local and national context, a theory-driven approach to service design, explicit eligibility verification processes, and a robust outreach strategy that centers equity and inclusion. It leverages ThriveUp’s 14-platform technology ecosystem, the MAP-GAP continuous improvement framework, the Structured Adherence/Fidelity Protocol (SALP), and the Three Realities lens (Research Reality, Political Reality, Ground-Level Reality) to drive measurable outcomes for youth ages 16–24 who face significant barriers to education and employment.

Context and Statement of Need
National data consistently demonstrate that youth and young adults face disproportionate barriers to meaningful employment and credential attainment, with persistent gaps in earnings and labor market attachment for those experiencing housing instability, caregiving responsibilities, English-language challenges, and prior involvement with the justice system. The U.S. Department of Labor’s WIOA data and related national analyses show that youth with barriers—whether dropout risk, basic skills deficiencies, English learner status, or disability—require a coordinated set of services that combines education, occupational skills training, work experiences, mentoring, and intensive supportive services to achieve sustained employment and credential attainment. In addition, persistent disparities exist by race/ethnicity and income level, underscoring the need for equity-centered outreach and culturally responsive service delivery.

Locally, ThriveUp Academy operates within a regional labor market characterized by growth in health care, advanced manufacturing, information technology, and services sectors that demand credentialed youth. Employer partners consistently report that youth with hands-on experience and clear career pathways are more likely to be hired, retained, and promoted. However, many eligible OSY (Out-of-School Youth) and ISY (In-School Youth) encounter barriers that impede access to training opportunities, reliable transportation, childcare, literacy and numeracy supports, and navigation to postsecondary opportunities. Our local data collection from partner schools, community-based organizations, and the Local Workforce Development Board (LWDB) indicate that 60–70% of OSY in the region face at least one barrier (e.g., housing instability, basic skills gap, or prior involvement with the juvenile justice system) and that a sizable segment of youth would benefit from paid/unpaid work experiences and structured mentoring if provided within a coordinated pathway.

ThriveUp recognizes that a fragmented service landscape often leads to poor coordination across education, training, and employment services. Therefore, our approach is designed to deliver a seamless youth pathway by combining ThriveUp’s education platforms, the Incubator’s program design capabilities, MCE’s economic empowerment tools, and LifeBridge’s community benefits navigation with platform-level fidelity monitoring through RPLICE and outcomes evaluation via Better Science Lab. This strategy aligns with evidence-based practices that emphasize work-based learning, targeted mentoring, academic enrichment, financial literacy, and supportive services as core determinants of sustainable employment and credential attainment for youth facing barriers (DOL/ETA, 2022; RAND, 2020; Institute for Research on Poverty, 2019). The plan also embraces the MAP-GAP framework—Monitoring, Assessing, Predicting—Gap analysis as a living process to identify barriers early, implement targeted interventions, and adjust pathways in real time. The SALP framework ensures fidelity to program design and data-driven decision-making across platform teams. The Three Realities lens ensures our strategy remains grounded in research evidence, policy context, and ground-level experiences of youth participants, family members, and employers. Taken together, ThriveUp’s Eligibility and Outreach Plan is designed to achieve measurable improvements in employment, credential attainment, and skill gains for OSY and ISY while strengthening the region’s talent pipeline through a scalable, equity-centered model.

Program Design and Theory of Change
ThriveUp’s program design rests on a theory of change that links diverse supports to concrete labor market outcomes. At the heart of our model is a youth-centered Career Pathway Alignment process that begins with a purposeful intake and a rigorous gap analysis using MAP-GAP. The gap analysis identifies learning needs, credential prerequisites, work-readiness deficits, and barrier reduction requirements (transportation, childcare, housing stability, financial literacy, health access). The Integrated Pathways Plan then coordinates services across ThriveUp Academy’s core educational offerings, the Incubator’s program design capabilities, MCE’s economic empowerment tools, and LifeBridge’s benefits navigation to ensure a seamless service continuum. A central premise is that paid or unpaid work experiences embedded within a structured apprenticeship or pre-apprenticeship model increase both immediate earnings and long-term credential attainment; this is reinforced by adult mentoring, financial literacy education, and leadership development offered through the 14-platform ecosystem. The program’s evidence base draws on well-established components of youth workforce development: high-quality career pathways with linked work experiences, mentoring, rigorous occupational skills training, concurrent education and workforce preparation, and robust supportive services (Kemple & CTE program research, 2007; DOL ETA, 2022; RAND, 2019).
