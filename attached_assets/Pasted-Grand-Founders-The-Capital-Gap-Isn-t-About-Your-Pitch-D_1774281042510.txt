The Capital Gap Isn’t About Your Pitch Deck. It’s About Who’s in the Room.
For founders shut out of traditional investor networks, a 90-day structured pathway is replacing cold outreach with verified relationships and measurable outcomes.
Grand Founders
Mar 23, 2026



There is a well-documented funding gap in the startup ecosystem. Venture capital flows overwhelmingly to founders who already have access to established networks — the warm introductions, the right alumni circles, the investor dinners that never make it onto a public calendar. For veterans transitioning out of military service, women building companies in male-dominated sectors, Black founders and outleaders operating outside of traditional capital circles, and immigrants navigating an unfamiliar landscape, the barrier is rarely a lack of talent or ambition. It is a lack of access.

That gap is what Network-to-Capital was built to close — a structured 90-day initiative that pairs underrepresented and emerging founders with a verified network of investors, family offices, and strategic partners. The program is run by Grand Founders, a New York–based 501(c)(3) non-profit foundation focused on strengthening national security and advancing economic development through entrepreneurial support.

It is not an accelerator. It is not a pitch competition. And that distinction matters.

The Problem With “Access”
The startup world talks constantly about access to capital. Conferences are organized around it. Panels are dedicated to it. Yet for most underrepresented founders, the actual mechanics of access remain opaque. Knowing that investors exist is not the same as knowing which investors are credible, active, and aligned with your sector. Sending a cold email is not the same as receiving a trusted introduction. And attending a networking event is not the same as being in a room where decisions are made.

Network-to-Capital was designed around this reality. Instead of generic programming, it offers a specific, time-bound path: ninety days of disciplined preparation, verified investor matching, and strategic introductions — all within an ecosystem where both sides of the table have been screened for credibility.

“Access to capital is rarely just about funding — it is built on trusted relationships. Network-to-Capital creates a disciplined pathway from introduction to revenue, contracts, and long-term partnerships.”

— Richard Ogilchyn, CEO at Grand Founders

How It Works
If you are a founder entering this program, every investor introduction you receive has been verified. The person across the table is active, credible, and aligned with your sector — not a name pulled from a database. If you are an investor, every founder you meet has been screened for execution capability, market readiness, and leadership quality. That mutual verification is handled through Validus, a credence-based nexus that brings together identity assurance, business intelligence, and matchmaking to ensure both sides of the table are operating in good faith.

The program runs on a 90-day execution cycle. The emphasis is on outcomes you can measure — contracts closed, revenue generated, partnerships formed — not on how polished a slide deck looks under stage lighting. Participants receive disciplined preparation, strategic introductions, and direct engagement with investors and partners across the network.

At the end of each cycle, the top five performers earn a seat at a private salon dinner alongside Fortune 500 C-suite executives, family office principals, investors, and senior industry leaders. For founders who have spent years trying to get into these rooms through cold outreach, that seat represents something more than recognition — it is the beginning of relationships that compound over time.

Why Credence Changes Everything
One of the quieter problems in the startup ecosystem is trust — and it costs everyone. Founders waste months pursuing investors who turn out to be unserious, inactive, or misaligned. Investors wade through hundreds of inbound pitches with no reliable signal. Family offices hesitate to engage with founders they cannot independently verify. The result is a system that rewards persistence and proximity over substance and readiness.

This is the problem verification solves. When both sides of a capital relationship have been screened — through identity assurance, behavioral credibility, and demonstrated track record — the nature of the first meeting changes entirely. A founder is not pitching into a void. An investor is not guessing at credibility. A family office principal can engage knowing that the introduction was made with their standards in mind. That shift from uncertainty to credence is what makes the 90-day cycle productive rather than performative.

“Identifying and investing in underrepresented founders strengthens the innovation ecosystem by unlocking talent and perspectives often overlooked by traditional investors. Programs such as Network-to-Capital connect teams with credible investors and actionable insights, bridging the gap between emerging leaders and the businesses seeking their ideas and products. Building strong, enduring networks amplifies this impact and creates lasting platforms.”

— Vlad Lukashevsky, Investor at Tenfore Holdings.

Who It’s For
If you are a veteran who developed leadership under pressure but left the service without venture connections — this was built for you. If you are a woman founder navigating funding environments where less than three percent of VC dollars historically flow to all-female teams. If you are a Black founder, an outleader, a Ukrainian innovator building a company in a new market, or an immigrant entrepreneur with limited institutional support. The program serves founders from underrepresented backgrounds who have the capability to execute but lack a credible on-ramp to capital.

The common thread is not a demographic checkbox. It is a gap between capability and access. These are founders who can execute — what they need is a credible pathway into the rooms where capital decisions happen.

Execution Over Everything
Perhaps the most meaningful aspect of Network-to-Capital for participants is what it does not include. There are no weeks of workshops before you speak with an investor. No cohort sessions that delay the work you are already doing. The 90-day cycle is designed to compress the timeline between preparation and action, between introduction and outcome.

The measure of each cycle is tangible: did you close a contract, generate revenue, secure an investment, or form a strategic partnership? The private salon dinner at the end of each cycle is not a graduation ceremony — it places high performers directly in front of the decision-makers who can advance their trajectory. For investors and family offices, it surfaces founders who have already demonstrated they can deliver.

In a landscape crowded with programs that promise access but deliver little beyond a logo on a landing page, that focus on measurable outcomes is what founders, investors, and partners stand to gain.

“This initiative creates an environment where execution matters more than presentation,”

— Doug Doan, Founder and GP of Hivers and Strivers Investment Fund.
