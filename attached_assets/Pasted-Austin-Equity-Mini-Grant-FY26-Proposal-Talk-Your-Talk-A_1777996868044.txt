Austin Equity Mini Grant FY26 — Proposal
Talk Your Talk: A Belonging & Voice Initiative for Austin's Immigrant, Refugee, and Dialect-Diverse Communities
Applicant: Talk Your Talk (Speech Bridge) Request: $25,000 Project period: July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2027 Service area: City of Austin (with priority focus on Eastern Crescent: 78702, 78721, 78723, 78724, 78744, 78753, 78758) Primary populations served: Spanish-speaking immigrant families, Haitian Creole-speaking newcomers, Vietnamese elders, Arabic- and Dari-speaking refugees, AAVE and Spanglish speakers, Deaf/Hard-of-Hearing residents, stroke survivors, neurodivergent adults, and any Austin resident whose first language or dialect is not standard institutional English.

1. Project Summary (150 words)
Talk Your Talk is a free, dialect-aware, accessibility-centered communication platform that already exists, already works, and is already in production. We are not asking for funding to build a prototype — we are asking for $25,000 to operationalize what we have built into Austin's most language-isolated neighborhoods over twelve months.

The platform delivers real-time interpretation across 107 spoken languages and 18 sign languages, plain-language explainers for the documents that decide people's lives (school notices, court summons, eviction notices, USCIS letters, medical bills), printable "Know Your Rights" cards in 13 languages, and a peer-to-peer mentor network that connects newcomers with neighbors who arrived a few years before them. Every feature was designed with — not for — the communities who use it. This grant funds outreach, in-person training, multilingual community navigators, printed materials, and outcome reporting back to the Equity Office.

3. Project Need
In Austin, roughly one in three residents speaks a language other than English at home, and a meaningful share of those households have limited English proficiency. The city's growth has not been matched by growth in linguistically and culturally appropriate services — and the gap falls hardest on the Eastern Crescent, where displacement pressure is also highest.

The result is a daily pattern that shows up in every immigrant-serving organization in this city:

A parent receives a school notice about a discipline incident and cannot tell whether it requires a response, a meeting, or a lawyer.
A tenant receives an eviction notice with a 3-day deadline and has no way to know that responding wrong forfeits their case.
A worker receives a wage-theft document and cannot identify Workers Defense Project as the right place to call.
A grandmother who speaks Vietnamese watches her grandchild translate a medical consent form she does not understand and then signs anyway.
A Deaf resident in a hospital intake room is handed an iPad with no interpreter on it.
Existing tools fail these residents in three specific ways:

They flatten dialect. Automated translation strips out the version of Spanish, English, or Arabic the person actually speaks, and replies in a register that does not sound like home.
They translate words but not stakes. Google Translate will render an eviction notice in Spanish; it will not tell you that you have 3 days, that you should not pay partial rent, and that BASTA Austin is the number to call.
They are not accountable to anyone. Big tech translation tools have no relationship to Austin organizations, no feedback loop to Austin equity outcomes, and no obligation to the people who use them.
Talk Your Talk closes all three gaps — and it does so as infrastructure that local organizations can adopt without paying a license fee.

7. Equity & Accountability Practices
Community advisory group: Five members from served communities, paid stipends, meet quarterly. They review outcomes data and have authority to adjust priorities mid-grant.
Consent-based data: Anonymized outcome metrics only. Users explicitly opt in before any data is shared with the broader ecosystem.
No data sale, no advertising, no algorithmic profiling of users. Period.
Open methodology: Our measurement approach is published and auditable. We will not report numbers we cannot back up with database evidence.
Pay community labor: Translators, testers, advisory members, and host organizations are all compensated. We do not extract free labor from the communities we serve.
Child-safe by default: Family Circle members tagged as children only see safety-appropriate content, with no override required from a parent.
8. Sustainability Beyond the Grant Year
This grant is operational, not foundational. The technical platform exists and continues to operate regardless of this award. After Year 1, sustainability comes from three sources:

Partner organization adoption: Trained navigators stay embedded; partner orgs absorb usage into their existing case-management workflows.
Other regional grants and city contracts: Documented Year-1 outcomes become the evidence base for state, federal (HHS Office of Refugee Resettlement, USDOJ Access to Justice), and additional Austin/Travis County funding.
The technology itself is free to the user, forever. There is no subscription model that fails when funding lapses.
9. Why Now, Why Austin, Why Us
Austin is a city that has spent the last decade making "equity" a stated value while watching the residents most affected by inequity get pushed further east, further out, and further from the institutions making decisions about their lives. Language is one of the levers of that displacement. When you cannot read the notice taped to your door, you cannot fight the eviction. When you cannot understand the school's discipline letter, your child's record follows them for years. When you cannot ask the question you need to ask the doctor, your health outcome is worse.

Talk Your Talk does not solve those structural problems. It does, however, refuse to let language be the reason a family loses ground. For $25,000, this grant puts a free, multilingual, community-accountable communication tool into the hands of the Austin residents the city's growth has cost the most.
