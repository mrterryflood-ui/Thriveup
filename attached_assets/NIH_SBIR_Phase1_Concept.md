## Health Problem & Significance

### The Health Equity Gap
Health disparities in the United States remain stubbornly persistent. Communities with the highest Social Vulnerability Index (SVI) scores experience:
- 2.5x higher rates of preventable hospitalization
- 40% lower access to primary care
- Significantly higher rates of chronic disease, mental health conditions, and substance use disorders
- Lower health insurance coverage (up to 25% uninsured in high-SVI communities vs. 8% national average)

Yet frontline health workers serving these communities — community health workers, peer navigators, faith-based health ministers, and social workers — lack access to integrated, real-time data about the communities they serve. They navigate fragmented systems, make referrals to services they can't verify, and lack the data to advocate for their communities with policymakers and funders.

### The Innovation: AI-Powered SDOH Intelligence
ThriveUp Academy's health equity platform cluster addresses this gap through:

**1. Real-Time Community Health Intelligence**
- Integrates 8 federal health and social data sources (Census ACS, CDC PLACES, CDC/ATSDR SVI, SAMHSA, USDA Food Atlas, HUD, BLS, FBI Crime Data)
- Computes Social Vulnerability Index at the ZCTA level using CDC/ATSDR methodology
- Identifies health-relevant community strengths and challenges with specific solutions
- Matches communities to relevant federal health grants and programs

**2. Six Integrated Health Platforms**

| Platform | Health Focus | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Sankofa Health Network** | Health equity coordination | Community health data, resource mapping, disparity analysis |
| **Black Maternal Health Network** | Maternal mortality reduction | Prenatal/postnatal tracking, risk factor identification, provider connection |
| **Black Men's Health Hub** | Men's health disparities | Screening reminders, culturally responsive health education, provider matching |
| **Holistic Black Feminine Health Hub** | Women's holistic health | Whole-person health assessment, integrative care navigation |
| **SafeCogniCare** | Cognitive health monitoring | AI-assisted cognitive screening, caregiver support, early intervention |
| **PillScheduler** | Medication adherence | Smart scheduling, refill reminders, interaction checking |

**3. Benefits Navigation & Eligibility Screening**
- 5-county benefits intelligence system (Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop, Caldwell)
- Automated eligibility screening for Medicaid, CHIP, SNAP, WIC, housing assistance
- Enrollment gap analysis identifying underutilization of available benefits
- Barrier index computation for access obstacles

**4. 4-Engine Consensus AI for Health Communication**
- Multi-model architecture eliminates single-model bias in health information
- Compassionate framing: "This is DATA, not characterization"
- Health literacy-appropriate output for community audiences
- Implementation science validation (RPLICE, CFIR 2.0, RE-AIM)

## Research Foundation

### Published Research
**Stillwell, C. (2026).** SVI-based geographic accountability in higher education accreditation. Under review at *Nature*. Key finding: SVI inversely correlates with bachelor's degree attainment across all 3,144 U.S. counties — a 9.3 percentage-point gap between highest and lowest SVI quintiles. This validates SVI as a proxy for health and economic opportunity, and positions SDOH-integrated technology as an evidence-based intervention.

### Established Frameworks
- **CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index** — 16-variable, 4-theme vulnerability assessment
- **CDC PLACES** — Model-based local health outcome estimates at census tract level
- **CFIR 2.0** (Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research)
- **RE-AIM** (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance)
- **RPLICE** — Research-Practice-Linkage for Implementation, Community, and Equity

## Specific Aims

### Aim 1: Validate AI-Generated Community Health Profiles
Compare ThriveUp Neighborhood Intelligence health assessments against gold-standard community health needs assessments (CHNAs) in 10 Austin-area ZIP codes. Hypothesis: AI-generated profiles will identify ≥80% of health priorities identified by traditional CHNAs, at <1% of the time and cost.

### Aim 2: Assess Frontline Health Worker Adoption
Deploy the platform with 50 community health workers and faith-based health ministers in high-SVI Austin communities. Measure: technology acceptance (TAM), self-efficacy in health advocacy, frequency of data-informed decision-making, and benefits enrollment rates.

### Aim 3: Measure Community Health Agency Outcomes
Pre/post assessment of community members (n=200) exposed to Neighborhood Intelligence data stories via community health workers. Primary outcome: perceived health self-efficacy (validated scale). Secondary outcomes: benefits enrollment, primary care connection, mental health screening completion.

## Innovation & Broader Impacts
This project advances NIH's mission by:
1. Creating the first AI-powered platform that integrates all major federal SDOH datasets into a single, community-accessible tool
2. Validating a novel multi-model AI consensus architecture that reduces bias in health communication to vulnerable populations
3. Providing an implementation science-validated instrument (RPLICE) for measuring health equity intervention fidelity
4. Demonstrating that community-level health agency can be enhanced through compassionate data access — not just clinical interventions
