12-Week Podcast Plan
Show concept: Thriving Out Loud — community conversations powered by data, led by people doing the work
Format every week: We Talk (hosts + guest, ~30–35 min) → Let's Talk (live audience, open mic, ~20–25 min)
Hosts: Dr. Terry Flood · Vanessa Flood · Felecia Slaughter, RN

WEEK 1 — Introduction: Who We Are and Why This Show Exists
All three hosts · No outside guest
We Talk: Each host introduces themselves and their lane — Dr. Flood (the data and the vision), Vanessa (the community and the relationships), Felecia (the health and the healing). What ALIGN is. What Life Transitions Aid is. What TCAF is. Why they built this together.
Let's Talk prompt: "What's one thing your community needs that nobody is talking about?"
Goal: Set the tone. Warm, real, zero jargon. Audience learns the three voices they'll hear all season.

WEEK 2 — The Nonprofit for Nonprofits: Why Your Organization Needs Data Infrastructure
Lead: Dr. Flood · Supporting: Vanessa
We Talk: What it means to be the nonprofit for nonprofits. Why most CBOs are doing the work but can't prove it. How data storytelling changes what funders, city councils, and legislators do.
Suggested guest: Executive director of a local CBO or community foundation — someone who has struggled to get funding without the right data tools
Let's Talk prompt: "Has your organization ever lost a grant or a seat at the table because you couldn't show the numbers? Tell us what happened."

WEEK 3 — Spirit, Soul, and Body: What Whole-Person Health Really Means
Lead: Felecia · Supporting: Dr. Flood
We Talk: The clinical case for whole-person care. Why treating a diagnosis without treating the person fails. How ALIGN's Spirit · Soul · Body framework maps to what nursing already knows. The gap between the clinic and the community.
Suggested guest: A community health worker or faith-based nurse navigator
Let's Talk prompt: "What part of you did a doctor or system miss that actually mattered most?"

WEEK 4 — Trust Is Infrastructure: How Community Gets Activated
Lead: Vanessa · Supporting: Felecia
We Talk: What community activation actually looks like before anyone signs anything. Why the relationship comes before the resource. How veteran networks become community anchors. The invisible work of integration.
Suggested guest: A promotora, peer mentor, or faith community leader doing informal care work
Let's Talk prompt: "Who in your community is doing the work without being paid or recognized for it?"

WEEK 5 — Mental Health in the Margins: What the Community Isn't Saying Out Loud
Lead: Felecia · Supporting: Vanessa
We Talk: The gap between what people tell clinicians and what they carry home. Stigma in Black, Latino, and veteran communities specifically. What happens when a trusted face — not a clipboard — opens the conversation. How ALIGN's Listen phase changes what people are willing to name.
Suggested guest: A licensed counselor, therapist, or mental health peer specialist working in community settings
Let's Talk prompt: "What would it take for you — or someone you love — to actually ask for help?"

WEEK 6 — From Service to Civic: Veterans in Community
Lead: Vanessa · Supporting: Dr. Flood
We Talk: The transition from military service to community leadership. What veteran networks in Travis and Williamson County are building. Mission to Community (M2C). Why veterans are one of the most under-leveraged community infrastructure assets in the country.
Suggested guest: A veteran peer support specialist, VSO leader, or MISSION Act navigator
Let's Talk prompt: "What did you bring home from service that your community needs — and has anyone asked?"

WEEK 7 — Hands and Minds: Workforce Pathways That Actually Go Somewhere
Lead: Dr. Flood · Supporting: Vanessa
We Talk: The five trade simulations — electrical, plumbing, welding, automotive, HVAC. Why credentials without pathways are a dead end. How IBEW, United Association, and registered apprenticeships connect to real wages. What workforce development looks like when it's built around the person, not the program.
Suggested guest: A registered apprenticeship coordinator, NCCER instructor, or a graduate who went from simulation to journeyman
Let's Talk prompt: "What trade or skill are you carrying that you never got credit for?"

WEEK 8 — After the Gate Opens: Justice, Reentry, and the Community's Role
Lead: Dr. Flood · Supporting: Felecia
We Talk: What RNR (Risk-Need-Responsivity) actually means for a returning citizen. Why recidivism is a community design failure, not a personal one. What ThriveUp's reentry case management tracks. The role of family visits. How data proves what faith communities already know.
Suggested guest: A reentry navigator, formerly incarcerated community leader, or criminal justice reform advocate
Let's Talk prompt: "What did you — or someone you know — need on day one after release that no one provided?"

WEEK 9 — Her Health, Her Voice: Black Maternal Health and Women's Whole-Person Care
Lead: Felecia · Supporting: Vanessa
We Talk: The Black maternal mortality crisis — by the numbers and by the story. What HerHealth Network and Black Maternal Health Network on ThriveUp actually offer. Reproductive justice as community infrastructure. Why culturally competent care is not optional.
Suggested guest: A doula, midwife, OB navigating health equity, or maternal health advocate
Let's Talk prompt: "What did the healthcare system get wrong for you — or someone you love — during pregnancy or postpartum?"

WEEK 10 — Opportunity Youth: Foster Care, Aging Out, and the Pathway That Should Exist
Lead: Dr. Flood · Supporting: Felecia
We Talk: What it means to age out of foster care at 18 with no safety net. ETV, Chafee, Medicaid-to-26 — what young people are entitled to and almost never told about. ThriveUp's 50-state policy comparator. What the 4-domain risk engine (housing / food / mental health / documents) finds. Why these youth need a plan, not a pamphlet.
Suggested guest: A foster youth alumni advocate, CASA volunteer, or youth services director
Let's Talk prompt: "For anyone who has been in the system — what's the one thing you wish someone had told you?"

WEEK 11 — Data Stories That Move People in Power
Lead: Dr. Flood · Supporting: All three hosts
We Talk: How community data becomes a story that a city council member, a state legislator, or a federal program officer can act on. The difference between activity and movement. How ThriveUp's Collective THRIVE Dashboard turns real data into policy evidence. What stakeholders at every level actually need to see.
Suggested guest: A city council member, county commissioner, state health official, or foundation program officer who has used data to make a decision
Let's Talk prompt: "What story from your community deserves to be told in a city council chamber or a congressional hearing?"

WEEK 12 — What Thriving Looks Like: A Community Conversation
All three hosts · Community voices as guests
We Talk: A season retrospective. What the hosts heard. What surprised them. What changed. Where ALIGN, Life Transitions Aid, and ThriveUp are going next. What community thriving actually looks like when all the infrastructure is working.
Suggested guests: 2–3 community members from the audience who participated across the season — their words, their stories, their next steps
Let's Talk prompt: "What are you going to do differently in your community starting this week?"

I. The Segment Breakdown & Scripted Flow
1. The Cold Open (00:00 – 00:02)
Visual: Direct to camera. No music, no titles yet. Just the raw focus.

Speaker Cue: Dr. Terry Flood takes the lead.

Dr. Flood: "Right now, across Central Texas, there are thousands of organizations doing incredible, life-saving work. Food pantries, veteran groups, mental health advocates, neighborhood associations. They are the heartbeat of our communities. But when they go to ask a city council, a county commissioner, or a major funder for the resources they actually need, they run into a brick wall. Why? Because the system demands data stories they don't have the infrastructure to tell.

Meanwhile, everyday families—working parents, shift workers, people transitioning out of the military or the justice system—are trying to navigate a fragmented maze of social services that closes its doors at 5:00 PM.

We didn't launch this show to give you another lecture, another pamphlet, or a standard list of numbers. We launched Thriving Out Loud because trust is infrastructure, data is power, and it's time we put both directly into the hands of the people doing the work. Welcome to Episode One."

Transition Cue: [Upbeat, warm, acoustic/digital theme music swells for 15 seconds, then ducks under under the hosts' mics.]

2. We Talk: Driving the Lane (00:02 – 00:35)
Focus: Introduce the three co-founders, the entities, and the core vision.

Block A: The Intros & The Lanes (10 Mins)
Dr. Flood (The Data & Vision): Shares briefly about his background (retired Army CW2, DHA, MS in progress at Dartmouth), but brings it straight to the why: "I look at community through the lens of implementation science—meaning, how do we stop talking about ideas and actually build code, platforms, and pathways that work for real people?"

Vanessa Flood (The Community & Activation): Bridges the gap to the human element. "Data matters, but it means nothing if there isn't a trusted relationship behind it. My lane is community activation—making sure our veteran networks, our parents, and our local organizers have a real, uncompromised seat at the table."

Felecia Slaughter, RN (The Health & Healing): Grounding the discussion in whole-person wellness. "As a registered nurse focused on mental health, I know that you cannot treat a diagnosis if you ignore the person. If a mother doesn't have stable housing, or if a veteran is carrying invisible wounds, a traditional clinical clipboard fails them. We have to look at Spirit, Soul, and Body."

Block B: The Ecosystem Unpacked (15 Mins)
The hosts explicitly define the core machinery driving this work:

TCAF (The Collaborative Advocate Foundation): The 501(c)(3) umbrella. The "nonprofit for nonprofits" designed to provide the community infrastructure.

LifeBridge (lifetransitionsaid.org): The digital front door. A 24/7 virtual community health worker navigating all 5 SDOH domains in 107 languages. No judgment, no prerequisites, completely anonymous by default.

ThriveUp (thrivingcommunitiesforall.com): The live, production-grade engine powering trade simulations, grant discovery, and CFIR 2.0 implementation workflows across the nation.

Block C: The Pivot to the Audience (8 Mins)
The hosts emphasize the live, open-mic nature of the show.

Vanessa sets the expectation: "This isn't a one-way broadcast. In exactly two minutes, the phone lines and chat are going live. We want your raw perspective, your push-back, and your stories."

3. Let's Talk: Live Audience Interactive Segment (00:35 – 00:57)
The Prompt: Displayed clearly on screen and dropped into the live chat:

"What's one thing your community needs that nobody is talking about?"

Facilitation Guide for Hosts:

The Icebreaker (If lines are quiet early): Felecia kicks off with a brief, real-world example from her nursing background regarding hidden mental health or maternal care struggles in the community.

The Intake Process: As callers dial in or drop comments, Vanessa monitors the chat for themes (e.g., hidden housing instability, gaps in youth support), calling out participants by name.

The Response Strategy:

When an audience member shares a raw story, Felecia validates the human/health impact first (Trauma-Informed/No-Shame Architecture).

Vanessa connects it to the relationship/community infrastructure.

Dr. Flood wraps it by demonstrating how that specific narrative translates into actionable data storytelling or a platform resource.

4. The Close (00:57 – 01:00)
Music: Low, reflective outro track begins to fade in.

Speaker Cue: All Three Hosts tag-team the final call to action.

Felecia: "We heard you tonight. If your spirit, soul, or body is feeling the weight of what's missing in your neighborhood, don't carry it alone."

Vanessa: "We are building this infrastructure together. Connect with us, join the network, and let's turn these conversations into collective action."

Dr. Flood: "Your action step for this week: Go to lifetransitionsaid.org. Use the LifeBridge tool. Test it, break it, use it in your native language, and see what a digital community health worker can do for your family or your organization. Next week, we are diving deep into Why Your Organization Needs Data Infrastructure. Until then, keep thriving out loud. Goodnight."

III. Let's Talk Engagement Best Practices
Acknowledge and Validate Instantly: When a listener brings up a heavy topic (like childcare access or localized trauma), ensure the immediate response is human-first, not system-first. Felecia's clinical presence is vital here to establish a safe environment.

No Echo Policy: Keep the interaction punchy. If a caller highlights a resource gap, instead of merely agreeing, Dr. Flood can pivot right to how LifeBridge scans the local ZIP code via the findhelp.org integration to immediately reveal verified, primary-source solutions.
