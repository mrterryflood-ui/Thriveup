What this gets right:

The division of labor maps perfectly to how organizations actually experience the problem. They don't wake up thinking "I need a grant." They wake up thinking "I have a drone program and I need resources." ThriveUp is where that plain-language reality lives. GrantPath Pro is where the professional execution machinery lives. Those are genuinely different jobs and they should not be collapsed into one interface.

The flowchart you pasted is also the right sequence. Discovery → strategy → path selection → handoff → execution → feedback loop. That's a complete lifecycle, not a feature list.

The one thing I'd push on:

The feedback loop at the bottom — "status, milestones, and lessons return to ThriveUp" — is the most undervalued part of this whole architecture. That's where the system gets smarter over time. When GrantPath Pro knows that Carver ATX Foundation won the NEA grant and lost the IMLS grant, and that data flows back to ThriveUp, the next org with a similar profile gets better recommendations from day one. That's the compounding intelligence layer. It needs to be designed deliberately, not bolted on later.

What this means practically:

The mirror integration you already have — the ingest webhook, the embed endpoint — that's the data pipe between the two systems. Right now it moves opportunity data one direction. It needs to eventually move outcome data the other direction. Win/loss, award amounts, lessons learned, funder relationships. That closes the loop.

My honest take:

Don't build the ecosystem intelligence inside GrantPath Pro at all. Build it in ThriveUp. What GrantPath Pro needs is a clean, well-structured handoff API — a "here is the verified org package and the selected opportunity, now manage the pursuit" contract. The mirror endpoint you have is already 80% of that.
