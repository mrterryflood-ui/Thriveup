ThriveUp's program design for WIOA Title I Youth Programs is grounded in a rigorous, ecosystem-based approach that accelerates career pathways for youth facing barriers while ensuring sustained employment, credential attainment, and measurable skill development. The model centers on youth, aligns with labor-market demand, and leverages the breadth of ThriveUp’s 14-platform ecosystem to deliver integrated supports, real-world experiences, and continuous improvement. It is designed to meet WIOA requirements for youth services—combining robust career pathways, high-quality training, work-based learning, and strong employer engagement—while incorporating ThriveUp’s unique differentiators: Three Realities as a planning and implementation compass, MAP-GAP as our continuous-improvement framework, and SALP as our fidelity assurance mechanism. Our service delivery is intentionally multi-layered, data-informed, and anchored in youth development principles to produce concrete, transformative outcomes for OSY and youth facing barriers.

Our service delivery design begins with a targeted, equity-centered intake and determination of needs. We will identify participants ages 16–24 who are out-of-school or disconnected from the labor market and who encounter multiple barriers—economic hardship, unstable housing, foster-care histories, prior justice involvement, language or disability-related challenges, or limited access to postsecondary opportunities. Leveraging LifeBridge, the platform for community voice, youth will help shape pathway design by providing feedback on curricula relevance, scheduling, and supportive services. The intake process uses standardized, trauma-informed assessments to determine readiness, skill gaps, and preferred career interests, and to map participants to one of ThriveUp’s defined career pathways. Throughout intake, we will ensure language access, transportation and childcare supports, and digital access through our 14-platform ecosystem, enabling participation regardless of initial resource constraints.

Career pathways and curricula are designed around targeted sectors with strong local labor-market demand and clear wage progression. We will implement three primary industry pathways—healthcare and health support, information technology and cybersecurity fundamentals, and advanced manufacturing and construction trades—with a fourth pathway for community-based entrepreneurship and care economy roles, as appropriate to local need. Each pathway comprises a stackable sequence of aligned credentials, starting with foundational soft and employability skills (resume development, interview readiness, digital literacy) and progressing to sector-specific technical credentials (for example: Certified Nursing Assistant or equivalent health-support credentials; CompTIA IT Fundamentals or IT-related certifications; NCCER or OSHA-based certifications for construction and manufacturing; solar installer certifications where market demand supports it). All credentials will align with recognized industry standards and be coordinated with local postsecondary partners for credit-bearing opportunities when feasible.

A core element of our design is integrated work-based learning (WBL), including paid internships, structured on-the-job training, and apprenticeship-like experiences co-designed with employers. Each participant will complete a minimum of 120 hours of paid WBL during the program year, with opportunities to extend into longer-term placements dependent on pathway achievement and employer needs. Our WBL experiences are matched to the participant’s pathway and readiness, with employers providing real-world projects and mentored learning. We will embed WBL within a broader employment services plan that includes job readiness coaching, career navigation, and placement assistance for unsubsidized employment or enrollment in postsecondary education or battery of credential-focused programs. To ensure quality and fidelity, all work-based learning activities will be governed by RPLICE practices and monitored for adherence to defined learning objectives, safety standards, and equitable access.

Employer engagement is central to ThriveUp’s design and is anchored in a proactive, sustained strategy rather than episodic hiring events. Key components include: (1) an Employer Advisory Council (EAC) comprising at least 15 local employers across healthcare, IT, advanced manufacturing, and construction sectors, contributing to curriculum design, credential alignment, and the sequencing of WBL opportunities; (2) co-designed curricula and credentials with employers to ensure labor-market relevance and stackable credentials that lead to wage progression; (3) formalized MOUs that specify wage reimbursement, stipends, supervision standards, and data-sharing protocols for performance reporting; (4) a centralized Employer Partner Hub that matches participants to internships, supports on-site mentors, and provides job coaching; and (5) shared benefits for employers, including TA/LOA support, safety training, and access to a ready pipeline of motivated youth leveraging SafeReport to address workplace concerns promptly. Through this approach, ThriveUp will generate a robust, diverse employer ecosystem with a steady stream of qualified candidates and clear pipelines into unsubsidized employment or further credential attainment.

To ensure quality, fidelity, and continuous improvement, ThriveUp will implement MAP-GAP throughout program delivery. MAP-GAP (Monitoring, Assessing, Predicting—Gap analysis) provides an iterative cycle of planning, doing, studying, and acting, using real-time data to refine curricula, pacing, and supports. Data from LifeBridge community input, RPLICE adherence checks, and SafeReport safety feedback will inform adjustments to scheduling, modality (hybrid in-person/remote), and resource allocation. The Three Realities framework—Research Reality, Policy Reality, and Ground-Level Reality—guides every decision: we will align with the latest evidence on effective youth career pathways, anticipate and adapt to policy and funding shifts, and ground every component in the lived experiences and assets of the youth and communities we serve.
