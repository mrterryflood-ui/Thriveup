Context and Statement of Need

Our service region—comprising urban, suburban, and rural districts linked through the Local Workforce Development Board (LWDB)—is characterized by persistent youth unemployment and a high concentration of barriers to education and employment among ages 16–24. National data consistently show that youth in today’s labor market face disproportionate challenges compared with their older peers, including elevated unemployment rates, gaps in credentials, and limited access to paid work experiences. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that the youth unemployment rate remains higher than the overall rate, with persistent disparities across racial/ethnic groups and disability status (BLS, 2023). Youth-serving systems—schools, juvenile justice, child welfare, public health—document substantial proportions of out-of-school youth (OSY) who are disconnected from education and employment opportunities. In our region, 20–30 percent of high-school-aged youth experience at least one major barrier to employment (e.g., basic skills deficiency, housing insecurity, English-language needs). This reality translates into elevated risk of long-term labor market exclusion without coordinated, career-focused interventions that address both academic and non-academic barriers.

Evidence-based practice in youth workforce development demonstrates that integrated service models that combine education, career pathway planning, and work-based learning yield stronger credential attainment and faster pathways to employment than siloed approaches. Meta-analytic reviews of youth programs emphasize the value of comprehensive support services—tutoring, mentoring, financial literacy, and transitional guidance—paired with meaningful work experiences. The National Association of Workforce Development Professionals and research syntheses commissioned by the RAND Corporation highlight the potency of structured mentoring, industry-aligned credentials, and employer-engaged apprenticeship-like experiences for dislocated and underserved youth. Our theory of change explicitly centers this integrated approach, leveraging MAP-GAP (Monitoring, Assessing, Predicting — Gap analysis), a structured fidelity protocol (SALP), and the Three Realities framework (Research Reality, Political Reality, Ground-Level Reality) to ensure responsive and evidence-informed program design. The goal is to reverse disconnection among OSY while simultaneously maximizing outcomes measured by WIOA performance indicators: employment rates at 2 and 4 quarters post-exit, credential attainment within four quarters, measurable skill gains, and demonstrated effectiveness in serving employers. We anchor these ambitions in ThriveUp Academy’s 14-platform ecosystem, which enables a holistic set of interventions—from SEL and mental health supports to health equity, veteran and military-to-civilian transitions, and youth entrepreneurship—so every participant can access integrated services through a single, data-driven pathway.

Program Design and Theory of Change

Our program design rests on a clear, evidence-based theory of change that aligns with WIOA objectives and local need. At the core is an integrated service model that connects education, career preparation, and authentic work experiences through the 14-platform ecosystem. ThriveUp Academy provides the educational scaffolding: tutoring, credential-attainment support, concurrent education and workforce preparation, and comprehensive guidance and counseling. The Incubator translates career pathways into practical, sector-relevant trainings and project-based experiences that anchor learning in real-world contexts.
