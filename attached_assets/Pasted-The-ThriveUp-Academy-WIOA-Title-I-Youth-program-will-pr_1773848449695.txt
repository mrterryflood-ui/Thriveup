The ThriveUp WIOA Title I Youth program will provide a comprehensive, career-focused continuum of services for youth ages 16–24 who face barriers to education and employment. The program leverages ThriveUp’s 14-platform ecosystem, our MAP-GAP continuous improvement framework, the structured fidelity protocol SALP, and the Three Realities methodology to deliver all 14 required elements in an integrated, data-driven, youth-centered model. The design centers on evidence-based practices that promote accelerated credential attainment, meaningful work experiences, and sustainable transitions to postsecondary education and sustainable employment. The following narrative describes how we will implement and operationalize each of the 14 required elements in a cohesive plan that aligns with WIOA expectations, local labor market needs, and the unique competencies of ThriveUp’s ecosystem.

Context and Statement of Need
Our region faces persistent challenges for youth ages 16–24 seeking education and employment pathways. Local labor market data indicate a youth unemployment rate consistently above state benchmarks, with pronounced disparities among OSY and youth facing multiple barriers. In the county we serve, an estimated 9,500 young people are ages 16–24, of whom approximately 4,200 are out-of-school youths (OSY) and eligible for WIOA Title I services. Nearly 40% of OSY in the target area hold a high school diploma or equivalent, while 60% of OSY are either still pursuing education or are disconnected from education and workforce activities. National data corroborate that youth facing barriers—such as homelessness, foster care involvement, English-language learner status, disability, or justice-system contact—experience elevated dropout rates, inconsistent access to high-quality career pathways, and lower probability of credential attainment without targeted supports. Evidence-based practice underscores the efficacy of integrated, coordinated services that combine tutoring, work-based learning, mentoring, counseling, financial literacy, and labor market information within a career pathways framework. The plan here is to operationalize those insights through ThriveUp’s 14-platform ecosystem, which provides a robust, scalable, and evidence-informed approach to address both individual outcomes and broader workforce system outcomes.

The program design rests on a theory of change that links early engagement and academic supports to accelerated credential attainment and stable employment, mediated by meaningful work experiences and enhanced employability skills. The logic model begins with targeted outreach to OSY and in-school youth (ISY) and proceeds through a structured intake and assessment process that identifies barriers, career interests, and skill gaps. Participants then engage with a sequenced set of activities aligned with the 14 program elements: targeted tutoring and study skills, alternative schooling options when needed, paid or unpaid work experiences, occupational skills training, concurrent education with workforce preparation, leadership development, and a full package of supportive services. The theory of change posits that when youth receive integrated academic supports (tutoring, study skills, counseling), hands-on work experiences, targeted vocational training, and sustained mentoring, they achieve measurable skill gains, credential attainment, and improved employment outcomes. Our MAP-GAP framework operationalizes this change pathway by monitoring inputs, assessing progress, predicting potential barriers, and closing gaps through evidence-informed, continuous improvement loops. This approach is reinforced by Three Realities—Research Reality, Political Reality, and Ground-Level Reality—to ensure that interventions are scientifically grounded, policy-aligned, and responsive to on-the-ground circumstances, including student readiness, community context, and workforce demand. The 14-platform ecosystem serves as the implementation backbone, with each platform contributing to specific elements while ensuring interoperability, fidelity, and continuous data-informed refinement.

Program Design and Theory of Change
The program is designed around a blended learning and work-based learning model that emphasizes career pathways, credentials, and sustainment of post-exit success. At its core, we deploy a sequenced, competency-based curriculum that blends academic tutoring, career exploration, and occupation-specific training through ThriveUp’s core instructional design, the Incubator for program design and adaptation, and partner platforms that extend service delivery across the 14 elements. The evidence base for this approach draws on multiple strands of youth workforce research. First, research on career pathways demonstrates that integrating rigorous occupational training with supportive services and meaningful work experiences substantially improves credential attainment and immediate post-exit earnings. Second, meta-analytic findings on mentoring, supportive services, and labor market information indicate that coordinated, long-term mentoring and counselor-led guidance increase retention in programs and advancement to employment and postsecondary opportunities. Third, work-based learning models—whether paid internships, pre-apprenticeships, or on-the-job training (OJT)—enhance employability by building job-specific skills while reinforcing educational attainment. ThriveUp’s MAP-GAP framework codifies these evidence-informed practices into a rigorous, data-driven, continuous improvement process that actively identifies gaps in service delivery, tests targeted interventions, and scales those that prove effective.

To translate theory into practice, we operationalize the 14 elements through our ecosystem’s platforms with explicit workflows, standardized assessments, and fidelity measures. The 14-element design begins with a robust intake and assessment process that draws from a standardized set of tools to determine barrier severity, career interests, prior credentials, literacy and numeracy levels, soft skills, and readiness for work-based learning. We assign youth to defined pathways—Healthcare and Life Sciences; Advanced Manufacturing and Technology; Creative and Digital Economy; and Construction and Skilled Trades—based on the participant’s interests, aptitude, and labor market signals. Each pathway is reinforced by a set of platform-enabled activities that address the 14 elements in a coordinated manner.
