Context and Statement of Need: ThriveUp recognizes that local and national data show persistent gaps between youth potential and labor market outcomes, particularly for individuals ages 16–24 who face multiple barriers to education and work. Nationally, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that in 2023 the unemployment rate for workers aged 16–24 hovered around 9–10 percent, with persistent gaps by race, gender, and immigrant or justice-involved status; these disparities translate into lower credential attainment, reduced earnings, and diminished long-term labor market mobility. Within our service region, needs assessments conducted by the Local Workforce Development Board and ThriveUp’s analytics team indicate that OSY populations are disproportionately impacted by housing instability, inconsistent access to transportation, and limited exposure to structured career pathways. In addition, youth who are LGBTQ+, homeless or housing-insecure, or involved with the juvenile or adult justice systems experience compounding barriers that impede timely credential attainment and sustainable employment. Our analysis aligns with national evidence that youth facing multiple barriers benefit most from coordinated, multi-system approaches that combine rigorous work-based learning with supportive services, mentorship, and targeted skill development. This program’s proposed employer partnerships are designed to address these gaps by embedding high-quality work experiences within a deliberately structured, evidence-based framework. The aim is not only to place youth into jobs but to create pathways that culminate in industry-recognized credentials, measurable skill gains, and a continuum of supports that extend at least 12 months post-exit, in accordance with WIOA requirements. Grounded in the research-supported MAP-GAP framework (Monitoring, Assessing, Predicting — Gap analysis) and aligned with the SALP (Structured Adherence/Fidelity Protocol), ThriveUp’s approach integrates the Three Realities (Research Reality, Political Reality, Ground-Level Reality) to ensure that employer partnerships remain adaptive, culturally responsive, and scalable.
