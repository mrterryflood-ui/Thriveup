The GIS Maps — What They Do, How They Work, What They Tell
What they are technically: Leaflet-powered interactive maps rendered in the Community Intelligence Portal (/explore/compare and /explore/workspace). They pull real data from NCES, Census ACS, NIEER, KFF, BLS, and Perplexity-sourced research, assembled in the backend comparison engine.

What they show:

Need-heat circles — every geography you add gets a circle. Size = overall need score (0–100 composite). Color = heat (red = high need, amber = moderate, green = low). One glance tells you which county is in crisis and which isn't.
Correlation arcs — Polyline curves connecting geographies where a statistical relationship (Pearson r) exists between two metrics. If childcare desert % and child poverty rate are strongly correlated in your comparison, you see a visible arc between them. This is not decoration — it is a causal pathway signal.
The comparison matrix — side-by-side metric table behind the map tab. Metrics across rows, geographies across columns. Leader/laggard arrows. Gap column. CSV export.
The policy grid — state-level policy differences: PreK mandate type (universal, income-eligible, pilot, none), QRIS structure, subsidy FPL threshold, home visiting coverage.
The correlation graph (D3 force-directed) — geo nodes and metric nodes. Pull them apart. See which metrics cluster together. See which geographies share structural problems.
The data story — auto-generated narrative: need score ranking, biggest disparities, interstate policy gaps, strong correlations. Plain language. Exportable.
The stories they tell:

The desert story — Harris County TX shows a 68% childcare desert rate. Mecklenburg County NC shows 41%. The arc connecting desert rate to poverty rate is strong and red. The story writes itself: Harris County isn't just under-served, it's structurally trapped.
The policy gap story — Texas has income-eligible PreK. North Carolina has a universal PreK mandate. That single policy difference explains half the enrollment gap. The policy grid makes that visible in one row.
The investment ROI story — When ThriveUp's Chainweb scopes are granted, this gets dramatically more powerful: you can show the economic return on early childhood investment for each county, traced through to workforce outcomes at age 26.
The advocacy story — A United Way officer walks into a county commissioner meeting with a PNG export showing their county in red (need score 74), compared to a peer county at 51, with a sourced data story explaining the three policy differences that account for the gap. That is a grant argument, a budget argument, and a legislative argument in one image.
What it enables:

Funders making geographic investment decisions without a research team
State agencies identifying which districts need targeted intervention
Advocates walking into legislative hearings with sourced, visual, exportable evidence
Programs proving community need for new grant applications
Anyone in America — no login, no cost — checking the early childhood health of any county or district
