⚠️ Risk 1 — Geography Framing (High Priority Fix)
The pitch opens with Marcus aging out of foster care, which is vivid and right. But the pilot section reads as Texas-specific: “school-district anchor signed, community-college partner in outreach, veterans pipeline routed” with no geographic context given — and a reader who’s done basic diligence knows TCAF is Pflugerville-based.
The problem: Zero Texas grantees in 2024. If a Smart Family Fund reader mentally tags this as “Texas pilot,” you’re fighting an implicit geographic filter.
The fix: One sentence in the pilot section should nationalize the frame explicitly. Something like: “The summer pilot is anchored in Central Texas as execution proof — credential-routing logic, AI-tutor efficacy, and cohort-completion data that translate directly to DC, California, and Ohio workforce pipelines.” Name their geographies. Show Texas as the lab, not the identity.

⚠️ Risk 2 — Ask Is Unanchored (Medium Priority Fix)
The pitch closes on vision (“the chapter where a new 501(c)(3) becomes national public-good infrastructure”) but never states a number. For a rolling-deadline, relationship-driven shop, that’s sometimes fine — but not here, because they’re under capital-deployment pressure this cycle and need to know where to slot you.

🔧 Three Language Fixes
1. Cut “national 501(c)(3) determined in January 2026.” The phrase “determined in January 2026” sounds like a legal filing date, not a strength. For a funder, a brand-new 501(c)(3) is a risk flag, not a credential. Either bury it in the contact block or reframe: “TCAF received 501(c)(3) determination in January 2026 — structured from day one around implementation-science frameworks, not retrofitted to them.” That turns recency into intentionality.
2. “Four-engine AI tutor (Gemini, Claude, GPT, DeepSeek)” needs one clarifying phrase. The Smart Family Fund isn’t a tech funder. Roland will get it; Archie may not. Add: ”…four AI engines working in parallel so no single provider’s downtime interrupts a learner’s session.” Now it reads as resilience architecture, not feature-stacking.
3. The foster-youth ecosystem paragraph buries its own punch line. “A foster-youth alum aging out, a behavioral-health client stabilizing, a Spanish-monolingual newcomer settling in: each finds Trade Sims through their own door, not ours” — that line is genuinely good. But it’s mid-paragraph. It should be the paragraph’s last sentence, not the second-to-last. Move “Surgical delivery to whoever is ready, wherever they already are” to the end and let the three personas be the close.
