Five-Platform Ecosystem Integration Script
How It All Connects — No Gaps, No Dead Ends
Author: Dr. Terry Flood, DMSc — U.S. Army (20 years), Former VCL Responder Last Updated: March 2026 Purpose: This document maps how all five platforms in the veteran suicide prevention ecosystem interrelate, share users, and operate as one coordinated system. Use for grant narratives (SSG Fox, SAMHSA), investor/stakeholder conversations, partnership pitches, and internal alignment.

The Core Premise
Veteran suicide is not a single-point problem. It's a continuum — from the moment someone separates from service, through life transitions, into crisis, through stabilization, and into long-term recovery. No single app, hotline, or VA program covers the full spectrum.

This ecosystem does.

Five platforms. One mission. Each serves a distinct phase of that continuum. Together, they ensure that no matter where a veteran is — geographically, emotionally, or in their journey — there is always a next step. Never a dead end.

Platform-by-Platform: What It Is, Who It Serves, How It Connects
1. Whole-Person Health Ecosystem (Hub Platform)
URL: mentalwellnesssupport.net Role: The connective tissue of the entire ecosystem. Every other platform routes through it or links to it.

What It Does:

20,670+ verified mental health resources searchable by location, type, and population
Interactive validated screenings (C-SSRS, PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5) — all on-device, private
Safety Plan Builder (Stanley-Brown model) with offline access
Preparedness Plan — proactive pre-crisis planning with trusted advisors, personal baseline, lethal means safety
Reach a Vet — veteran-to-veteran support network with no-dead-ends 4-step safety net
MAP-GAP 7-domain whole-person assessment with personalized pathway generation
60+ evidence-based condition guides
2,091+ community support groups directory
19 population-specific hubs (Veterans, Neurodiversity, School, Recovery, Family, Adults, SDOH, etc.)
Care Summary Generator and Refer-a-Patient tool for providers
Full PWA with offline capability for safety-critical features
Quick Exit button on every page (redirects to weather.com, replaces browser history)
Who It Serves:

Veterans and service members (primary)
Military families
General population seeking mental health resources
Providers and community health workers
Researchers and public health professionals
How It Connects to Other Platforms:

Links to Mission Transition for separation/reintegration resources
Links to Life Transitions Aid for non-military life change support
Uses SALP Science frameworks for evidence-based safety assessment
Links to EasyAI Learning for youth pathways and workforce development
Veterans Hub (/veterans) serves as the entry point to the full ecosystem
Veteran Ecosystem page (/veteran-ecosystem) maps all 5 platforms visually
2. Mission Transition
URL: mission-transition--mrterryflood.replit.app Role: Covers the highest-risk period — the military-to-civilian transition.

What It Does:

Transition timeline guidance from 12 months pre-separation through 24 months post
Career translation tools (MOS/AFSC to civilian skills)
Benefits navigation (VA healthcare enrollment, GI Bill, disability claims)
Housing and financial planning resources
Community connection (veteran service organizations, peer groups)
Identity transition support (from service member to civilian identity)
Who It Serves:

Active duty service members approaching separation
Recently separated veterans (0–24 months post-service)
National Guard and Reserve members transitioning
Military spouses and families during transition
Why It Exists as a Separate Platform: The transition period has its own distinct user journey, language, and needs. A service member 6 months from ETS thinks differently than a veteran 5 years out. This platform speaks their language at that specific moment.

How It Connects to Other Platforms:

→ Whole-Person Health: Once a veteran transitions, their ongoing mental health needs are served by the main platform. Mission Transition links to safety plans, crisis tools, find-help, and community groups. Veterans who complete transition can continue their journey on the Whole-Person platform.
→ Life Transitions Aid: Transition doesn't end when you get a civilian job. Divorce, career changes, and identity struggles often follow. Mission Transition routes users to Life Transitions Aid for ongoing life-change support.
→ SALP Science: Safety assessment during transition is critical — the first 12 months post-separation have the highest suicide risk. SALP Science's risk frameworks inform the assessment tools on this platform.
→ EasyAI Learning: For transitioning service members under 25, or those with children ages 14–24, EasyAI Learning provides workforce development pathways that give purpose during a vulnerable period.
Cross-Link Implementation:

Mission Transition homepage links to Whole-Person Health crisis tools
"If you're struggling right now" banner links directly to 988 + Reach a Vet
Benefits navigation section links to Whole-Person Health's insurance guide
Career resources link to EasyAI Learning for AI workforce skills
Transition timeline includes mental health check-in prompts that link to screenings
3. Life Transitions Aid
URL: life-transitions-aid--mrterryflood.replit.app Role: Addresses the non-combat, non-transition life events that drive veteran suicide.

What It Does:

Life event support guides for divorce, job loss, retirement, health diagnosis, bereavement, financial crisis, relocation, and identity shifts
Coping strategy libraries specific to each life event type
Peer stories from veterans who navigated similar transitions
Resource matching based on current life situation
Connection to community support groups
"Where am I?" self-assessment for life satisfaction across domains
Who It Serves:

Veterans experiencing major life changes (any era, any age)
Military family members going through transitions
Anyone navigating a life event that impacts mental health
Why It Exists as a Separate Platform: 60% of veteran suicides occur among those NOT connected to VA care. Many of these veterans aren't struggling with combat trauma — they're struggling with divorce, financial ruin, identity loss, chronic illness, or aging. This platform meets them where they are, without requiring them to identify as "in crisis" or "mentally ill."

How It Connects to Other Platforms:

→ Whole-Person Health: Life Transitions Aid routes users to clinical screening tools (PHQ-9, GAD-7, C-SSRS) when their self-assessment indicates elevated risk. Links to the full resource database, safety plan, and crisis tools.
→ Mission Transition: For veterans whose life transition is specifically about leaving the military, Life Transitions Aid routes them to Mission Transition for military-specific guidance.
→ SALP Science: Research on life transitions and suicide risk informs the content and assessment tools. SALP Science provides the evidence base for which life events carry the highest risk.
→ EasyAI Learning: For those experiencing career transitions, EasyAI Learning offers a pathway to new purpose through AI education and workforce development.
Cross-Link Implementation:

Each life event guide includes a "Check in with yourself" link to screenings
"If this is more than a rough patch" section links to crisis tools and Reach a Vet
Peer stories link to Whole-Person Health community groups for ongoing support
Career transition section links to both Mission Transition and EasyAI Learning
Footer includes persistent link to 988, Whole-Person Health, and safety plan
4. SALP Science (Safety Assessment and Lethality Prevention)
URL: salp-science--mrterryflood.replit.app Role: The clinical and research backbone of the entire ecosystem.

What It Does:

Evidence-based safety assessment methodologies
Lethality risk evaluation frameworks
Prevention science research summaries and applications
Clinical protocol documentation (C-SSRS implementation, Stanley-Brown safety planning, means safety)
Implementation science frameworks (CFIR, RE-AIM, EPIS, NPT, PRISM)
Research-to-practice translation guides
Outcome measurement and evaluation tools
Grant-aligned documentation (SSG Fox, SAMHSA, CDC)
Who It Serves:

Clinicians and mental health providers
Researchers and academics
Public health professionals
Grant evaluators and funders
Organizational leadership implementing suicide prevention programs
Community health workers seeking evidence-based training
Why It Exists as a Separate Platform: The clinical rigor and research depth needed for provider-facing and grant-facing content is fundamentally different from what a veteran in crisis needs. Mixing them would dilute both. SALP Science provides the evidence that all other platforms are built on — it's the "show your work" platform.

How It Connects to Other Platforms:

→ Whole-Person Health: SALP Science's validated screening tools (C-SSRS, PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5) are implemented interactively in the Whole-Person platform. Safety plan methodology (Stanley-Brown) is directly implemented. Means safety guidance informs the Preparedness Plan. The Research Hub on the Whole-Person platform is a consumer-facing version of SALP Science content.
→ Mission Transition: SALP Science provides the research evidence for why the transition period is highest-risk and what interventions are most effective during that window.
→ Life Transitions Aid: Research on non-combat risk factors (divorce, financial crisis, identity loss) and their correlation to suicidality informs Life Transitions Aid content.
→ EasyAI Learning: Prevention science research on youth mental health and protective factors informs EasyAI Learning's approach to building resilience through purpose and workforce development.
Cross-Link Implementation:

Each research summary links to where that research is applied across the ecosystem
Clinical protocols link to their interactive implementations (e.g., C-SSRS → Screenings page)
Implementation science frameworks link to how they're applied in each platform
Grant documentation references all ecosystem platforms as the "intervention" being funded
Provider-facing content links to Whole-Person Health's provider portal and referral tools
5. EasyAI Learning
URL: easyailearning.com Role: Upstream prevention through purpose, education, and workforce development for youth.

What It Does:

AI literacy education for ages 14–24
Workforce development pathways (from beginner to job-ready)
Mentorship connections with industry professionals
Project-based learning with real-world applications
Career exploration and skills assessment
Community of learners and peer support
Accessible curriculum designed for under-resourced communities
Who It Serves:

Under-resourced youth ages 14–24
Children of veterans and military families
Young adults seeking career direction and purpose
Transitioning service members under 25 seeking new career paths
Educators and community organizations serving youth
Why It Exists as a Separate Platform: Suicide prevention isn't only about crisis intervention — it's about building a life worth living. EasyAI Learning addresses the upstream factors: purpose, skills, community, and pathways to meaningful work. For veterans' children and at-risk youth, having a future they can see and work toward is itself a protective factor against suicide.

How It Connects to Other Platforms:

→ Whole-Person Health: EasyAI Learning links to mental health resources for learners who may be struggling. The Neurodiversity Hub on Whole-Person Health provides accommodations and support strategies relevant to neurodiverse learners.
→ Mission Transition: Transitioning service members under 25 are routed to EasyAI Learning for AI career pathways as part of their transition plan.
→ Life Transitions Aid: Career transitions and job loss — at any age — can route to EasyAI Learning for reskilling and new career direction.
→ SALP Science: Research on protective factors for youth (purpose, education, employment, social connection) informs EasyAI Learning's curriculum design and mentorship model.
Cross-Link Implementation:

Mental health resource links on every learning page (not intrusive, always available)
"Struggling with more than coursework?" section links to crisis tools and Whole-Person Health
Mentor training includes QPR and Mental Health First Aid awareness
Youth community forums include moderation with crisis escalation protocols
Parent/guardian section links to Family Hub on Whole-Person Health

Shared Design Principles Across All Platforms
1. No Dead Ends
Every page on every platform has at least one forward path. No user should ever land on a page and feel stuck. If they can't find what they need, the page routes them somewhere else in the ecosystem.

2. Always a Safety Net
Every platform displays crisis resources. The 988 Veterans Crisis Line is accessible from every page. No matter which platform a user is on, they are never more than one click from crisis support.

3. Privacy First
All screening results, safety plans, and personal data stay on the user's device (localStorage). No accounts required for crisis tools. No tracking of vulnerable behavior. The platform never holds data that could be used against a user.

4. Free for Individuals
No individual user ever pays for anything on any platform. Revenue comes from B2B (provider listings, institutional partnerships, white-label licensing) and grants. Veterans, families, and youth access everything free forever.

5. Offline-Capable
Safety-critical features (safety plan, crisis contacts, preparedness plan) work offline via PWA service worker caching. A veteran in a rural area with no cell service can still access their safety plan.

6. Veteran-Informed Design
Built by a veteran (20yr Army, VCL responder). Language, tone, and design reflect military culture — direct, no-nonsense, respectful of autonomy. No clinical jargon. No condescension. No "just breathe" platitudes without substance behind them.

7. Quick Exit
Every platform includes a Quick Exit button that immediately redirects to a benign website (weather.com) and replaces browser history. For users in domestic violence situations, controlling environments, or simply needing privacy.

Journey 1: Transitioning Service Member
Entry: Mission Transition (12 months before ETS)

Builds transition timeline on Mission Transition
Completes benefits navigation checklist
Referred to Whole-Person Health for mental health screening (PHQ-9, PCL-5)
Screening suggests moderate PTSD symptoms → linked to VA mental health services via Find Help
Builds Safety Plan and Preparedness Plan on Whole-Person Health
Post-separation: career coaching on Mission Transition routes to EasyAI Learning for AI skills
6 months post-separation: going through divorce → Life Transitions Aid for divorce support
Life Transitions Aid links back to Whole-Person Health for re-screening and community groups
Joins veteran peer support group via Community Groups directory
Journey 2: Veteran Family Member
Entry: Whole-Person Health → Family Hub

Spouse searches for resources to support veteran with PTSD
Finds condition guide on PTSD, learns about CPT and PE therapy
Uses Find Help to locate couple's therapy providers in their area
Discovers Preparedness Plan → helps veteran build trusted advisor list
Learns about lethal means safety → secures firearms with VA-provided gun lock
Connects with military family support group via Community Groups
Veteran's child (age 16) → routed to EasyAI Learning for AI education and mentorship
Family uses Life Transitions Aid when veteran faces medical retirement
Journey 3: Veteran in Crisis (Not Connected to VA)
Entry: Google search → Whole-Person Health Crisis Page

Sees 988 banner immediately, but not ready to call
Uses breathing exercise on Crisis page to regulate
Sees Reach a Vet → calls trusted person from Preparedness Plan
Trusted person answers → talks them through the moment
Next day: completes C-SSRS screening → score indicates active ideation
Screening results page links directly to 988 and Reach a Vet
Uses Find Help to locate nearest VA → walks into ER
VA provider uses Care Summary (which veteran printed) to understand history
Stabilized → builds Safety Plan with therapist using the platform
Long-term: joins community group, reads peer stories, re-engages with purpose through EasyAI Learning
Journey 4: At-Risk Youth (Age 17)
Entry: EasyAI Learning (school counselor referral)

Starts AI literacy course, builds first project
Connects with mentor in AI industry

Routed to Whole-Person Health → School Hub for age-appropriate resources
Completes PHQ-9 screening → moderate depression indicated
Linked to school-based mental health services via Find Help
Parent (veteran) sees Family Hub → learns about supporting their teen
Youth continues EasyAI Learning, builds portfolio, applies for internship
Purpose and pathways serve as protective factors
Journey 5: Community Health Worker
Entry: Whole-Person Health → Providers page → CHW section

Finds training/certification resources for CHW role

Competitive Advantages as a Unified Ecosystem
No single competitor covers the full continuum. Crisis apps don't do transition. Transition apps don't do crisis. Resource directories don't do screening. This ecosystem does all of it.

Built by someone who's been on both sides. Dr. Flood has been the veteran in crisis AND the person on the other end of the line. This isn't theoretical — it's operational.

Already built and operational. This isn't a proposal. The platforms exist, the tools work, the resources are verified. Grant reviewers can visit every URL today.

Privacy-first architecture. No data leaves the user's device. No accounts required for crisis tools. In an era of data breaches and veteran distrust of systems, this is a competitive differentiator.

Targets the unreached 60%. Specifically designed for veterans NOT connected to VA care — the population where the majority of veteran suicides occur.

Multi-platform ecosystem creates compounding value. Each platform makes the others more valuable. A veteran on Mission Transition becomes a user of Whole-Person Health becomes a mentor on EasyAI Learning becomes a volunteer on Reach a Vet.
