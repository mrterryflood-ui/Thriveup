**Income inequality**
- US household income Gini coefficient hit 0.49 in 2024, up from 0.43 in 1990 — among the highest of any wealthy nation. Among OECD countries, income inequality is "very high" in the US, comparable to Costa Rica and Mexico, and low in Belgium, Czechia, Norway.
- Within the US itself: New York and DC have the widest gaps (~0.52 Gini), Utah the narrowest.

**Child poverty**
- More than 1 in 5 children across the 40 richest countries lives in poverty (69 million kids), with the US and UK singled out for particularly bad standings.
- The US ranks near the bottom of wealthy countries for child well-being overall — higher child poverty, infant mortality, and firearm deaths than peers; US kids' firearm mortality rate is over 9.5x Canada's, the next-highest peer nation.

**Homelessness**
- Despite being the world's dominant economic power, the US has one of the worst homelessness problems among wealthy nations. 771,480 people were homeless on a single night in 2024 — the highest count ever recorded by HUD.

**Healthcare cost/access — the starkest outlier**
- The US spends about $10,700 per person on healthcare — nearly double Germany ($5,700), the next-highest spender, and 3-4x South Korea or New Zealand — yet is the only country in that comparison group without universal coverage.
- The US is the only major industrialized nation that doesn't guarantee healthcare access to all citizens.
- About 66.5% of US bankruptcies are linked to medical expenses — a phenomenon that's essentially nonexistent in peer countries with universal coverage.
- Roughly 29 million Americans are one accident or major illness away from bankruptcy or homelessness.

Pattern holding steady across every category so far: the US is not uniformly bad — it's a country of extremes, where the *floor* (bottom quartile outcomes) sits far below peer nations even though the *ceiling* (top performers, top institutions, top earners) is world-class. That gap between floor and ceiling is itself the most important number for a differences-in-differences tool, more than any single national average.
