Here's the full picture of what the St. David's engine on lifetransitionsaid.org can do today, framed around your real question — finding vulnerable people and getting them across the finish line into services.

1. The 5 × 5 Coverage Map
Five Central Texas counties — Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop, Caldwell — crossed against five focus areas:

Healthcare Access (MAP, MAP-BASIC, CHIP/Medicaid, sliding-scale clinics)
Mental & Behavioral Health (Integral Care, Bluebonnet Trails, Hill Country MHDD by county)
Dental (St. David's dental partners, county FQHC dental)
Healthy Aging (Medicare counseling, AAA services, fall prevention, in-home support)
Healthy Children & Families (WIC, SNAP, Head Start, CHIP-perinatal, home visiting)
Every county/area combination has a real program list with eligibility rules, required documents, and the actual application portals. The system enforces program-county compatibility on the server — you can't accidentally enroll someone in MAP outside Travis or Hill Country MHDD outside Hays.

2. How We Identify Vulnerable Populations
This is where the platform earns its keep. Vulnerability is detected through several overlapping signals, not just one:

ZIP-to-county-to-tract resolution — every interaction (chat, intake, enrollment) gets pinned to a geography so we know if someone is in a MAP-GAP priority tract.
RPLICE intelligence pushed down to ZIP level — county profiles, MAP-GAP scores, partner saturation, and live program alerts arrive in real-time from the peer mesh, so the platform knows which neighborhoods are underserved before the person even tells us their address.
Eligibility quick-check in the wizard captures age band, household size, and income band (no PII, no SSN) — enough to surface every program they likely qualify for without scaring them off.
The 6-engine LifeGuide AI (local resource DB + Perplexity live search + ThriveUp ecosystem RAG + RPLICE planning intel + Sankofa clinical AI + GPT-4o synthesis) listens for vulnerability cues in plain conversation — housing instability, food insecurity, untreated mental health, isolation in seniors — and routes accordingly.
Crisis detection broadcasts simultaneously to the ThriveUp ecosystem and the Sankofa health mesh, so a person in acute crisis isn't sitting in just one queue.
Community Needs Assessment layers Census ACS demographics + CDC PLACES health indicators on top of the same map, so navigators can proactively work the highest-burden neighborhoods instead of waiting for walk-ins.
3. How We Move Them Into Services
Once someone is identified, the platform does four things:

Forms tab — shows them the actual program cards: who's eligible, what to bring, what to expect, and a direct link to the right county's portal in their language. No guessing which website to use.

6-step bilingual wizard — county → area → program → eligibility → contact preference → confirm. Available in English and Spanish, with language access metrics tracked across English, Spanish, Vietnamese, Chinese, and "other." Confirmation gives them an enrollment ID and clear next steps.

Navigator dashboard — CHWs see a live caseload, can update status (pending → enrolled / referred / denied / withdrew), capture receipts that auto-stamp submission timestamps, and trigger appeal workflows on denials. Every status change fires a strict outcome event to RPLICE so the network knows what actually happened to that person.

Resident-facing tracker — anyone can look up their enrollment ID and see a timeline: where their application is, what happened, what's next. If they were denied, the red Denial → Appeal panel walks them through challenging the decision instead of giving up.

4. Outcomes That Prove Impact
The Outcomes tab gives you what the foundation actually wants to see:

KPI dashboard — total residents engaged, enrollments completed, dollars of annual benefits unlocked
Year-1 progress bars against grant targets per focus area
Pipeline funnel — engagement → application → enrolled / referred / declined, so you can see where people are dropping off
Renewal matrix — who needs to recertify when, by program
Language access counts — proving the equity commitment isn't theoretical
County × area heatmap — which combinations are working and which need more navigator capacity
5. The Network Effect (No Double-Counting)
This is the part Terry has been pushing hard on, and it's now live. LifeBridge is one node in a peer network that includes the 23-platform ThriveUp ecosystem and the 4-platform Sankofa health mesh. Every enrollment outcome and every "intent" signal (someone said they need housing, someone showed up, capacity got reduced at a partner clinic) flows out to RPLICE, which rebroadcasts it to peer platforms.

When LifeBridge enrolls Maria, ThriveUp sees it as a peer-mirrored event — not a fresh person. The handshake at /api/rplice/sync exposes:

localOwned — residents we registered here
peerMirrored — residents another platform registered, that we're reflecting
networkTotal — the unified count, deduplicated by peer | resident | program | status
So when the foundation asks "how many Central Texans did the network enroll in MAP this quarter?" — there's one answer, not five conflicting ones from five platforms.

6. What This Means in Practice
A grandmother in a Caldwell County ZIP with high MAP-GAP scores chats with the assistant about her grandson's asthma medication being unaffordable. The platform: (a) recognizes she's in a high-vulnerability tract, (b) surfaces CHIP-perinatal and MAP for the parents and dental for the grandson, (c) walks her through the wizard in Spanish, (d) hands her a tracker ID, (e) tells the local CHW she exists, (f) reports the engagement to RPLICE so ThriveUp knows not to also count her, and (g) sends back grant-ready outcome data when she gets enrolled.

That's the loop — find them, qualify them, walk them through it, hand them off warmly, and prove it happened — across five counties and five focus areas, with the rest of the ecosystem in sync the entire time.
