You're onto something real, and you're not missing major prior art — but you're also not standing on a recognized doctrine. What you're describing exists in the literature as eight separate strands that nobody has yet compiled into the single sentence you just said. That's the gap.

The closest existing pieces
Strand	Field	What it gets right	What it misses
Positive Deviance (Sternin, Pascale, Singhal; Save the Children Vietnam 1990s → The Power of Positive Deviance, 2010)	Public health, organizational behavior	"Find the families/communities already succeeding against the odds and amplify what they're doing." This is the closest single-name precedent for what you said.	Treats it as a discovery method, not as the central job of the briefing apparatus. Stops short of the legalize-and-resource step.
Harm reduction (Marlatt; Strang)	Substance use, sexual health	"Meet people where they already are; legitimize the safer version of what they're already doing instead of suppressing it." Same moral architecture as your framing.	Domain-trapped in drugs/HIV. Has not generalized to a broader theory of implementation.
Community Health Workers / task-shifting (WHO; Lewin et al. Cochrane; Earth Institute 1M CHWs report)	Global health systems	Communities were already doing this work informally; the policy move was to recognize, train, pay, and integrate. Direct analog of your sentence.	Framed as a labor/workforce intervention, not as a meta-principle of how implementation should always work.
Asset-Based Community Development (McKnight & Kretzmann, 1993)	Community development	"Start from what the community already has, not what it lacks."	Doesn't connect to the policy/briefing/counterfactual machinery. Stays at the community-organizing layer.
Diffusion of Innovations — reinvention (Rogers, 1962/2003, esp. ch. 5–6)	Sociology, innovation studies	Explicitly: users modifying an innovation as they adopt it is normal, expected, and predictive of sustained adoption. Closest IS-canon match for the "adaptation in the wild is the data" idea.	Frames reinvention as adopter behavior, not as a target the formal system should chase and codify.
FRAME / FRAME-IS (Stirman 2019; Miller 2021)	Implementation science	Gives us the vocabulary for adaptations and even rates whether they preserve fidelity.	Backwards-facing: documents adaptations after the fact. Doesn't yet treat the un-documented community adaptation as the evidence input.
Street-level bureaucracy (Lipsky, 1980)	Public administration	Policy is what frontline actors actually do, not what the statute says — they're already adapting.	Descriptive, sometimes elegiac. Doesn't prescribe "and the policy apparatus should chase and ratify those adaptations."
Practice-based evidence (Green; Ammerman et al., 2014, AJPH)	Public health, primary care	Explicit inversion of EBP: practitioners are doing things that work; document them and build evidence around them.	Methodological piece. Doesn't take the policy/briefing leap you're taking.
There are two more honorable mentions — prefigurative politics in social-movement theory (communities embodying the future system before it exists) and co-production of services (Ostrom; Boyle) — but they're further from the briefing/counterfactual frame.

What's actually new in your formulation
Three things, in order of importance:

You're locating the mechanism at the briefing layer, not the intervention layer. Every literature above acts on the community or the practitioner. You're saying the scientific/policy briefing is the actor — its job is to do the seeing, the translating, and the resourcing. That re-positions implementation science as a recognition-and-ratification function rather than a design-and-deploy function. I don't see that framing cleanly stated anywhere in the IS literature.
The dignity clause is doing real work. "Bring it into the fold with dignity — not displace it." That's a normative constraint most of the strands above leave implicit. Codifying it as part of the mechanism rules out the standard failure mode where formalization captures, professionalizes, and ultimately kills the informal practice (the recurring CHW story; the harm-reduction → medicalization story; the peer-support → clinician-supervised-protocol story). Making non-displacement a definitional requirement of "this counts as implementation" is, as far as I know, original to the way you just said it.
The counterfactual claim is the bite. "That's the actual mechanism for changing the counterfactual." You're not saying this is one way to do implementation — you're saying it's the only path that actually moves the dependent variable, because any approach that displaces existing adaptation is fighting a current rather than riding one. That's a falsifiable, testable thesis. It would actually make a hell of a Q&A line on June 3 if a committee member asks how implementation science is different from program evaluation or policy design.
How this lands on your capstone
Your sign-up title is already this sentence in compressed form: "Meeting Veterans Where They Already Are: A Trial of App-Based, Community-Embedded Suicide Prevention." The 57.3% statistic + the 10-minute window are the evidence that the formal system is missing. The M2C Transition app is the seeing-and-resourcing apparatus. Veterans are already adapting — building peer networks, deferring to faith/hunting/fishing communities, declining VA care — and the policy bet is that meeting them there with a dignified, legal, resourced channel is the only thing that moves the counterfactual on suicide.
