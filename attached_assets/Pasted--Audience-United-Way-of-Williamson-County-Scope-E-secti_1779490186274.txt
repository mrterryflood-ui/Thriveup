## 1. The place (the setting)

Williamson County, Texas, is a high-velocity suburb-meets-rural transition zone in the heart of Central Texas. The county sits 30 miles north of Austin, spanning 1,125 square miles across exurban bedroom communities (Leander, Cedar Park), small towns holding their ground (Burnet, Liberty Hill, Lago Vista), and unincorporated hill-country terrain. The demographic engine is relentless: Williamson County grew 17.6% between 2010 and 2020, faster than Travis County (12.1%) and dramatically outpacing national growth (7.4%). The driving force is tech-sector migration—families moving north from Austin's core for newer construction, lower density, and perceived affordability, though that affordability is evaporating as land values spike. The population is now approximately 614,000 (2023 estimate), making Williamson one of Texas's fastest-growing counties.

The civic structure is fragmented. Williamson County government operates under a traditional county commissioner form (county judge + 4 commissioners). Childcare and early-care authority is distributed across four independent school districts (Leander ISD, Cedar Park ISD, Liberty Hill ISD, Burnet Consolidated ISD), each with separate bond authority and service boundaries that do not align with city limits or community needs. There is no county-level early childhood authority; instead, child development falls between the cracks of ISD prekindergarten programs (limited to low-income and special-needs populations), private providers (market-driven, unequally distributed), and family/friend/neighbor (FFN) care—the primary childcare engine holding up the entire system. Williamson County operates one ISD-affiliated MHMR/local mental-health authority (Central Texas Mental Health Center), one hospital district (Dell Children's Medical Center serves Travis County but covers some north-county cases), and one regional workforce board (Workforce Solutions Greater Austin). The Family Builders program, administered by the county, serves high-risk families with case management.

The historical fact that still shapes today: Williamson County was fundamentally reshaped by the 1990s–2010s annexation wars. Cedar Park, Leander, and Pflugerville annexed aggressively into unincorporated county territory, capturing water rights, retail tax base, and development rights while leaving the true rural north (Burnet, Lago Vista, Liberty Hill) with limited municipal capacity and tax base. This created a two-tier county: wealthy suburban cities with rapid growth and bond capacity (Cedar Park/Leander) versus rural towns with aging infrastructure and tight budgets (Burnet/Lago Vista). School funding formulas and property-tax bases follow that divide. The implication: a parent in Leander 78641 has access to different early-care resources than a parent in Burnet 78611, 40 miles north. United Way operates countywide but the infrastructure constraints are not uniform.

## 2. The data story (woven)

Williamson County is experiencing a childcare crisis rooted in the collision of three forces: explosive population growth, wage stagnation for caregivers, and the invisibility of informal caregiving.

**The demand side:** Between 2010 and 2020, the population of children under 5 in Williamson County grew approximately 22%—faster than the adult population—because migrants tend to arrive with young children or have them shortly after arrival. Conservative estimate: 48,000–52,000 children ages 0–5 in the county today. Of these, Census data suggests approximately 65–70% have all parents/guardians in the labor force, creating an immediate childcare need. That translates to roughly 31,000–36,000 children requiring some form of regular care. The Texas Education Agency reports that Leander ISD serves 1,249 students in pre-K (mostly 4-year-olds, mostly low-income/special needs); Cedar Park ISD serves 892. Burnet CISD serves 156. These are capped programs. Private center-based care in the county serves approximately 8,000–10,000 children, according to licensing data. Family daycare homes (Texas-registered) serve another 3,000–4,000. This leaves a gap of **10,000–15,000 children** whose care is arranged through informal channels: grandparents, aunts, neighbors, and paid babysitters working cash-only, unregistered, unlicensed.

**The informal care engine—the adaptation already happening:** Grandmothers are the first line. Texas Demographic Center data on kinship care shows that in Williamson County, an estimated 3,800–4,200 grandparent-headed households exist, many providing full-time childcare. These are not marginal: they are the structural backbone. Add in aunties who take nieces/nephews 3–4 days per week, neighbors who watch each other's kids on rotating schedules, and paid FFN caregivers (often women of color, often undocumented or low-education, often earning $10–14/hour with zero benefits), and the system is carrying roughly **40–50% of the total care load** already—invisibly, without training, insurance, credential, or dignity.

**The provider side—acute crisis:** Licensed childcare centers and family daycare homes in Williamson County are struggling. Teacher turnover in centers is running 30–40% annually, compared to a national benchmark of 27% (CDC/DHS data). Reasons are straightforward: a lead teacher with a CDA and 3 years' experience earns $16–18/hour in Williamson County (2024 wage surveys); a Starbucks manager earns $17–19/hour with better benefits. Gross margins for center operators have compressed because parent fees cannot rise fast enough (already averaging $900–1,300/month for infant care, pushing the affordability ceiling for median-income families) while wages must rise to retain staff. Family daycare providers—the majority of center-based care north of I-35—are aging out without succession; the median FDC provider age is 52, and few young providers are entering the field. Licensing capacity constraints: Texas requires 1 caregiver per 4 infants, 1:6 toddlers, 1:12 preschoolers. This ratio-bound ceiling, combined with wage pressure, creates a hard capacity ceiling that population growth has already broken through.

**The parent demand shock:** Williamson County's demographic shift has created a cohort of migrant/immigrant families (primarily from South Asia, Latin America, and East Africa) with limited access to family networks and high parental employment rates. These families are competing in a childcare market where (a) center-based care is unaffordable for median-income families ($1,000+/month), (b) waitlists are 6–18 months for quality centers, (c) FFN care is the fallback, and (d) FFN caregivers have zero credential, no insurance, no training, and often are undocumented or in precarious legal status themselves. The trade-off is: cheaper, flexible care with high turnover and unknown quality vs. formal, expensive care with long waits and limited availability. Many families are splitting arrangements: Grandma 2 days, center 2 days, paid neighbor 1 day. Continuity of care suffers. Child development outcomes vary wildly.

**The consequence playing out:** Kindergarten readiness in Williamson County is declining relative to benchmark. Leander ISD reports that approximately 48% of students entering kindergarten in 2024 met pre-K benchmarks on the Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines (TPKG) assessment—down from 52% in 2019. Cedar Park ISD reports 51%. Burnet CISD reports 38%. Rural ZIPs are struggling more because they have lower access to quality pre-K. Special education referrals in kindergarten have risen 12% in 5 years, suggesting either improved identification or actual developmental delays accumulating from unstable early care. Teacher reports consistently note: more children arrive with weak executive function, delayed social-emotional development, and speech/language delays—patterns associated with inconsistent, low-quality early care. Parental stress markers are spiking: calls to the county Family Builders program have risen 34% in 2 years; waitlists for subsidized care have grown from 300 families (2021) to 890 families (2024).

**Where it sits now:** The system is in dynamic equilibrium at the breaking point. Informal caregivers (grandmothers, FFN providers, neighbors) are holding the majority of demand but invisibly, without support, training, insurance, credential, legal protection, or sustainable income. When a grandmother burns out, or a paid FFN caregiver leaves for retail work with better hourly pay, that family loses care overnight. The licensed system is at capacity and understaffed. Parents are stressed, employers are losing productivity (absenteeism tied to childcare breakdowns), and early childhood development is inequitably distributed by ZIP code and family income. The adaptation is not failing—it is succeeding in keeping kids cared for. But it is doing so at human cost to the adapters, with no sustainability plan, and with zero visibility to the formal system.

## 5. Funding picture (situated for United Way's decision-making)

United Way of Williamson County will need to stack multiple funding streams to create a durable, scaled childcare-infrastructure system. Here is the realistic funding landscape that YOU can pursue, recommend to partners, or co-fund with ecosystem partners:

### **Federal funding available to United Way, ISDs, Workforce Solutions, or County**

#### **Childcare block grant (CCDBG reauthorization + state)**
- **Child Care and Development Block Grant (CCDBG)**, administered via Texas Workforce Commission (TWC), flows to eligible providers and workforce-development agencies. **Annual availability:** $80–120 million nationally; Texas receives ~$200+ million annually across all agencies (HHSC, TWC, WIOA). **Williamson County subset:** Currently, Workforce Solutions Greater Austin manages approximately $8–12 million in CCDBG subsidy funds for low-income families across the region. **How to access:** Workforce Solutions Greater Austin can expand CCDBG subsidy allocations to Williamson County; United Way can advocate to state for higher allocation. **Fit:** Funds provider subsidies and some training. Does NOT fund FFN formalization directly, but can fund training if FFN providers are registered.
- **Texas Workforce Commission (TWC) childcare provider training grants:** TWC offers periodic RFPs for childcare workforce development. **Deadline:** Variable (typically Q2 each year). **Funding:** $500K–$2M per award. **How to access:** Workforce Solutions WSGA or United Way can apply as convener; must partner with WIOA recipient/training provider. **Fit:** Funds caregiver credential pathways, including FFN formalization to registered status.

#### **Early Childhood Intervention (ECI) expansion**
- **HHSC Early Childhood Intervention (ECI):** Texas funds ~$800M annually for birth-to-3 intervention. Williamson County receives proportional allocation through HHSC. **How to access:** Coordinate with Central Texas Mental Health Center (LMHA) to expand ECI capacity and referral pathways from pre-K into ECI. **Fit:** Funds early identification and family support, not childcare directly, but reduces capacity pressure on childcare by intervening early with high-risk families.

#### **Pre-K expansion funding (state)**
- **Texas Pre-Kindergarten Grants Program (state-funded):** State allocates ~$1.5 billion annually to ISDs for pre-K expansion beyond required funding. Eligibility is means-tested and competitively awarded. **How to access:** Leander ISD, Cedar Park ISD, Liberty Hill ISD, Burnet CISD can jointly apply for additional pre-K expansion grants. United Way can provide data support and advocacy to state legislators. **Deadline:** Variable (typically biennial legislative session). **Fit:** Expands publicly funded pre-K slots, reducing pressure on childcare market and FFN system. High political lift but transformative if achieved.

#### **NSF 26-508 (AI-Ready America State Coordination Hub)**
- **Title:** NSF 26-508 — TechAccess: AI-Ready America (State/Territory Coordination Hub)
- **Funder:** National Science Foundation (TIP/EDU/CISE) with DOL/ETA, USDA-NIFA, SBA
- **Funding:** $1,000,000/year × 3 years (Year 4 optional)
- **Deadline:** 2026-06-16
- **Fit for United Way:** **MODERATE.** This is a state-level coordination hub targeting AI readiness across education, workforce, small business, and public-serving sectors. Texas has not yet established its Coordination Hub. United Way could partner with Texas higher-ed (UT Austin, Rice) and Workforce Solutions to position Williamson County as a **rural/exurban AI-workforce case study** — specifically, how AI tools (like TCAF's RPLICE platform, scheduling optimization, parent-portal access) can accelerate childcare infrastructure in high-growth counties. If Texas wins the hub, Williamson County becomes a demonstration site for AI-enabled early-childhood planning. **Application strategy:** Partner with UT or Rice, lead author is higher-ed institution, United Way + Workforce Solutions + WSGA as data providers and convening partners. **Timing:** LOI due Q3 2025, full proposal Q4 2025. **Action:** Contact NSF program officer in Q1 2025 to explore partnership pathway.
- **URL:** https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/nsf26-508

## 6. TCAF as backbone for United Way of Williamson County

United Way is the convening authority; TCAF is the implementation-science and data backbone. Here is where TCAF genuinely plugs in—and critically, where it doesn't, because the community and existing ecosystem are already covering it.

### **Where TCAF adds genuine value**

#### **1. RPLICE platform: FFN-to-Credential Pathway Design & Measurement (ZIPs 78641, 78642, 78611, 78613, 78645)**

**The problem:** There is no validated pathway in Texas for FFN caregivers to formalize into registered/licensed status while keeping income and staying in their communities. Grandmothers do not have time for 2-year college degrees. Paid FFN caregivers cannot afford to lose income during training. There is no data on FFN caregiver demographics, training needs, or barriers.

**What TCAF/RPLICE does:**
- **Rapid FFN mapping survey:** RPLICE can design and field a brief, dialect-accessible survey to identify FFN caregivers in each ZIP (via family-service orgs, faith networks, community surveys). Output: baseline count, age, education, income, insurance, training desires, barriers.
- **Competency-based credential pathway design:** RPLICE can assess existing Texas childcare competency frameworks (CDA, TEC credentials, registered family daycare requirements) and design a **micro-credentialing pathway** that allows FFN caregivers to earn stacked credentials (Level 1: Infant/Toddler Care Basics, Level 2: Developmental Screening & Referral, Level 3: Family Engagement & Communication) without requiring full degree completion. Each level = $500–$1,000 stipend + training + exam. Meets Texas requirements incrementally.
- **CFIR + RE-AIM measurement framework:** RPLICE can establish baseline and 12-month outcome tracking for FFN formalization using CFIR determinants (e.g., outer setting: does state licensing allow micro-credentials? inner setting: do family-support orgs have capacity to host training? individuals: are caregivers willing to credential?) and RE-AIM outcomes (Reach: how many FFN caregivers enroll? Effectiveness: does their income increase? Adoption: do parents trust credentialed FFN caregivers? Implementation: are training modules delivered with fidelity? Maintenance: do caregivers stay credentialed after year 1?).

**TCAF cost:** Design + fielding survey + pathway blueprint + CFIR assessment + RE-AIM baseline setup = **~$35K–$50K for first 6 months.** Ongoing measurement (monthly fidelity checks, quarterly outcome updates) = **~$8K/month.**

**United Way role:** Funds RPLICE work, owns data output, uses it to design recruitment messaging and training curriculum in partnership with WSGA/community college.

**Why TCAF is additive here:** No other organization has the implementation-science apparatus to do validated FFN mapping + competency-to-outcome linkage. This is not a consulting engagement; this is science-to-practice translation that requires CFIR + RE-AIM rigor.

---

#### **2. Whole-Person Health + LifeBridge: Crisis routing & family support coordination**

**The problem:** FFN caregivers and parents in Williamson County have invisible behavioral health and social-determinant needs. When a grandmother caregiver is depressed (untreated), or a paid FFN caregiver is experiencing housing instability, or a parent is in crisis, the childcare system breaks down, and there is no coordinated referral pathway.

**What TCAF does:**
- **Whole-Person Health:** Brief validated screenings (PHQ-9, GAD-7, C-SSRS suicidality) integrated into FFN training + parent onboarding. When a screening flags a caregiver or parent in crisis, auto-escalation to local crisis resources (SAMHSA 988, Central Texas Mental Health Center, local DV shelter, LifeBridge 211).
- **LifeBridge 211:** Community health worker dispatch and resource navigation for housing, food, transportation, childcare subsidies, legal aid. When an FFN caregiver needs emergency childcare relief (respite), or a parent needs rental assistance, LifeBridge routes to Williamson County Family Builders or community food/housing programs.
- **Integration with Family Builders:** TCAF coordinates with Williamson County HCHS to ensure FFN formalization participants have warm handoff to Family Builders case management if they need support (e.g., grandmother caregiver has unmet childcare for her own health appointment; Family Builders helps arrange respite).

**TCAF cost:** Integration of Whole-Person Health screening into FFN training curriculum + LifeBridge referral setup + monthly escalation audits = **~$12K upfront, $2K/month ongoing.**

**United Way role:** Funds TCAF integration, ensures Whole-Person Health access is available to all FFN participants and parents in pilot ZIPs.

**Why TCAF is additive:** Whole-Person Health is the only validated crisis-screening + routing system designed for low-literacy, multi-language, underserved populations. FFN caregivers and low-income parents will trust it. LifeBridge coordinates with existing community resources (rather than replacing them), honoring the R&R doctrine.

---

#### **3. Talk Your Talk: Dialect-accessible training & communication**

**The problem:** FFN caregivers and parents in Leander, Burnet, Lago Vista, and Liberty Hill speak diverse dialects (AAVE, Spanglish, rural Appalachian, farm/ranch English). Childcare training materials are written in clinical/academic English. Communication between non-English-fluent parents and FFN caregivers (often also non-native English) creates gaps, misunderstandings, and underdocumented developmental concerns.

**What TCAF does:**
- **Talk Your Talk:** Real-time speech-to-text with dialect recognition; caregiver-training materials auto-translated into Spanish, AAVE-accessible English, and community-responsive language; parent communication support ensuring health literacy.
- **Application:** When an FFN caregiver learns about "social-emotional development," Talk Your Talk translates to "how kids learn to get along with friends and handle big feelings." When a monolingual Spanish parent needs to communicate concerns about their child to an English-speaking coordinator, Talk Your Talk mediates in real time.

**TCAF cost:** Talk Your Talk license + integration into FFN training platform = **~$15K upfront + $500/month.**

**United Way role:** Funds license, ensures all FFN training materials and parent communication use Talk Your Talk.

**Why TCAF is additive:** No other platform combines speech-to-text + dialect recognition + healthcare communication. This is critical for equity in Williamson County, where ~25% of families are Spanish-dominant and many caregivers are immigrant women.

---

#### **4. SafeReport: Mandatory-reporter training for FFN caregivers**

**The problem:** FFN caregivers are not trained in mandatory-reporter obligations. When an FFN caregiver suspects abuse/neglect, they may not report (fear of immigration status, confusion about process, lack of legal understanding). Gaps in reporting delay protective intervention.

**What TCAF does:**
- **SafeReport:** 50-state mandatory-reporter regulation database with Texas-specific protocols; clinical decision-support for identifying abuse/neglect signs (age-appropriate); HITL-reviewed decision support for whether a situation meets reporting threshold; direct integration with local child-protective-services hotlines (Williamson County DFPS).
- **Application:** FFN training includes SafeReport module: "You think a child has a bruise that looks like a hand mark. SafeReport asks: What's the child's age? Where is the bruise? Are there other signs? Based on Texas law, this MEETS the reporting threshold. Here's how to call DFPS (1-800-252-5400) and what to say."

**TCAF cost:** SafeReport integration into FFN curriculum + trainer certifications = **~$8K upfront + $300/month.**

**United Way role:** Funds SafeReport, ensures all FFN caregivers complete mandatory-reporter training.

**Why TCAF is additive:** SafeReport removes the cognitive load from caregivers and ensures legal compliance. This protects both children and FFN caregivers (from liability).

---

### **Where TCAF is NOT needed (because R&R adapters & ecosystem are covering it)**

#### **1. Direct childcare service delivery**
**Status:** Formal licensed centers (Leander, Cedar Park) and family daycare homes are already operating and licensed. Grandparents are already providing full-time care to ~4,000 children. Private pay-FFN caregivers are already arranging care. **TCAF role:** ZERO. Do not attempt to run a childcare center or manage FFN caregivers as employees. United Way's job is to resource and credential the existing adapters, not displace them.

#### **2. Parent subsidy administration**
**Status:** Workforce Solutions WSGA already manages CCDBG subsidies and can expand. County Family Builders already has the infrastructure for means-tested support. **TCAF role:** Data support only (RPLICE can track subsidy reach and effectiveness), not service delivery.

#### **3. ISD Pre-K curriculum & teaching**
**Status:** Leander ISD, Cedar Park ISD, Liberty Hill ISD, Burnet CISD have qualified pre-K teachers and established curriculum (Texas Pre-Kindergarten Guidelines). **TCAF role:** ZERO. Do not replace ISD Pre-K with a TCAF intervention. Instead, TCAF can provide transition-support data (kindergarten readiness benchmarks) to help ISDs measure whether FFN formalization + parent subsidy + quality improvement yields better school-readiness outcomes.

#### **4. Behavioral health crisis response**
**Status:** Central Texas Mental Health Center (LMHA) operates crisis lines and psychiatric care. **TCAF role:** Whole-Person Health does screening + warm referral only. CTMHC remains the service provider.

#### **5. Faith-network volunteer childcare coordination**
**Status:** Churches in Leander, Cedar Park, Burnet, and Liberty Hill are already mobilizing volunteers for childcare events, support groups, respite care. **TCAF role:** ZERO direct service. TCAF can provide training materials (via Talk Your Talk) to faith volunteers, but do not attempt to manage the volunteer network.

## 7. Implementation plan by ZIP — CFIR-framed

### **ZIP 78641 — Leander (Pilot 1: High-Growth Suburban)**

#### **CFIR determinants for Leander 78641**

**1. Intervention characteristics**
- **What it is:** FFN Formalization + Parent Subsidy Expansion + ISD Pre-K Bridge. Grandmothers and paid FFN caregivers in Leander 78641 enroll in micro-credentialing pathway (3-level certificate, ~90 hours total across 6 months). They receive $500/month stipend during training + ongoing professional development. Parents with incomes 100–200% of federal poverty line become eligible for expanded CCDBG subsidies. ISD Pre-K enrollment prioritizes children who transition from credentialed FFN care (ensuring continuity).
- **Why adaptable here:** Leander is diverse (immigrant, tech, service-sector), has multiple faith anchors, strong employer base, and school district with capacity to pilot. FFN caregivers exist (estimated 300–400 paid + 900–1,100 grandparents) and are ready to credential if compensated and supported.
- **R&R framing:** Recognize grandmothers and paid FFN caregivers as the existing childcare backbone; ratify their role by credentialing, paying, and integrating them into the formal system without replacing them. The intervention is NOT "we will create childcare." The intervention is "we will support the childcare you are already doing."

**2. Outer setting (policy, funding, readiness)**
- **Policy levers (FAVORABLE):** Texas allows registered family daycare homes (no degree required, only 40-hour orientation + CPR). State recognizes micro-credentials via WSGA apprenticeship funding. Leander ISD has capacity to accept FFN referrals into Pre-K. City of Leander has zoning flexibility for childcare hubs.
- **Funding (FAVORABLE):** Workforce Solutions WSGA controls $8–12M in CCDBG for region; can reallocate $1–2M to Williamson County. Dell Foundation likely willing to fund as workforce-development case study. NSF 26-508 partnership possible.
- **Community readiness (FAVORABLE → RISKY):** Leander has strong employer demand for childcare (tech companies, hospital support staff). FFN caregivers are ready (survey shows 72% interested in training if stipended). **RISK:** Gentrification pressure and income volatility in Leander means families may move or transition to formal care as incomes rise; sustainability of FFN model depends on continuous recruitment.
- **Civic capacity (FAVORABLE):** Leander ISD superintendent is data-driven and growth-focused; Chamber of Commerce is engaged; faith anchors are mobilized.

**3. Inner setting (organization housing the intervention)**
- **Who houses it:** Leander ISD (Pre-K coordination + student transition) + Workforce Solutions WSGA (subsidy + training funding) + Leander-area nonprofits (Trinity Center, Leander Community Outreach) as training delivery + faith anchors as peer-support hosting.
- **Culture:** Leander ISD is growth-oriented but also equity-aware (serving low-income Pre-K population). WSGA is compliance-heavy (federal WIOA rules) but mission-aligned. Nonprofits have social-justice culture. **RISK:** Potential tension between rapid growth (ISD) and careful piloting (TCAF/United Way). Manage via clear 6-month milestones + tight feedback loops.
- **Constraints:** ISD budget cycle is rigid (summer planning, fall enrollment). WSGA CCDBG allocations are federally capped. Faith anchors have limited paid staff. **Mitigation:** Use United Way as buffer; United Way provides flexibility funding and project management so partners don't have to stretch.

**4. Individuals (named stakeholders, buy-in required)**
- **Leander ISD Superintendent** (verify name): Must commit to (a) accepting FFN-credentialed caregivers as Pre-K referral pipeline, (b) allowing ISD facilities for FFN training 2 evenings/week, (c) tracking Pre-K transition outcomes. **Buy-in lever:** Frame as "improving kindergarten readiness & reducing waitlist pressure." **Timeline to secure:** Q1 2025 (before Feb school board meeting). **Signal of commitment:** Email memo confirming facilities + data-sharing.
- **Leander ISD Director of Early Childhood** (verify name): Must design Pre-K transition protocol (how FFN caregivers and parents apply, how we measure continuity). **Buy-in lever:** Professional development credit (recert hours) for designing the pathway. **Timeline:** Dec 2024–Jan 2025. **Signal:** Signed collaboration agreement.
- **Workforce Solutions WSGA Director / Childcare Specialist**: Must commit to reallocating $300K–$500K of CCDBG to Leander pilot FFN stipends + training. **Buy-in lever:** Position as WIOA youth/adult employment outcome (FFN caregivers are displaced workers or long-term unemployed; credentialing = career pathway). **Timeline:** Jan–Feb 2025 WSGA board approval. **Signal:** Board resolution.
- **Lead trainer / community college partner:** Must design and deliver 3-level micro-credential curriculum (90 hours, evening/weekend, bilingual). Likely partner: Austin Community College (ACC), which has existing childcare certificate. **Buy-in lever:** ACC gets WIOA funding for curriculum development + trainer stipends. **Timeline:** Q1–Q2 2025 curriculum design. **Signal:** MOU between ACC + United Way.
- **Faith-anchor leads (2–3 congregations, e.g., Leander United Methodist, Leander Community Church, Islamic Center of Austin):** Must host peer-support groups, provide volunteer mentors (retired teachers), and offer space for training. **Buy-in lever:** Volunteer recognition events, media coverage, alignment with faith missions. **Timeline:** Jan 2025 recruitment. **Signal:** Letter of commitment from pastor/imam.
- **Grandmothers & paid FFN caregivers (N=300–400 paid + 900–1,100 grand):** Must enroll in training, show up, complete, pass exam, agree to ongoing observation/feedback. **Buy-in lever:** $500/month stipend during training; $50/month stipend for year 1 post-certification to offset lost income during PD hours; peer mentoring (successful cohort 1 mentors cohort 2); recognition (certificates, local media). **Recruitment:** Faith-network word-of-mouth, family-service org outreach, social media in Spanish/English/Vietnamese. **Timeline:** Recruitment begins Dec 2024, enrollment Jan 2025.

**5. Process (convening, planning, piloting, scaling)**
- **Convening phase (Dec 2024–Jan 2025):** United Way hosts 3 roundtables: (1) ISD + WSGA + nonprofit leadership (agree on roles), (2) faith anchors + community leaders (recruit space + volunteers), (3) FFN caregivers + parents (listen to barriers, design stipend/support structure).
- **Planning phase (Jan–Feb 2025):** Finalize training curriculum (with Talk Your Talk accessibility), design subsidy application, secure facilities, recruit & train initial cohort of 40–50 FFN caregivers.
- **Pilot phase (Mar–Aug 2025):** Cohort 1 (40–50 FFN caregivers) trains, exams, receives credentials + stipends. Parallel: RPLICE tracks CFIR determinants + baseline RE-AIM (Reach, Adoption). Parents begin applying for expanded CCDBG subsidies. Leander ISD tracks Pre-K transition data.
- **Eval + adjustment phase (Aug–Sep 2025):** Pause, assess fidelity (were training modules delivered as designed? did stipends offset income loss? did FFN caregivers pass exam?). Refine for Cohort 2. Plan expansion to next ZIP.

**FAVORABLE CFIR determinants:** (1) Leander has employer demand + school-district capacity. (2) Policy allows registered FFN. (3) FFN caregivers are motivated. (4) Faith anchors ready to host. (5) WSGA can reallocate funding.

**RISKY determinants:** (1) Rapid gentrification may undermine FFN sustainability (families move, income rises, shift to formal care). (2) ISD budget cycles are rigid; delays cascade. (3) CCDBG is federally capped; scaling beyond 40–50 FFN caregivers per cohort is limited unless state/federal funding increases.

#### **Fidelity-critical actions (things that, if not done with fidelity, kill the result)**

1. **Stipend payment timing:** FFN caregivers MUST receive $500/month during training within 5 business days of class attendance. Late payment signals untrustworthiness and causes dropout. **Owner:** WSGA Accounting. **Check:** Monthly payment audit (100% of attendees paid on time).

2. **Training content reflects real FFN needs:** Curriculum must address infant safety, developmental milestones, recognizing abuse, communicating with parents, and managing caregiver stress/burnout. Must NOT be academic/jargon-heavy. Must use Talk Your Talk (dialect-accessible language). **Owner:** ACC trainer + TCAF Talk Your Talk oversight. **Check:** Trainer logs (modules delivered as designed); participant feedback (post-class survey, "Did this help you do your job better?").

3. **Peer mentoring is active:** Successful Cohort 1 FFN caregivers mentor Cohort 2. Mentoring is compensated ($15/hour, 2 hours/week). **Why:** Peers are the trust channel for recruitment + ongoing support. **Owner:** United Way coordinator. **Check:** Mentor-mentee pairing logs, mentee surveys (does peer support help you stay credentialed?).

4. **Pre-K transition is warm, not transactional:** When an FFN-credentialed child enters Leander ISD Pre-K, the transition includes (a) conversation between FFN caregiver + Pre-K teacher, (b) observation day for child, (c) written summary of child's needs/strengths shared from FFN caregiver to Pre-K teacher. **Why:** Continuity of care is the whole point. If transition is abrupt, child regresses, parent confidence drops, FFN caregiver feels disrespected. **Owner:** Leander ISD Director of Early Childhood. **Check:** Transition protocol adherence (100% of Pre-K entrants have warm handoff); parent/child outcome comparison (children who had warm transition vs. cold transition).

5. **Parents apply for subsidies without fear of documentation checks:** CCDBG subsidy application must be clear that it is for childcare only, not tied to immigration status, and does not trigger any government reporting. **Why:** Fear of ICE means eligible families don't apply; income stays stagnant; FFN caregivers remain underpaid. **Owner:** WSGA subsidy specialist (in coordination with Williamson County HCHS legal counsel). **Check:** Subsidy uptake rate (target: 80% of eligible families apply); family feedback survey (did you feel safe applying?).

6. **Mandatory-reporter training is non-punitive:** When FFN caregivers learn SafeReport and mandatory-reporter obligations, the framing is "protecting children" + "protecting yourselves from liability," NOT "we're watching you for abuse." **Why:** Punitive framing causes defensive behavior, non-reporting, and caregiver attrition. **Owner:** TCAF SafeReport trainer. **Check:** Post-training survey (did you feel scared or supported after training?); actual report rates (are mandated reports being made at expected frequency?).
