# Equity-Loss Engine — Specification & Decision Log

**Status:** requirements, pre-implementation
**Scope:** domestic only (tract / city / county / state). International rows are static reference.
**Companion files:** `001_equity_schema.sql`, `atkinson.ts`, `equity-loss-engine.ts`, `atkinson.test.ts`, `seed_ihdi_reference.sql`

---

## 0. Purpose in one sentence

Compute, per US geography, the percentage of human development forfeited to inequality — using UNDP's Atkinson method — so a community sees the gap between what it could deliver and what it delivers to its actual residents.

---

## 1. Non-negotiable design rules

These exist because a specific failure mode was identified for each. Do not "simplify" them away.

| Rule | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| `rank_confidence` must be checked in the **query layer**, not the render layer | A display component written later renders a fabricated middle-of-list rank |
| `peer_class` is separate from `geography_id` | A rural tract inside a metro county gets benchmarked against its metro county average |
| Three distinct trust tiers, enumerated | A modeled number is presented with the same authority as a computed one |
| Suppress rather than estimate | A high-growth tract with no reliable mortality denominator reports suspiciously excellent life expectancy |
| Platform computes the loss; never ingests a pre-computed loss | The claim-grounding engine cannot certify a number it did not derive |
| `race_ethnicity_stratum` is a row dimension, not a footnote | Stratification is flattened the first time someone writes a naive display component |

---

## 2. The formula

UNDP IHDI, Atkinson inequality measure, inequality-aversion parameter ε = 1.

For a dimension distribution `x = {x_1 … x_n}`:

```
A_x = 1 − (geometric_mean(x) / arithmetic_mean(x))
```

Adjusted dimension index: `I*_x = I_x × (1 − A_x)`

IHDI: `IHDI = (I*_health × I*_education × I*_income)^(1/3)`

Overall loss: `Loss = 1 − [(1 − A_health)(1 − A_education)(1 − A_income)]^(1/3)`

**Property worth preserving deliberately:** the geometric mean is multiplicative. A geography cannot offset catastrophic inequality in one dimension with excellence in another. Do not substitute an arithmetic mean for "smoother" scores.

### Dimension index goalposts (UNDP defaults, configurable)

- Life expectancy index: `(LE − 20) / (85 − 20)`
- Education index: `((MYS / 15) + (EYS / 18)) / 2`
- Income index: `(ln(income) − ln(100)) / (ln(75000) − ln(100))`

Goalposts are UNDP's international scale. At sub-national US resolution nearly every geography sits in the upper part of that range, which compresses variance. **Decision required before ship:** keep UNDP goalposts (comparable to the international reference rows, low contrast) or adopt US-specific goalposts (high contrast, not comparable to Iceland's 5.0%). Default in code: UNDP goalposts, because comparability to the proven floor is the point of the product. Flag `goalpost_scheme` on every row so this can be revisited without silent breakage.

---

## 3. Known deviation from UNDP method — MUST be labeled

UNDP computes **inequality in life expectancy** from the distribution of ages at death in a population's abridged life table — inequality *within* a population.

The sub-national adaptation instead uses **geographic dispersion of life expectancy across tracts within a parent geography**. These are different quantities. A county could have low geographic dispersion and high within-population dispersion, or the reverse.

**Actions:**
1. Verify the exact UNDP health-inequality definition against HDR25 Technical Note 2 before shipping. Do not take this spec's characterization as final.
2. Carry `health_inequality_method = 'geographic_dispersion' | 'life_table_age_at_death'` on every computed row.
3. Any row using `geographic_dispersion` is **at most** trust tier `derived_with_stated_assumption`, never `computed`.

This deviation is the single largest methodological risk in the engine. It does not invalidate the approach — geographic dispersion is arguably the more policy-relevant quantity — but it means the sub-national number is **not** the same statistic as the UN's national number, and must not be presented as directly comparable without a stated caveat.

## 4. Data sources and their assigned jobs

| Dimension | Source | Grain | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income | ACS 5-year (B19001 household income distribution) | tract / place / county | distribution → A_income |
| Education | ACS 5-year (B15003 attainment) → MYS/EYS conversion | tract / place / county | distribution → A_education |
| Health — level | IHME US Health Map (county, annual) | county | current LE level |
| Health — dispersion | USALEEP (tract, 2010–2015) | tract | within-county relative dispersion |
| Peer classification | USDA ERS RUCA 2020 (tract) | tract | `peer_class` assignment |

### Source reconciliation rule (do not blend)

USALEEP and IHME are **not** averaged. They are decomposed:

```
county_LE_level      := IHME(county, most_recent_year)
within_county_shape  := relative dispersion of USALEEP tract LE within that county
estimated_tract_LE_t := county_LE_level × (usaleep_tract_LE_t / usaleep_county_mean_LE)
```

**Stated assumption, carried on every row produced this way:**
> "Within-county lifespan dispersion is assumed stable between 2010–2015 and the current IHME year. COVID-19 mortality was not evenly distributed within counties; this assumption is plausible but unverified."

Trust tier: `derived_with_stated_assumption`. Never `computed`.

### Source coverage gaps — encode as flags, never as nulls

- USALEEP excludes **Maine** and **Wisconsin** entirely (65,662 tracts = 88.7% of US tracts). Those two states get level-only, no within-county dispersion. `coverage_flag = 'usaleep_state_excluded'`.
- USALEEP excluded tracts with 6-year population < 5,000, and 764 tracts with excessive standard error. `coverage_flag = 'usaleep_tract_suppressed'`.
- Some USALEEP tract values are predicted rather than observed. Where the source distinguishes these, carry `value_basis = 'observed' | 'predicted' | 'mixed'`.
- USALEEP has had **no update since the 2010–2015 period** and is archived. `source_vintage_years_stale` should be computed and surfaced, not hidden.

## 5. Peer classification

`peer_class` answers "what should this unit be compared against," which is **not** its parent geography.

Composite key:
```
peer_class = RUCA_primary × density_band × growth_band
```

- **RUCA primary (2020 vintage)** — USDA ERS, census-tract level, classifies metropolitan / micropolitan / small-town / rural cores and their commuting areas by largest commuting flow. Secondary codes available for finer splits. Source: https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/rural-urban-commuting-area-codes
- **density_band** — quintile of population per land area (RUCA tract file ships population and land area)
- **growth_band** — decadal population change, banded

### The three simultaneous comparisons

Every unit gets computed against all three, stored as separate rows:

1. `vs_parent_county` — local inequity
2. `vs_state` — state-context inequity
3. `vs_national_peer_class` — categorical disadvantage

**Divergence between (1) and (3) is itself the finding.** A rural tract may read as deprived against its metro county and advantaged against national rural peers. Both true; different policy responses. Surface the divergence, do not average it away.

Motivating case (real, use as a fixture): **Williamson County, TX** contains northern Austin city limits, the Round Rock / Georgetown / Cedar Park / Leander suburban ring, and rural tracts. Meanwhile Austin itself spans Travis and Williamson counties. County-as-unit fails in both directions simultaneously. ERS names this exact scenario — "remote, rural communities in large metropolitan counties" — as RUCA's motivating problem.

---

## 6. Suppression rules — a visible gap beats a confident guess

Emit `suppressed` with a reason code; do **not** emit a number with a wide band.

| Condition | Reason code |
|---|---|
| tract population < 5,000 (USALEEP threshold) | `below_population_threshold` |
| decadal population growth > 25% (mortality denominator unreliable; in-migrants skew young/healthy) | `high_growth_unreliable_denominator` |
| USALEEP state excluded (ME, WI) and within-county dispersion requested | `source_coverage_gap` |
| any dimension input missing | `incomplete_dimensions` |
| ACS margin of error exceeds configured threshold on a dimension input | `moe_exceeds_threshold` |

Rationale: the same discipline that left the state-ranking middles blank rather than fabricating them.

---

## 7. Trust tiers

```
computed                      -- every input from live data, method matches UNDP, no assumption applied
derived_with_stated_assumption -- an assumption was applied; assumption_text is REQUIRED and non-empty
ai_estimate                    -- not produced by this engine; listed only to make the boundary explicit
```

Enforcement: rows with tier `derived_with_stated_assumption` and empty `assumption_text` must fail a CHECK constraint at the DB level, not a code review.

Any row using `health_inequality_method = 'geographic_dispersion'` OR the USALEEP/IHME hybrid is capped at tier 2. There is currently **no** path to tier 1 for the health dimension at sub-county grain. Say so in the UI rather than implying otherwise.

## 8. Acceptance tests

### 8.1 Formula validation (clean)

Reproduce UNDP's published US figures from UNDP's own published component inputs (HDR 2025 Statistical Annex Table 3, 2023 data):

```
HDI                 0.938
IHDI                0.832
Overall loss        11.3 %
A_health             5.5 %
A_education          2.7 %
A_income            23.9 %
```

If `computeOverallLoss(0.055, 0.027, 0.239)` returns 11.3% ± 0.05, the Atkinson/geometric-mean implementation is correct.

Additional fixtures (same table): Iceland 5.0 / Norway 6.3 / Denmark 5.5 / Czechia 5.2 / Slovenia 4.9 / OECD avg 11.4 / Very-high-HD group 10.2.

### 8.2 Health input validation (not clean — acknowledge)

8.1 validates the **formula only**. It says nothing about whether the county health input is right, because UNDP's national health inequality comes from a different pipeline than either USALEEP or IHME. There is no clean test for the health input. Do not let a green 8.1 be reported as end-to-end validation.

### 8.3 Reference-row invariance

Seeded international rows must never be mutated by any computation. Enforce with a DB-level `is_reference` boolean and a write guard.

---

## 9. Reference data — versioning

UNDP revises retroactively; the 2025 report carries 2023 data, and component values get restated across editions. Key reference rows on `hdr_edition` (e.g. `'HDR25'`), **not** on data year alone. A join on year alone will silently mix editions.

Reference rows are display-only comparison context — "the floor that's been proven achievable." They are never blended into a US geography's own computed score.

---

## 10. Build order

1. Seed international/national reference rows (`seed_ihdi_reference.sql`) — static, no dependencies
2. Implement Atkinson as a pure function (`atkinson.ts`) — testable in isolation
3. Run acceptance test 8.1 — must pass before any county number is trusted
4. Wire RUCA 2020 tract file → `peer_class` assignment
5. Wire ACS income + education distributions (already fetched per county in `community-context.ts`; extend to tract/place)
6. Wire IHME county life expectancy — **new pipeline**
7. Wire USALEEP tract dispersion — **new pipeline**, archived source, treat as fixed historical asset
8. Compose engine, register in orchestration registry
9. Only then expose to the Navigator / claim-grounding engine

Steps 1–3 are testable with no new data pipeline. Do them first.

---

## 11. Open decisions requiring a human

1. Goalpost scheme (§2) — UNDP international vs. US-specific
2. Verify UNDP health-inequality definition against Technical Note 2 (§3)
3. City geometry: census tracts nest cleanly into counties but **not** into incorporated places. City rollup needs block-group-weighted crosswalk or areal apportionment. Pick one, quantify the error once, carry it as a field. ACS publishes income and education at place level directly, so only the health dimension needs the crosswalk — that is the narrow problem.
4. High-growth suppression threshold (spec defaults to 25%; unvalidated)
5. ACS MOE threshold for suppression
