# Is America "The Greatest Country"? A Data Baseline for a National / State / Local Equity Tool

**Compiled:** August 2026
**Purpose:** Source-anchored baseline for a differences-in-differences (DiD) policy tool that lets communities see their own deficits against national and international benchmarks.

**Honest framing up front:** The verdict is *no, not across the board.* The United States leads on aggregate economic output, research universities, innovation capital, and military capacity. It trails — often finishing last among peer nations — on health, safety, mobility, and equity. The distinguishing feature is not that America is bad; it is that America has **first-world ceilings sitting on top of developing-nation floors**, and the floor/ceiling gap tracks race, geography, and access more reliably than it tracks effort or ability. That gap, not the national average, is the analytic object.

## PART 2 — THE FIVE STRUCTURAL MECHANISMS (why the floor is low)

These are the *causal levers* your DiD tool should treat as treatment variables. Each is a policy choice, not a natural condition.

### 1. Health coverage is a state-level lottery
- **10 states have never expanded Medicaid:** AL, FL, GA (partial "Pathways" only), KS, MS, SC, TN, TX, WI (partial), WY.
- ~2.9M people fall in the resulting coverage gap — too poor for marketplace subsidies, too "rich" for state Medicaid.
- Texas covers parents only to ~18% FPL. Florida ~41% FPL.
- Federal match is 90/10 — non-expansion forfeits ~$9 of federal money for every $1 of state spend.
- **Measured effect:** maternal death rates are **18–49% higher** in non-expansion states.
- **2027 shock incoming:** work requirements (80 hrs/mo) begin Jan 1, 2027 under the 2025 reconciliation law; ~10M projected to lose coverage. *This is a natural experiment your DiD tool should be pre-positioned to capture.*

### 2. School funding is tied to property values, which were set by segregation
- Districts with the most students of color receive **$2,700 less per student** in state + local funding.
- Only **18 states** give high-poverty districts even 10% more; **~1/3 give them less**.
- Along the 100 most segregating district borders, the local-revenue gap averages **$4,119 per pupil**.
- US public schools are among the **most inequitably funded of any industrialized nation**.
- Compounding effect: high-need districts need *more* per pupil to reach the same outcome, so equal funding is still unequal.

### 3. Environmental exposure tracks race independent of income
- People of color face higher PM2.5 exposure from **nearly every major emission source**, across all states, urban and rural, **and at every income level**.
- Higher-income Black Americans face greater pollution-related risk than lower-income white Americans.
- Implication for your tool: **do not use income as a proxy for exposure.** Race and geography are independent predictors.

### 4. Rural access collapse
- **~35% of US counties are maternity care deserts**, affecting 2.4M women of reproductive age and ~149,000 births/year; **60% of desert counties are rural**.
- Only **41% of rural hospitals** still offer labor & delivery; 116–139 rural L&D closures since end of 2020; 96 closures across 35 states Jan 2024–May 2026.
- Another **127 rural L&D units (13%)** are flagged at risk of closing.
- Rural patients travel 30–50+ minutes vs. <20 minutes urban.
- **Workforce cascade:** a rural hospital closure is associated with an **8.3% drop in primary care physicians** and **4.8% drop in OB-GYNs** in that county afterward. Closure is not an endpoint — it's a workforce extraction event.
- **53% of rural hospitals operate in the red in the 10 non-expansion states.**
- Rural residents face **9% greater risk of severe maternal morbidity**.

### 5. Criminal-legal system as a race-stratified institution
- Black adults imprisoned at **1,218 per 100k** vs. white **231 per 100k** — **5.3x**.
- AIAN adults: **1,045 per 100k** (4.5x white rate). Hispanic: 606 (2.6x).
- Black Americans = 13% of population, **33% of sentenced prisoners**.
- Black men born in 2001 still face **4x** the lifetime imprisonment likelihood of white peers.
- Black youth: **4x+** as likely to be detained/committed as white peers.
- Sentencing Project attributes disparity drivers to **extreme sentences for violent offenses and criminal-history-based sentencing** — both policy, both changeable.
- **Every US state, if it were a country, has an extreme incarceration rate.** Massachusetts — the *lowest* — would rank ~30th in the world, above Iran, Colombia, and all founding NATO nations.
- Cost: **~$445B/year**, up from $87B in 2015. 2.7M children have a parent incarcerated on any given day.

Where I show only top/bottom bands, **that is deliberate — I am not inventing the middle of a list I did not verify.** Fill middles directly from the linked source files before publishing.

### 3A. Overall Health (America's Health Rankings 2025 — 99 measures, 31 sources)

**Healthiest:** 1. New Hampshire · 2. Massachusetts · 3. Vermont · 4. Connecticut · 5. Utah
**Least healthy:** 50. Louisiana · 49. Arkansas · 48. Mississippi · 47. Alabama · 46. West Virginia

Louisiana ranked **50th** in Social & Economic Factors, Behaviors, and Health Outcomes; 48th Physical Environment; 38th Clinical Care — *note that its clinical care rank is far better than its outcomes rank, which is the signature of an SDOH-driven deficit, not a medicine-quality deficit.*

New Hampshire's listed **challenges** include high Black/white residential segregation and **low per capita public health funding** — proof that a #1 rank still conceals internal inequity. Your tool should surface this.

### 3D. Firearm Deaths (age-adjusted, per 100,000)

**Highest gun suicide (2024):** Wyoming 19.7 · Alaska 18.7 · Montana 16.9 · New Mexico 14.3 · Idaho 13.7
**Lowest gun suicide:** DC 1.5 · Hawaii 1.9 · New Jersey 2.1 · Massachusetts 2.3 · New York 2.3
**Lowest gun homicide:** New Hampshire 0.9 · Maine 1.2 · Rhode Island 1.2 · Idaho 1.3 · North Dakota 1.3
**Highest overall firearm mortality:** Mississippi (22.9 per 100k, 2021), then New Mexico, Alaska, Alabama, Wyoming, Louisiana

**Critical international framing:** Death rates from firearm violence in US states are **closer to rates in countries experiencing active armed conflict** than to peer nations. Nearly every US state has a higher firearm mortality rate than most other *countries* on earth.

**Policy alignment:** High-gun states show firearm homicide victimization **36x** other high-income countries; low-gun states **13.5x**. Both are catastrophic — but the 2.7x spread between them is state policy. Background-check strengthening and permit-to-purchase laws are associated in JAMA-published research with reduced firearm homicide.

### 3E. Drug Overdose (age-adjusted, per 100,000)

**Highest (2021):** West Virginia 90.9 · DC 63.6
**Lowest:** Nebraska 11.4 · South Dakota 12.6 · Iowa 15.3
**National (2021 peak era):** 32.4 → **20.9 (12 mo ending Oct 2025)** — a genuine, large improvement.

2024 saw a **~24–27% single-year national decline**, the largest ever measured, credited to naloxone access, harm reduction, safer prescribing, and prevention investment. **This is your strongest positive DiD case study in the entire dataset — a deliberate policy intervention with a measurable, rapid, attributable effect.**

But the improvement was unequal: Black Americans' 2024 overdose rate remained **36.0 per 100k — 1.51x the national average** — even after posting the *largest* percentage decline of any group. AIAN rates also remained elevated. **Equity of improvement is a separate variable from improvement.**

### 3F. Income Inequality (Gini, household income, 2024 — Census)

**Most unequal:** DC and New York (~0.52) · **Most equal:** Utah (0.42) · **US overall:** 0.49

### 3G. Incarceration
Every state is an international outlier. **Massachusetts (lowest) would rank ~30th globally as a country.** Use the *international* baseline, not the national one, when showing a community its incarceration rate — the national average is a broken yardstick.

### 3H. Rural Health Access
Track by state: % of rural hospitals retaining L&D (national: 41%); count of maternity care deserts; # at-risk L&D units (national: 127); rural hospital operating margin (53% negative in non-expansion states). Chartis 2025 State of the State publishes this state-by-state.

### 3I. Education Funding Equity
Track by state: (a) does the state give high-poverty districts ≥10% more? (only 18 do); (b) per-pupil local revenue gap across the state's most segregating district borders; (c) state share vs. local property tax share of school revenue. **Ohio** gives high-poverty districts **14% more**; **Pennsylvania gives them 11% less** — same country, opposite policy, measurable outcome divergence. That pair is a ready-made DiD comparison.

### 3J. Workforce & Labor Policy
Paid family leave benefits currently available in: CA, CO, CT, DE, DC, ME, MA, MN, NJ, NY, OR, RI, WA. Maryland begins 2028; Virginia December 2028. **Everywhere else: nothing.** Absent state policy, only **27% of workers** have paid family leave through their jobs, and **low-wage workers are 10x less likely** to have access than the highest paid. The US is the only peer nation without guaranteed paid parental leave or postpartum home visits.

## PART 4 — HOW STATES GOT HERE, AND WHAT KEEPS THEM THERE

Five self-reinforcing loops. Each is a candidate DiD specification.

**Loop 1 — Coverage → Outcomes → Provider flight → Worse coverage.**
Non-expansion → hospitals absorb uncompensated care → rural hospitals close L&D → physicians and OB-GYNs leave the county (−8.3% / −4.8%) → outcomes worsen → more closures. *Break point: expansion status. NC expanded Dec 2023 — a clean pre/post case.*

**Loop 2 — Segregation → Property value → School funding → Earnings → Segregation.**
Historic housing discrimination set district boundaries and property values → funding gaps of $2,700–$4,119/pupil → attainment gaps → earnings gaps → residential sorting reproduces the boundary. *Break point: state funding formula weighting. Compare OH vs. PA.*

**Loop 3 — Criminal-legal contact → Employment/housing/voting exclusion → Concentrated disadvantage → More contact.**
5.3x imprisonment disparity → collateral consequences → 2.7M children/day with an incarcerated parent → intergenerational transmission. *Break points: sentencing-length statutes, criminal-history enhancements, parole-grant equity.*

**Loop 4 — Siting → Exposure → Chronic disease → Cost → Reduced political capacity.**
Industrial/highway siting in communities of color → PM2.5 exposure at every income level → chronic disease burden → medical debt (66.5% of bankruptcies) → reduced wealth and civic bandwidth. *Break point: permitting and cumulative-impact review.*

**Loop 5 — Aggregate wealth as political anesthesia.**
High national GDP and elite institutions make the country legible as "greatest," which suppresses the urgency to fix the floor. The national average is the mechanism of concealment. **This is precisely what a community-facing tool defeats: it replaces the national average with the local reality.**

## PART 5 — TOOL DESIGN RECOMMENDATIONS

**Core inversion:** Don't rank places. Rank *gaps*. A community's headline number should be its distance from the best achievable benchmark, not its rank among peers who are all failing.

**Three-tier benchmark for every metric:**
1. Local value
2. National average
3. **Best-performing peer nation** (the real target)

New Hampshire's neonatal mortality of 2.0 proves the peer-nation benchmark is achievable *inside the US* — this is not utopian.

**Treatment variables (policy on/off, with dates) to encode:**
Medicaid expansion + date · Medicaid work requirements (2027) · abortion access status · paid family leave + start date · school funding weight for poverty · minimum wage · sentencing/criminal-history enhancements · permit-to-purchase and background check laws · midwifery scope of practice · rural hospital standby-capacity payment models

**Equity stratification is mandatory, not optional.** Commonwealth Fund's 2025 Scorecard already stratifies all 25 indicators by race/ethnicity within state and demonstrates why: Hispanic residents in California score 45; Hispanic residents in Texas score 6; white residents in California score 87. **Three different countries inside one nation.** Any state-level number that isn't stratified is hiding the finding.

**Natural experiments already available:**
- North Carolina Medicaid expansion (Dec 2023) — pre/post
- Medicaid work requirements (Jan 2027, 44 states) — the largest coverage shock in a decade
- Nebraska work requirements (May 1, 2026) — first mover, early signal
- 2024 overdose decline — a *successful* intervention worth reverse-engineering
- 40 states' school finance reforms 1990–2022 (reduced income-based gaps ~$1,300/pupil — but research found some reforms **worsened racial inequality**; do not assume a funding reform is an equity reform)
- Ohio vs. Pennsylvania school funding weights
- Abortion-access vs. abortion-ban states, maternal mortality

## PART 6 — WHAT THE US GENUINELY LEADS

Include these. A tool that only indicts is a tool people distrust.

Total GDP and economic output · venture capital formation and startup density · concentration of top-ranked research universities · military capability and spending · specialty and tertiary medical care at the top of the system · **life expectancy at age 80 is comparable to peer nations** (the US disadvantage is concentrated in deaths *before* old age — injuries, overdoses, homicide, infant mortality) · a demonstrated capacity for rapid course correction, as the 2024 overdose decline shows.

That last one matters most. The 27% overdose drop is proof the floor is not fixed. It moved because policy moved.

## VERIFICATION NOTES

- Middle-of-list state rankings are intentionally left to source retrieval. Do not publish an ordering I did not verify.
- Maternal mortality figures vary by measurement system (PMSS vs. NVSS vs. OECD); the US ratio ranges 17.2–26.4 depending on method and year. State comparisons must hold method constant.
- Gun homicide international comparisons vary by source (7x–26x) depending on whether the comparison set is "high-income countries," "high-income >10M population," or all 204 GBD countries. Cite the comparison set explicitly every time.
- IHME/GBD age-standardizes to the WHO World Standard Population; CDC does not by default. Do not mix.
- Incarceration: US rank moved from 1st to ~5th globally not because US rates fell dramatically but because El Salvador, Cuba, Rwanda, and Turkmenistan rose. Rate: ~541 per 100k (2022 BJS data).
- Homelessness counts across countries are not methodologically comparable; the HUD PIT count is a US-internal series.
