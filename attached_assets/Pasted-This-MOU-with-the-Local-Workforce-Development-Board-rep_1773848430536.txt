This MOU with the Local Workforce Development Board represents a formal, binding commitment to deliver WIOA Title I Youth services in a manner that is consistent with federal statute, state guidance, and the strategic priorities of ThriveUp Academy and the broader ThriveUp ecosystem. The context for this partnership reflects a persistent national challenge: although youth unemployment has declined from pandemic peaks, disparities remain acute for individuals aged 16–24 who face multiple barriers to education and employment. Nationally, youth unemployment and underemployment are disproportionately concentrated among out-of-school youth, English learners, disconnected youth, and young people with foster care histories, disabilities, or housing insecurity (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2023; U.S. Department of Education/National Center for Education Statistics, 2021). Local data for our service area indicate a persistent gap between the youth population and workforce attachment, with OSY rates significantly underrepresented in postsecondary credentials and limited access to work-based learning opportunities. The needs are not merely transactional; they are rooted in structural barriers, gaps in career readiness, and insufficient access to integrated supports that enable sustained labor market success. In response, ThriveUp Academy will deploy a holistic, data-driven, industry-informed model, anchored in MAP-GAP (Monitoring, Assessing, Predicting — Gap analysis) and SALP (Structured Adherence/Fidelity Protocol), across a 14-platform ecosystem designed to accelerate pathway advancement, credential attainment, and meaningful employment for youth ages 16–24. This approach aligns with national evidence on work-based learning, integrated education and training, and structured mentoring as high-leverage strategies for youth outcomes (MDRC and RAND scattered evidence on youth employment programs; integrated IET models; mentoring and financial literacy interventions). It leverages three core lenses—Research Reality, Political Reality, and Ground-Level Reality—to ensure that program design remains adaptive to policy shifts, funding realities, and community conditions (Three Realities framework).

From a program design perspective, the theory of change is straightforward and data-informed: when OSY and in-school youth access a coordinated set of evidence-based interventions delivered through ThriveUp’s 14-platform ecosystem, then participants will progress along clearly defined career pathways, achieve postsecondary credentials, and secure employment with sustainable earnings. The causal chain begins with targeted intake and gap analysis using MAP-GAP to identify barriers, strengths, and opportunities; this informs an Individualized Employment Plan (IEP) guided by SALP to ensure fidelity to best practices and continuous improvement.
