The following Budget and Cost Allocation narrative presents a comprehensive, evidence-based plan for deploying WIOA Title I Youth funds in a manner consistent with federal requirements, local workforce demand, and ThriveUp’s distinctive capabilities. This narrative weaves together program design, theory of change, target populations, service delivery, measurable outcomes, implementation sequencing, organizational capacity, and sustainability into a single, coherent expenditure plan. It aligns with the WIOA framework, ensuring at least 75 percent of funds serve out-of-school youth (OSY) and that no less than 20 percent of funds are dedicated to paid or unpaid work experience (WEX). The budget target supporting the program’s annual activities is $420,000, a figure calibrated to maximize impact while maintaining fiscal prudence and strong compliance with cost-sharing expectations embedded in ThriveUp’s ecosystem of partners and platforms.

Context and Statement of Need
National data illuminate the scale and persistence of barriers that OSY face in securing meaningful employment and credentials. In 2023, the national youth unemployment rate for individuals ages 16–24 hovered in the mid-to-high single digits, with persistent disparities by race, ethnicity, and region. Youth who have dropped out of school, who are English learners, or who experience housing insecurity, foster care involvement, or involvement with the juvenile justice system are disproportionately affected. For example, while aggregate youth unemployment remained elevated, historical analyses consistently show Black and Hispanic youth experiencing unemployment rates that are two to three times higher than their White peers, and OSY are markedly more likely to experience basic skills deficiencies and limited access to paid work-based learning opportunities. In parallel, high school completion rates have shown improvement, yet significant gaps remain in postsecondary readiness for many OSY, including those facing housing instability, poverty, or caregiving responsibilities. The U.S. Department of Education’s longitudinal studies and the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ occupational data underscore the need for integrated career pathways that blend education, work experiences, credential attainment, and ongoing support services.
