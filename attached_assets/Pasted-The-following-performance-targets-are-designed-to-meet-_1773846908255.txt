The following performance targets are designed to meet and exceed the six WIOA Title I Youth primary indicators while reflecting ThriveUp’s rigorous approach to youth workforce development, our distinctive ecosystem, and our commitment to continuous improvement under MAP-GAP. Grounded in national evidence on youth employment outcomes and responsive to local labor market realities, these targets are articulated through a unified theory of change that integrates our 14-platform technology ecosystem, the Structured Adherence/Fidelity Protocol (SALP), and the Three Realities framework (Research Reality, Political Reality, Ground-Level Reality). Our targets are ambitious but achievable within a robust, multi-year program design that prioritizes Out-of-School Youth (OSY) predominantly, while leveraging Work Experience (WEX) as a central lever for credential attainment and employability.

Context and Statement of Need: The landscape for youth employment in the United States remains uneven, with OSY bearing a disproportionate burden of barriers to education and labor market entry. Nationally, OSY unemployment and underemployment persist at rates substantially higher than those of their in-school peers, and persistent attainment gaps persist for youth from marginalized communities (Bureau of Labor Statistics data, 2023–2024; U.S. Department of Labor research on WIOA outcomes). In our service area, local labor market analyses indicate growing demand in healthcare support, cybersecurity, advanced manufacturing, and community health initiatives, alongside shortages of skilled workers in credentialed pathways. Compounded by housing instability, mental health stressors, and gaps in basic skills and English-language proficiency for some OSY, a comprehensive, pathway-oriented approach is essential. The evidence base demonstrates that when youth receive a coordinated mix of tutoring, occupational skills training, paid/unpaid work experiences, leadership development, financial literacy, mentoring, and robust follow-up, credentials attainment and earnings gains improve meaningfully (Education and Training Administration/DOLE empirical syntheses; Mathematica and MDRC program evaluations of WIOA Youth programs). ThriveUp’s integration of MAP-GAP with a 14-platform ecosystem and fidelity monitoring (RPLICE) enhances fidelity to best practices while enabling rapid learning cycles and tailored supports, particularly for OSY with multiple barriers, such as housing instability, disability, English-language learning needs, and prior system involvement. This Performance Targets section operationalizes these imperatives by specifying measurable outcomes, phased implementation milestones, and data-driven accountability mechanisms.

Program Design and Theory of Change: Our program design rests on a theory of change that begins with targeted outreach to OSY populations and a rigorous intake/diagnostic process (built into the ThriveUp platform and aligned with MOU requirements from the Local Workforce Development Board). Participants progress through an integrated pathway that combines 14 platform capabilities—each mapped to the 14 required youth program elements—along a ladder of increasing credential quality and labor market relevance (e.g., tutoring and study skills via Perfectly Different; occupational skills training via Sankofa Health and WholeMind Learning; education offered concurrently with workforce preparation via ThriveUp; leadership development via LifeBridge and PillScheduler; and work experience pipelines via The Incubator).
