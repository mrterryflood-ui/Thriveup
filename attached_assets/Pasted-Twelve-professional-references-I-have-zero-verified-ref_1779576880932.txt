(D) At-risk pricing — my recommendation: KEEP
Strong recommendation to keep it. Reasoning:

It cannot hurt you on Criterion V scoring as currently structured. §8.5 now explicitly separates the base PEPM (the scored number on the Response Form) from the at-risk overlay (a separate optional contract addendum). The cost formula compares your base price against competitors' base prices. The at-risk overlay is invisible to that math.
It's the credible answer to a low-cost competitor on Criteria II/III/IV. When evaluators face a $200 PEPM Omada bid vs. a $500 PEPM Vitality bid, the at-risk overlay says "the cheaper vendor isn't on the hook for outcomes; we are." That's the value-versus-price story that wins value-based public procurement.
It converts the $2.09M capture frame from rhetoric into a commitment. The biggest weakness of the executive summary's GLP-1 capture argument is that any evaluator can say "nice model, what if it doesn't work?" The at-risk overlay is the answer. Stripping it leaves the $2.09M as a marketing claim with no skin behind it.
The County has signaled it wants outcomes-based procurement. Addendum #2 explicitly named outcomes as the orientation: "a broader support-focused program centered around outcomes, health coaching participation, and sustainable lifestyle changes." An at-risk pricing structure is the contractual expression of that orientation.
It signals operator confidence in the published clinical literature. The 30% retention threshold sits well below the 55–70% managed-GLP-1 retention range. By committing to a conservative number, you're saying "we believe the published evidence applies to us." Competitors who refuse at-risk pricing are tacitly saying they don't.
The downside is bounded. 15% withhold + a 5% bonus + a termination clause for sustained underperformance — that's a structure where the maximum loss is the 15% withhold, not the entire contract. It's a measured commitment, not a betting-the-firm one.
The only case for stripping it would be if HIS or any partner is genuinely uncomfortable with 15% of revenue at risk — i.e., if cash flow makes a 15% withhold financially material. If that's the operational concern, the alternative is to soften rather than strip: drop the withhold from 15% to 7.5%, keep the same three KPI thresholds and the same outperformance bonus. That preserves 100% of the evaluator-facing signal at half the operational exposure. Strip is a strategic loss; soften is a viable middle path.

My recommendation: keep it as drafted, but be ready to negotiate the percentage down to 10% or 7.5% if the County asks. Do not strip it.
