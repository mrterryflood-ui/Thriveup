II. THE PROBLEM
The Structural Gap in Minority Business Infrastructure
Statistic	Context
9.2 million	Minority-owned businesses in the U.S. generating $1.1 trillion in annual revenue
92%	Of federal contracts go to the same 1% of large firms (GAO)
<10%	Of contracts won by minority firms, despite representing 18.3% of all U.S. businesses
Five Root Causes
1. Fragmented Discovery — Minority business owners must navigate SAM.gov, Grants.gov, APEX Accelerators, state certification programs, SBA resources, and procurement portals separately. There is no single entry point. Each system has its own registration, search paradigm, and data format. A business owner in Atlanta looking for 8(a)-eligible construction contracts must search across 4+ platforms to find what MCE surfaces on one screen.

2. Certification Complexity — 9 federal certification programs, 50+ state programs, each with different eligibility criteria, documentation requirements, and processing timelines. The 8(a) application alone runs 100+ pages. Businesses don't know which certifications they qualify for, which ones unlock the most opportunities, or how to navigate the process. MCE's eligibility wizard scores matches across all 9 federal programs in 2 minutes.

3. Proposal Capacity Gap — Government proposals require past performance narratives, capability statements, technical volumes, management plans, and cost proposals — often 50-200 pages. Small minority firms don't have dedicated proposal shops. They either spend $15K-$50K on consultants per bid or submit weak proposals. MCE's AI-powered proposal tools (dual-AI review, document generation, pre-submission checks) reduce proposal development time by 60-80% at 1/100th the cost.

4. Teaming Barriers — Large contracts require joint ventures and teaming arrangements. Minority businesses need partners with complementary certifications (e.g., an 8(a) firm teaming with a HUBZone firm). Finding those partners today requires personal networks and conference attendance. MCE's Teaming Hub makes this discoverable and systematic.

5. Intelligence Asymmetry — Enterprise contractors use GovWin ($5K-$30K/year), Bloomberg Government, and dedicated BD teams to track agency spending patterns, identify recompetes, and position for upcoming opportunities. A minority business owner with $500K in revenue can't afford these tools. MCE delivers the same intelligence at $49-$149/month.

$400+ BILLION in untapped federal contracting potential sits on the table every year because minority businesses lack the tools, connections, and competitive intelligence to win.
