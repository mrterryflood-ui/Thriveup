One clarification from the completed deeper document audit:

## “ILT of everything” status

The exact phrase **“ILT of everything architecture”** does not appear in the build book or holistic-plan documents. The closest formal architecture is:

- **the person’s story as the spine**
- **nine connected lines of effort**
- **one evidence standard**
- **one delivery rhythm**
- **one funding logic**
- feedback from community, navigation, caregiving, care delivery, research, and operations into organizational learning

Therefore, ILT should currently be treated as the name for that integrated learning-and-transformation interpretation—not as an already implemented technical subsystem.

## Confirmed implementation boundary

The deeper audit confirms the earlier conclusion:

### Implemented

- Patient-local toolkit and tracking
- Public source-linked resource catalog
- Neuro orientation
- Basic consent records
- Peer-support request intake
- Listening-study response storage and withdrawal
- Public integration-contract discovery

### Specified but not implemented

- Referral completion tracking
- Clinician acknowledgement and reconciliation
- Provenance and immutable corrections
- Caregiver delegation and permissions
- Outcome and equity measurement
- Access history
- Content-review and correction pipelines
- Community moderation and learning
- Trial enrollment and participation
- MS Center of Excellence operating workflows
- Implementation events and fidelity measures
- Weekly/monthly/quarterly operating data products
- External healthcare, research, or person-level exchange

## Precise ILT gap

The intended loop is:

> Person’s story → service response → delivery status → outcome → evidence review → organizational learning → adaptation → renewed service delivery

The current executable loop generally stops at:

> Person’s story → preparation artifact or external pathway

It does not yet systematically record:

- who accepted responsibility
- whether the service was delivered
- whether the intended outcome occurred
- why delivery failed
- what was learned
- what changed as a result
- whether that adaptation worked
- whether results differed across stakeholder groups

That is the principal architecture gap—not the absence of more informational pages.
