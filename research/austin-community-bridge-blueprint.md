# Austin Community Bridge Resource & Pilot Blueprint

**Status:** Planning and resource-refinement artifact — no application build, public launch, production change, partner data import, or stakeholder attribution is authorized by this document.
**Prepared:** August 26, 2026
**Working purpose:** Use existing ThriveUp/TCAF community intelligence, RPLICE implementation-science resources, and partner-held local knowledge to identify **Priority Opportunity Areas** and co-design a bounded, youth-inclusive evidence-based intervention (EBI) for Austin.

---

## 1. Decision in Plain Language

The proposed pilot is not a “risk scoring platform” and not another survey.

It is a **community-governed, culturally anchored action loop**:

> community conditions → evidence and lived knowledge → partner-selected intervention → accountable action → implementation → outcomes → learning

The platform’s role is to make that loop traceable, privacy-preserving, and useful. It does **not** replace resident leadership, youth organizing, cultural stewardship, City decision-making, partner service delivery, or case-management systems.

### Working name

**Austin Community Bridge**

The working name is deliberately neutral until the participating organizations decide whether it should be public-facing, co-branded, or replaced.

## 3. EBI Design: Candidate, Not Yet a Claimed Proven Program

### Working EBI

**Youth, Housing, and Cultural Continuity Navigation & Action Loop**

This is a **candidate multicomponent intervention bundle**, not a claim that a single named program already has a proven effect in Austin. It may be called an EBI only after its core components are mapped to relevant RPLICE research, their fit and feasibility are reviewed, and the community/partner governance group authorizes that description.

### Population and place

The pilot serves a **partner-confirmed Austin geography** and includes:

- Youth and young adults selected through an agreed participation approach.
- Residents and trusted community contributors across generations.
- People facing housing instability, displacement pressure, barriers to opportunity, or barriers to accessing local support, only where they choose to participate.
- Partner staff and trusted messengers who can act on validated needs.

No participant is labeled “high risk.” The unit of analysis for place-level discovery is a geography, not an individual.

### Core intervention components

| Component | What happens | Why it belongs in the candidate EBI | Fidelity requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Youth and resident co-design | Youth and residents help define questions, review findings, and select feasible action priorities. | Prevents extraction and corrects data-only interpretations. | Decision influence, consent, and recognition must be recorded. |
| 2. Structured place-level discovery | Public data and local resource context identify candidate burden and response-gap patterns. | Makes conditions reviewable without treating AI output as fact. | Every displayed metric carries source, geography, vintage, status, and limits. |
| 3. Trusted navigation and warm handoffs | A participant who requests help can be connected to an appropriate verified resource or partner workflow. | Converts insight into an immediate, person-centered next step. | No claim of availability without verified status; referral outcome remains distinct from referral creation. |
| 4. Cultural-continuity and opportunity action sessions | Partners convene around a validated priority spanning housing, business, culture/history, or infrastructure. | Keeps place, identity, and opportunity in the intervention rather than treating them as side narratives. | The community determines what is public, attributed, restricted, or not collected. |
| 5. Accountable action and learning | Named action owners track commitments, implementation barriers, outcome signals, corrections, and adaptations. | Completes the Chainweb loop and prevents engagement without follow-through. | No status may be recorded as complete without a human source or verification record. |

### Theory of change — to be tested, not asserted

If a community-governed process combines transparent place-level evidence, youth/resident co-design, trusted navigation, culturally grounded action selection, and accountable follow-through, then the pilot should show:

1. More representative participation in priority-setting;
2. Clearer links between identified needs and next actions;
3. Better visibility into service/response gaps;
4. More completed, consented referrals or action commitments; and
5. A stronger basis for deciding whether to adapt, stop, or scale.

This is an **implementation hypothesis**. It is not a claim of population-level effectiveness.

## 4. Data-Led Priority Opportunity Method

### Geographic validity protocol — required before analysis

The first Austin analysis must name **one primary reporting and decision unit**. It may use supplementary geographies only when each measure is explicitly labeled and not converted into the primary unit without a documented method.

Before any classification is run, the baseline packet must record:

1. The legally/operationally relevant boundary and boundary date (for example, City limits, a partner-defined district, or another approved unit).
2. The primary unit used for comparison and decision-making.
3. A source-by-source geography table distinguishing ZIP delivery routes, Census ZCTAs, tracts, City boundaries, counties, and Continuums of Care.
4. A crosswalk method, including whether a relationship is direct, population-weighted, areal-weighted, or **not valid for conversion**.
5. A prohibition on presenting a CoC-wide HUD PIT value as an estimate for a ZIP/ZCTA, tract, district, or City unless the source itself supports that geography.
6. A MAUP/ecological-fallacy review: the result describes conditions in a place, never the circumstances or behavior of every resident in that place.

### The two-axis model

Candidate places are reviewed on two separate axes:

1. **Community burden** — the observed or carefully labeled modeled conditions that may warrant attention.
2. **Response gap** — the mismatch between community conditions and verified, accessible response coverage.

RPLICE evidence strength and implementation feasibility are a **decision overlay**, not part of either risk score. A place does not become “more at risk” because the platform has more research about it.

### Community burden dimensions

| Dimension | Candidate measures | Primary/authoritative sources | Expected geography | Handling rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Housing and displacement pressure | Housing instability, cost burden, poverty, rent/home context, homelessness trends where applicable | Census ACS; CDC/ATSDR SVI; HUD PIT; City of Austin housing data | ZIP/ZCTA, county, City-defined geography | Do not infer an eviction, homelessness, or displacement event for any person. |
| Health and access | CDC PLACES access and health-burden measures, uninsured rate, health-resource access | CDC PLACES; Census ACS; verified local sources | County/ZIP where source allows | Display source year and do not mix incompatible geography levels without disclosure. |
| Economic mobility | Poverty, unemployment, income, education, workforce barriers | Census ACS; verified workforce/public datasets | ZIP/ZCTA, county | Use place-level aggregates only. |
| Youth opportunity | Youth-relevant homelessness trends, education/workforce context, youth-defined barriers | HUD PIT where published; Census ACS; approved local/partner sources | CoC/county/ZIP depending on source | Youth stories are never aggregated or quoted without the relevant ITI consent. |
| Physical and social infrastructure | Food access, transportation, connectivity, safe public-space and service proximity measures where verifiable | USDA/CDC/Census/City sources; verified overlays | Geography dependent | Mark unavailable fields as unavailable, not zero. |
| Culture and continuity | Community-defined cultural assets, historic places, narratives of continuity or loss | Six Square/community-curated sources and approved public records | Partner-defined | Never manufacture this dimension from demographic proxies. |

### Response-gap dimensions

| Question | Evidence required | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Is a relevant service/resource physically or digitally reachable? | Verified location/category/access information | A directory listing is not proof of accepting capacity. |
| Is the service currently available? | Partner-confirmed capacity/freshness or an explicit “unknown” state | Never represent unknown capacity as available. |
| Is the response culturally and linguistically appropriate? | Partner-provided access information and resident/youth validation | No inference from organization name, location, or demographic data. |
| Are referrals or action commitments resolving? | Consent-appropriate aggregate lifecycle/outcome data | Public output uses suppression-first aggregates; private case data never enters a public ranking. |
| Does the community identify a gap even when data suggests coverage? | Facilitated community validation with consent | Community correction is a first-class evidence type, not an error to erase. |

### What qualifies as a response-gap finding

A resource overlay, directory count, or proximity measure is **coverage context**, not a response-gap finding. A response gap can be classified only when the packet documents, for the selected service/population:

1. The relevant population/need denominator or an explicit statement that it is unknown;
2. Eligibility requirements, cost, catchment area, hours, and referral route;
3. A dated capacity, waitlist, or availability statement from a responsible provider—or an explicit unavailable/unknown status;
4. Language, disability-access, digital-access, and transportation/travel-burden considerations;
5. Community/youth validation of whether the service is actually reachable and acceptable; and
6. The source owner, collection date, confidence, and limitations.

If these conditions are not met, the output must read **“coverage context only; response gap not established.”**

### Priority classification

The first analysis should not create a single “risk score.” It should create four **restricted internal-review statuses**. No candidate classification is public until geography, method, community validation, anti-stigma review, re-identification review, claims authorization, and release approval have all passed.

| Status | Meaning | Public-use rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Review first** | Predetermined burden rule is met and a valid response-gap finding is documented; still requires partner/community review. | Restricted; never a public ranking. |
| **Community validate** | A predetermined signal rule is met but data confidence, geography fit, or response evidence fails the response-gap standard. | Restricted question for validation, not a conclusion. |
| **Monitor** | Predetermined review rules are not met with the available valid evidence. | Restricted; never describe as “safe,” “solved,” or low need. |
| **Insufficient evidence** | Source quality, freshness, denominator, geography, or response information is not adequate for classification. | Restricted; never substitute a default score. |

### Scoring rule before any calculation

### Classification protocol — required before first calculation

Before the first calculation, the governance group must approve a versioned protocol that declares:

- The baseline period, source vintage window, and rule for temporal mismatch;
- Each measure, numerator/denominator, direction, transformation, and allowed missingness;
- Burden and response-gap thresholds, confidence/uncertainty rules, and the logic for conflicting signals or ties;
- A rule that missing/invalid data becomes **insufficient evidence**, not a low burden or low gap;
- A human review sequence, required reviewers, challenge/correction route, and decision record;
- A sensitivity analysis for alternate plausible thresholds/weights; and
- A check for differences by geography, age group, language/access need, and other relevant groups without creating person-level profiles.

If a composite is eventually useful, it must also be:

- Limited to a single declared geography grain;
- Reproducible from documented inputs;
- Versioned;
- Weighted only after community/partner review;
- Accompanied by each component measure;
- Explicit about missing data;
- Tested for differences across youth/adult, geography, language/access, and other relevant groups;
- Reviewed before public release.

No composite may drive service eligibility, policing, funding denial, surveillance, or an individual-level recommendation.

## 5. RPLICE’s Required Role

RPLICE is the **evidence-to-implementation layer**, not an automated decision maker.

For every candidate priority, it should produce a review packet that answers:

1. Which intervention components have relevant research support?
2. What implementation strategies match the setting?
3. What CFIR barriers and facilitators should the group assess?
4. What RE-AIM measures distinguish reach, adoption, implementation, effectiveness, and maintenance?
5. What local adaptations are acceptable without breaking the intervention’s core elements?
6. What is still unknown or unsupported by the available evidence?

### Required RPLICE outputs before the intervention is described or operated as an EBI

- Evidence map: candidate component → study/framework/source → applicability note.
- CFIR readiness worksheet: inner setting, outer setting, individuals, intervention characteristics, implementation process.
- RE-AIM measurement plan: implementation outcomes separate from population outcomes.
- Adaptation log: what changed locally, why, who authorized it, and whether core fidelity remains intact.
- Stop/adjust/scale rules: predeclared conditions for pausing, modifying, continuing, or expanding the pilot.

### Evidence discipline

RPLICE research must be cited at the study/framework level before any outcome claim is made. The evidence packet must state population, setting, study design/limitations, core component, and local applicability for each source. If evidence supports only a component, the blueprint must say “component-informed,” not “proven program.”

## 6. Pilot Logic Model and Measures

### Inputs

- Existing public data and source provenance
- Partner-confirmed resource information
- RPLICE research and implementation-science tools
- Youth/resident co-design time and recognition
- Convening capacity
- Navigation/referral capacity
- An action owner for each selected priority

### Activities

- Build a private, source-labeled Austin data packet.
- Identify and review candidate Priority Opportunity Areas.
- Hold youth/resident/partner validation sessions.
- Select one or two feasible priorities.
- Connect voluntary requests for help to verified navigation/referral routes.
- Assign actions, implementation milestones, and review dates.

### Implementation outcomes

| RE-AIM/implementation construct | First-pilot measure | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | Who participated, by role and geography, with suppression/consent protections | Do not claim representativeness without a denominator and method. |
| Adoption | Number and type of authorized partner action owners | An invitation or meeting attendance is not adoption. |
| Implementation | Completion of agreed core components and documented adaptations | “Completed” requires an accountable human record. |
| Feasibility | Whether required data, staffing, coordination, and referral paths operated as designed | Record failures and unavailable steps. |
| Acceptability | Youth/resident/partner feedback on safety, clarity, usefulness, and burden | Feedback collection must be voluntary and consent-appropriate. |
| Maintenance readiness | Whether the group has an owner, data process, and review cadence after the pilot | Do not declare sustainability from intent alone. |

### Early outcome signals

Early signals may include:

- A documented priority selected through the agreed process;
- A verified response-gap finding;
- A completed action commitment;
- A consented referral accepted or resolved;
- A community correction incorporated into the evidence record;
- A decision to stop or adapt based on evidence.

These are **pilot learning signals**, not proof that community-level conditions have changed.

## 7. Consent, Safety, and Data Ownership

### Non-negotiable boundaries

- Public data, partner-private information, and individual information remain separate.
- Individual stories, identities, and contact information are never used in AI synthesis, public reporting, or funder materials without the specific relevant consent.
- ITI consents remain independent, default-off, revocable, and visible to the contributor through the witness loop.
- Before any youth recruitment, story collection, navigation, referral, or warm-handoff activity, the governance group must approve an age/decision-capacity protocol: youth assent, guardian permission when required, a documented lawful exception where applicable, mandatory-reporting limits, safeguarding and crisis escalation, accessible/translated consent, and a safe withdrawal route.
- Do not invite stipended participation until a real compensation workflow, eligibility explanation, payment steward, and tax/privacy handling are approved.
- Data sharing requires an approved agreement that names purpose, minimum necessary fields, roles, access controls, retention/deletion, incident response, export limits, correction handling, and what withdrawal can and cannot retract from already-aggregated or accountability records.
- A referral/warm handoff must identify the receiving organization, consent boundary, escalation path, and outcome-status owner before it is offered.
- Public reporting uses small-cell suppression and does not show individual or household records.
- Any discrepancy, correction, source challenge, or unavailable data state remains visible in the evidence record.
- AI can recommend or summarize within approved evidence; a human authorizes consequential decisions and publication.

### Data ownership questions to settle with the group

1. Who owns locally contributed data?
2. Who can correct it?
3. Who can view it?
4. What is public, partner-private, or restricted?
5. How long is it retained?
6. What happens when a contributor withdraws consent?
7. What data may be exported, and by whom?

## 8. Stakeholder Design and Red-Team

| Stakeholder seat | What they need | What can go wrong | Required design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Youth participant | Real influence, understandable consent, recognition, safety, and control of their story | Tokenism or data extraction | Shared design rights, ITI, accessible language, witness loop, compensation/recognition decision. |
| Resident/community contributor | Transparency, a pathway to action, and the ability to correct the record | Being studied without follow-through | Show action owner/status; distinguish a concern from a completed response. |
| Cultural steward | Authority over cultural knowledge and public representation | Cultural data being mined or flattened | Partner-defined public/private rules and review before publishing cultural content. |
| Service provider/CHW | Accurate referral context without excess burden or PII leakage | Unverified capacity or duplicated intake | Capacity freshness, organization boundary, and referral lifecycle integrity. |
| City/public-sector participant | Method transparency, source provenance, and usable outputs | Platform claims a mandate or replaces public process | Present as a complementary decision-support and learning layer; require formal authorization for claims. |
| Funder | Credible implementation plan and measurable learning | Population-outcome claims before evidence exists | Separate implementation evidence from outcome claims and disclose limits. |
| Platform operator | Safe, maintainable, auditable workflow | Scope explosion or unreviewed automated action | Human publication gate, source contract, and staged build approval. |

## 9. Claims Authorization and Release Protocol

Before a statement, partner name, map, resource status, evidence label, or outcome is released publicly, record:

| Required field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Claim and approved wording | Prevents a release from expanding beyond what was reviewed. |
| Claim type | Partner role, City relationship, cultural representation, data observation, resource availability, EBI evidence status, action status, or outcome. |
| Source and source date | Binds the claim to evidence. |
| Authorization owner and date | Names the person/entity that approved release authority. |
| Expiry/revalidation date | Stops stale capacity, affiliation, or outcome claims from persisting. |
| Audience and data classification | Confirms public, partner-private, or restricted handling. |
| Publication decision and accountable publisher | Creates an auditable human release record. |

No “human publication gate” is complete without this record.

## 11. Execution Sequence Before Any Build

### Stage A — Resource and source packet

1. Approve the geographic validity protocol and selected analytical unit.
2. Inventory every candidate source, its owner, freshness, grain, public/private status, numerator/denominator, and conversion limits.
3. Pull a private Austin baseline packet using only the sources valid for the chosen unit.
4. Define and approve the classification protocol before calculating any status.
5. Mark every missing, incompatible, or insufficient source as unavailable/insufficient evidence.

**Exit evidence:** a source register, geographic-validity record, classification protocol, and baseline packet that another reviewer can reproduce.

### Stage B — Evidence and EBI packet

1. Map the candidate intervention components to RPLICE research and frameworks.
2. Identify what is evidence-supported, component-informed, locally hypothesized, or unknown, including applicability limits.
3. Complete the first CFIR readiness and RE-AIM measurement worksheets.
4. Define the core components, implementation strategies, adaptation/fidelity rules, and stop/adjust/scale rules.

**Exit evidence:** a reviewed EBI packet that does not overstate the research.

### Stage C — Community and partner validation

1. Review restricted candidate findings and evidence with the appropriate group.
2. Record corrections, disagreement, cultural-protection boundaries, and action feasibility.
3. Approve youth safety/consent/compensation and partner data-sharing procedures before any collection or handoff.
4. Select one or two active pilot priorities.
5. Assign decision rights, action owners, data stewards, review cadence, and claims-release owners.

**Exit evidence:** a signed or otherwise documented authorization record for the pilot scope.

### Stage D — Build authorization

Only after Stages A–C are complete should development begin. The build scope must be limited to the approved data contract, selected geography, pages, permissions, and pilot measures.

## 12. Build Gates and Stop Conditions

### Required gates to begin development

- [ ] Geography and analytical unit approved.
- [ ] Geographic crosswalk/conversion rules and MAUP/ecological-fallacy review approved.
- [ ] Candidate source register reviewed.
- [ ] Classification thresholds, uncertainty rules, and human-review protocol approved.
- [ ] Public/private/restricted data classifications approved.
- [ ] RPLICE evidence map completed.
- [ ] Youth assent/guardian/safeguarding/accessibility/compensation model approved where relevant.
- [ ] Data-sharing, retention, withdrawal, incident, and referral-handoff agreements approved where relevant.
- [ ] Initiative name and authorized public claims approved.
- [ ] Partner roles, action owners, and correction path assigned.
- [ ] First-priority selection approved.
- [ ] Public-page accessibility and language requirements identified.
- [ ] Claims authorization and release protocol completed for every proposed public claim.

### Stop or hold conditions

Pause the pilot or public release if:

- The analysis cannot distinguish observed data from modeled or unavailable data.
- The chosen source cannot support the selected geographic unit, or a crosswalk/conversion is not valid.
- The classification protocol, uncertainty handling, or challenge process has not been approved.
- A source is stale, geographically incompatible, or unauthorized for the intended display.
- Capacity is unknown but the page would imply an available service.
- Youth/resident participation lacks the required consent/assent, safeguarding, accessibility, compensation, or meaningful decision influence.
- Cultural knowledge is requested for display without steward approval.
- A claimed partner role, City relationship, or outcome cannot be verified.
- The group cannot name an accountable action owner for a selected priority.
