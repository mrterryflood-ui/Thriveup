## 0. THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION — Contracts vs. Grants vs. Cooperative Agreements

This distinction governs which rulebook applies. Getting it wrong invalidates your budget structure, your certifications, and your proposal format.

| Instrument | Governing Rule | When It Applies | Federal Benefit To |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grant** | 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance) | Funds transferred to accomplish a public purpose; agency does NOT directly receive goods/services | The public / community |
| **Cooperative Agreement** | 2 CFR Part 200 + agency supplements | Like a grant but with "substantial federal involvement" in program execution | The public, with federal co-direction |
| **Contract** | FAR (48 CFR Chapters 1–99) | Agency acquires goods or services for its OWN direct use/benefit | The federal agency itself |
| **Other Transaction (OT)** | Agency-specific authority (DoD, DHS, etc.) | R&D, prototypes; not bound by FAR or 2 CFR 200 | Agency / defense modernization |

**TCAF rule:** SSG Fox, SAMHSA, NIH, Promise Neighborhoods = GRANTS → 2 CFR 200. Any VA/DoD/HHS service contract = CONTRACTS → FAR. Always identify the instrument before writing the budget or the narrative.

**Key signal in the solicitation:**
- Says "NOFO" (Notice of Funding Opportunity) or "FOA" → Grant / Co-op Agreement
- Says "RFP," "RFQ," "IFB," or "Solicitation" and references FAR clauses (52.xxx) → Contract

## 3. SDVOSB — Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (TCAF's Biggest Advantage)

**Dr. Terry Flood is a medically retired U.S. Army veteran with a service-connected disability (MS diagnosis at retirement).** This makes TCAF presumptively eligible for SDVOSB certification — the single most powerful contracting advantage available.

### Why SDVOSB matters

- **VA Veterans First Contracting Program (38 U.S.C. § 8127–8128):** The VA is *required by statute* to award contracts to SDVOSBs when two or more SDVOSB firms can perform the work at a fair and reasonable price. VA must exhaust SDVOSB set-asides before competing the contract broadly.
- **FAR 19.1405 / VAAR 819.7005:** FAR and VA-specific regulations mandate SDVOSB set-asides for VA acquisitions above the micro-purchase threshold ($10,000) where two or more qualified SDVOSBs can compete.
- **GovernmentWide:** All federal agencies apply small business set-aside preferences; SDVOSB is a named preference category across DoD, HHS, DoL, and others.
- **Sole-source authority (FAR 19.1406):** VA may sole-source up to $5M (services) or $7M (manufacturing) to a certified SDVOSB without competition.
- **Annual federal SDVOSB contracting goal:** 3% of all prime contract dollars governmentwide.

### Certification path (SBA VTAP — as of 2023 reform)

The SBA Veterans Small Business Certification (VetCert) program replaced VA's CVE program in 2023.

1. **Verify eligibility:** (a) veteran with service-connected disability rating from VA ≥ 0%; (b) unconditional ownership of ≥51% of firm; (c) management and daily operations controlled by the veteran or another veteran (economic interest + operational control); (d) small business under primary NAICS.
2. **Gather documents:** DD-214 (Certificate of Release or Discharge) · VA disability rating letter · TCAF 501(c)(3) Articles of Incorporation + bylaws · SAM.gov active registration · Operating agreement or bylaws showing ownership structure · Personal financial statements (Dr. Flood).
3. **Apply:** certify.sba.gov → "Veteran-Owned Small Business Certification" → "Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business."
4. **Timeline:** SBA targets 90-day processing; plan for 120 days.
5. **Maintain:** Annual attestation; notify SBA of material changes within 30 days.

### TCAF Action Item — SDVOSB
- **File SDVOSB application immediately.** This takes 90–120 days. Every day without certification is a day TCAF cannot compete for SDVOSB set-asides.
- Pull Dr. Flood's VA disability rating letter from VA.gov or ebenefits.va.gov.
- Pull DD-214 (should be on file; certified copy may be needed).
- Once certified, add SDVOSB notation to all SAM representations, all proposals, and all capability statements.

### FAR Part 2 — Definitions (Know These Cold)

| Term | Definition | TCAF Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Solicitation** | Document requesting offers (RFP, RFQ, IFB) | Read every solicitation carefully; note amendment dates |
| **RFP** | Request for Proposals — negotiated; best value | TCAF's primary vehicle; technical + price factors |
| **RFQ** | Request for Quotations — simplified; no binding offer | Used for purchases ≤$250K; quote-based |
| **IFB** | Invitation for Bids — sealed; lowest price wins | Price-only competition; avoid when possible |
| **Offeror** | Entity submitting an offer in response to an RFP | TCAF in any proposal submission |
| **Contractor** | Entity that has a contract with the government | TCAF post-award |
| **Contracting Officer (CO)** | Authority to award/modify contracts | The only person whose instructions bind the government |
| **COR** | Contracting Officer's Representative | Day-to-day technical point of contact post-award |
| **PWS** | Performance Work Statement — output-focused | Preferred for service contracts; ties payment to outcomes |
| **SOW** | Statement of Work — input-focused | Describes tasks; less flexible than PWS |
| **SOO** | Statement of Objectives — goals only; offeror designs approach | Gives TCAF maximum latitude to propose its own solution |
| **LPTA** | Lowest Price Technically Acceptable | Price wins once technical threshold is met; avoid if possible |
| **Best Value** | Technical + price tradeoff | TCAF's competitive sweet spot — experience + innovation justify price premium |

| **Micro-purchase threshold** | $10,000 | Below this = no competition required |
| **Simplified Acquisition threshold** | $250,000 | Above = more complex requirements |

### FAR Part 9 — Contractor Qualifications

**Responsibility:** Before award, the CO must determine TCAF is a "responsible" contractor — meaning:
- Financial resources to perform
- Satisfactory record of past performance
- Satisfactory record of integrity and business ethics
- Necessary facilities, equipment, and technical skills
- Not debarred/suspended/proposed for debarment

**TCAF actions:**
1. Maintain SAM registration (required)
2. Document past performance on every completed grant and contract — dollar value, scope, POC, outcome
3. Never misrepresent capabilities; disclose limitations proactively
4. If new (no federal past performance): use analogous experience (federal grants, state/local contracts, Dr. Flood's personal federal employment track record)

**Past Performance Information Retrieval System (PPIRS/CPARS):** Federal contractors are rated by COs after every contract. Ratings feed into future evaluations. Treat every contract as a CPARS investment.

TCAF's peer navigation, training, workforce development, and platform services are "commercial items" (sold in the commercial marketplace). FAR Part 12 simplifies the contracting approach:

### FAR Part 13 — Simplified Acquisition Procedures (SAP)

For contracts ≤**$250,000** (SAT threshold):
- Less documentation required
- Faster award timelines
- Quote-based (no formal proposal required)
- Preferred competition method: 3 quotes minimum

**TCAF opportunity:** Many local VA, DoD, and HHS task orders fall under SAT. SDVOSB set-asides under SAT are aggressively used by VA. These are fast, lower-competition vehicles.

### FAR Part 15 — Contracting by Negotiation (THE CORE FOR PROPOSALS)

This is the rulebook for every competitive RFP. Master this.

**Solicitation structure (Section Letters — know them):**

| Section | Content | TCAF Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Solicitation/Contract Form (SF 33/1449) | Cover page; note contract type, NAICS, set-aside |
| **B** | Supplies or Services and Prices | CLIN structure; price/cost breakdown; hourly rates |
| **C** | Description/Specs/Work Statement (PWS/SOW/SOO) | THE REQUIREMENT — read every word 3× |
| **D** | Packaging and Marking | Low impact for services |
| **E** | Inspection and Acceptance | Quality standards; deliverable acceptance |
| **F** | Deliveries or Performance | Period of performance; locations; reporting requirements |
| **G** | Contract Administration Data | COR name; invoice instructions; reporting formats |
| **H** | Special Contract Requirements | Agency-specific terms; often critical |
| **I** | Contract Clauses (52.xxx) | FAR and agency supplement clauses incorporated |
| **J** | Attachments | PWS addenda, wage determinations, representations |
| **K** | Representations, Certifications and Other Statements | Sign these; SDVOSB/small biz certifications here |
| **L** | Instructions, Conditions, and Notices to Offerors | **HOW to respond — page limits, format, volumes** |
| **M** | Evaluation Factors for Award | **HOW you will be scored — weight, criteria, LPTA vs. best value** |

**Iron Rule applies here:** Section M is the scoring rubric. Section L is the instructions. Write every word of the proposal to Section M, not Section C. Mirror the evaluation factor language verbatim. This is identical to Iron Rule #5 (RFP Fidelity Doctrine) — it was built from FAR 15 logic.

**Past performance under FAR 15.305:**
- Evaluated on relevance (scope, magnitude, complexity) and quality (CPARS ratings or references)
- "No past performance" = neutral, not negative (for new offerors)
- Always provide analogous experience: federal grants count, Dr. Flood's VA/DoD employment counts if properly framed

**Oral presentations (FAR 15.102):** Some solicitations require oral presentations in lieu of or supplement to written proposals. Prepare Dr. Flood as primary presenter; Crystal Correa for financial questions.

**Best and Final Offer (BAFO):** If government issues a BAFO request after initial evaluation, TCAF can revise its technical approach and price. Never leave BAFO on the table.

### FAR Part 16 — Types of Contracts

| Contract Type | How It Works | TCAF Risk | When to Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FFP (Firm-Fixed-Price)** | Fixed price regardless of actual cost | TCAF bears cost risk | When scope is well-defined; maximize efficiency |
| **T&M (Time-and-Materials)** | Hourly labor rates + materials at cost | Government bears risk above ceiling | When scope is uncertain; common for consulting |
| **CPFF (Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee)** | Actual allowable costs + fixed fee (profit) | Government bears cost risk; requires cost accounting | Research; when costs can't be estimated reliably |
| **IDIQ (Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity)** | Task/delivery orders against master contract | TCAF guarantees min order; no guaranteed max | GSA Schedules, GWACs; pursue for recurring work |
| **BPA (Blanket Purchase Agreement)** | Simplified ordering from pre-vetted vendor | Low risk; no commitment until order placed | Relationship-based; good for follow-on work |
| **GWAC (Governmentwide Acquisition Contract)** | Multi-agency IDIQ; access across all agencies | Competitive to get on; powerful once on | Long-term strategy: Alliant 2, OASIS+, 8(a) STARS III |

**TCAF strategy:** Pursue FFP and T&M contracts initially. Long-term: get on at least one IDIQ/GWAC vehicle (OASIS+ small business pool or 8(a) STARS III if 8(a) eligible).

### FAR Part 22 — Labor Laws (Service Contract Act)

**The Service Contract Act (SCA) / McNamara-O'Hara Service Contract Act** applies to most federal service contracts over $2,500 with labor. Key rules:
- Workers must be paid at least the **wage determination** rate for their occupation and location
- Wage determinations are published on sam.gov (Wage Determinations Online, WDOL)
- Health and welfare benefits: currently $4.93/hour minimum
- Vacation: minimum 1 week/year for employees with ≤5 years service; 2 weeks for 5+ years
- Paid holidays: 11 per year minimum

**TCAF action:** Every service contract solicitation includes an SCA wage determination in Section J. Before pricing, look up the wage determination for Travis County TX (or relevant location) for your specific occupational codes. Under-pricing SCA requirements is a compliance violation.

**Common SCA job codes for TCAF:**
- 01312 — Health Aid (peer navigator equivalent)
- 01620 — Social Services Aide
- 10030 — Administrative Assistant
- 21020 — Program Manager (Dr. Flood role)

### FAR Part 31 — Contract Cost Principles (Allowable vs. Unallowable Costs)

**This is the budget bible for cost-reimbursement contracts AND informs grant budgets under 2 CFR 200.**

**General allowability test (FAR 31.201-2):** A cost is allowable only if it is:
1. Reasonable — what a prudent person would pay
2. Allocable — benefits the contract
3. Complies with FAR 31 and applicable CAS (Cost Accounting Standards)
4. Consistent with terms of the contract

**Key allowable costs for TCAF:**

| Cost Category | Allowable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Salaries / wages | YES | Must be consistent with established TCAF policy; no windfalls |
| Fringe benefits | YES | FICA, health, retirement, PTO — must be documented policy |
| Travel (per FAR 31.205-46) | YES | Actual cost; lodging ≤GSA per diem; meals ≤M&IE rate; coach airfare |
| Subcontractor costs | YES | Must flow down FAR requirements; cost/price analysis required |
| Materials and supplies | YES | Only items directly used for the contract |
| Consulting fees | YES | Must be reasonable, documented scope, arm's-length |
| Indirect / overhead / G&A | YES | Must be based on disclosed and consistently applied methodology |
| Training (job-related) | YES | Required for contract performance |
| Equipment rental | YES | At market rate |
| IT / software (contract-related) | YES | Allocate reasonably if shared |
| Advertising (recruitment) | YES | Help-wanted ads for contract staff |

**Always unallowable (FAR 31.205 series):**

| Cost | FAR Cite | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Entertainment | 31.205-14 | Tickets, shows, social events — always unallowable |
| Alcohol | 31.205-14 | No exceptions |
| Lobbying | 31.205-22 | Influencing legislation; public relations to promote company |
| Fines and penalties | 31.205-15 | Including interest on late payments to government |

**TCAF rule:** Build every federal budget starting from FAR 31 allowability. If in doubt, ask "Would a government auditor approve this?" before including it.

### FAR Part 37 — Service Contracting

TCAF is a service contractor. Key rules:
- **Advisory and Assistance Services (A&AS):** Often the contract type for TCAF's consulting, training, and program implementation work. Requires COR oversight.
- **Personal vs. Non-Personal Services:** The government cannot use service contracts to create an employer-employee relationship. TCAF staff take direction from Dr. Flood, not the government COR, on *how* work is done (COR directs *what* deliverables, not *how*).
- **Inherently Governmental Functions:** Cannot be contracted out. TCAF cannot make policy decisions, sign regulations, or obligate government funds.

## 7. Agency Supplements (Critical for TCAF's Primary Agencies)

FAR is the floor. Each agency adds its own supplement on top. Always read BOTH.

### VAAR — VA Acquisition Regulation (48 CFR Chapter 8)

**This is TCAF's most important supplement.** VA is TCAF's primary federal customer.

- **VAAR 819 (Small Business Programs):** Implements Veterans First Contracting. SDVOSB > VOSB > other small business preference order. VA's "Rule of Two" — if 2+ SDVOSBs can compete, CO must set aside for SDVOSBs.
- **VAAR 852 (Clauses):** VAAR-specific clauses included in VA solicitations. Watch for VAAR 852.219-10 (VA Notice of SDVOSB Set-Aside) and 852.219-11 (VA Notice of VOSB Set-Aside).
- **VA's Vendor Information Pages (VIP):** VA maintains its own vendor database for SDVOSB/VOSB verification. After SBA VetCert certification, ensure TCAF is listed in VIP.
- **VA Mission Act (2018):** Expanded VA's community care and support services — creates contracting opportunities for non-VA community mental health and peer support providers (TCAF's exact profile).

### HHSAR — HHS Acquisition Regulation (48 CFR Chapter 3)

Governs SAMHSA, NIH, HRSA, ACF, CMS contracts:
- HHSAR 315 (Source Selection): HHS uses past performance heavily; technical excellence weighted significantly
- HHSAR 352 (Clauses): Privacy Act compliance, HIPAA-adjacent safeguards on data deliverables
- **SAMHSA set-asides:** Watch for SDVOSB set-asides in behavioral health service contracts

### DFARS — Defense FAR Supplement (48 CFR Chapter 2)

Governs DoD (Army, Navy, Air Force, DHA) contracts:
- DFARS 252.225: Buy American, domestic sourcing
- DFARS 252.204-7012: Safeguarding Covered Defense Information (DFARS cyber) — if handling any DoD-sensitive data
- DoD SDVOSB set-aside rules: DoD follows FAR 19.14; less aggressive than VA's Veterans First, but still significant preference

### EDAR — ED Acquisition Regulation (48 CFR Chapter 34)

For Promise Neighborhoods, Title IV-E, and other ED contracts (vs. grants):
- Smaller supplement; follow FAR primarily
- EDAR emphasizes evidence-based programs consistent with IES/What Works Clearinghouse standards — aligns with CBHEM / CFIR framing

## 8. Proposal Writing Under FAR — Checklist

Before submitting any federal contract proposal:

**Pre-submission:**
- [ ] Read Section M (evaluation factors) first — before writing a single word
- [ ] Read Section L (instructions) for format, page limits, volumes, font requirements
- [ ] Read Section C (PWS/SOW) and H (special requirements) in full
- [ ] Note set-aside type (SDVOSB? Small business? Unrestricted?) and confirm eligibility
- [ ] Check for amendments to the solicitation on SAM.gov — amendments supersede base solicitation
- [ ] Identify all attachments (wage determinations, past performance questionnaires, pricing templates)
- [ ] Confirm SAM.gov registration is active (TCAF: expires 2027-05-06)
- [ ] Map each Section M subfactor to a narrative response — no unanswered factors

**Technical volume:**
- [ ] Open every response paragraph: "In response to [Section X.X]'s requirement that [verbatim language from PWS]..." (Iron Rule #5)
- [ ] Close every response paragraph: "[Evidence: ...]" citing specific TCAF/Dr. Flood credential or deliverable
- [ ] Include the CBHEM/CFIR/RE-AIM framing for any behavioral health or community service contract
- [ ] Include past performance for Dr. Flood's VA/DoD employment as analogous experience
- [ ] Add Crystal Correa (VA Rating Specialist) credentials wherever VA system knowledge is an evaluation factor

## 10. Protest Rights (FAR Part 33 / GAO)

If TCAF loses an award it believes should have won:
- **GAO Protest:** File within **10 calendar days** of knowing (or should have known) of the basis for protest. File at gao.gov/legal/bid-protests. GAO has 100 days to decide. Agency must implement favorable GAO decision.
- **COFC Protest:** U.S. Court of Federal Claims; more expensive, used for systemic issues.
- **Agency-Level Protest:** File with the CO or agency protest official. Faster but less independent.
- **Common protest grounds:** Flawed evaluation (scores don't match narrative), improper sole-source, failure to follow solicitation requirements, conflict of interest, misleading amendment.

**TCAF rule:** Request a debrief within 3 business days of any unfavorable award notification. Debriefs are required under FAR 15.506 and provide the basis for deciding whether to protest.
