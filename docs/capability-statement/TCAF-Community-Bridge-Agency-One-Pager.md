# TCAF Community Bridge / Community Impact
## Agency & Entity One-Pager

**A community-infrastructure approach from TCAF, delivered through the ThriveUp platform ecosystem**

### The story in one sentence

TCAF helps communities turn a documented local condition into an accountable action-and-learning loop: **condition → evidence → intervention → action → implementation → outcome → learning**.

### What it is

Community Bridge is planning, navigation, evidence, implementation, and learning infrastructure for communities moving beyond a needs assessment or one-time referral. It helps people and organizations see what is happening, decide what can responsibly be done, connect the right actors, document implementation, and learn from outcomes.

It does not replace resident leadership, cultural stewardship, agency authority, partner service delivery, case management, or an existing community organization. It helps those roles work together with clearer information and accountability.

### Why it matters

Communities often have data without a usable plan, services without a shared pathway, and promising ideas without a way to test, document, or improve them. Community Bridge makes the next responsible step visible—not unsupported promises—by connecting local evidence, community experience, resources, implementation decisions, and outcome learning.

### What TCAF provides

- **IGN:** readiness-aware guidance at each person’s or organization’s pace.
- **Community-ready information:** source-labeled data, context, limitations, and unavailable-data states.
- **Planning support:** shared goals, roles, readiness checks, and an appropriate local implementation path.
- **Navigation and capacity tools:** understandable next steps for residents and usable infrastructure for organizations.
- **Action and learning:** documented actions, results, open questions, and next improvements.
- **Training and support:** responsible use of tools, evidence, and decisions.

### What a scoped engagement can deliver

The participating entity sets the final scope. With documented need and authorization, an engagement can produce:

1. a shared view of a local condition and response gap;
2. an action pathway with clear decision owners;
3. consent, privacy, referral, and data-sharing boundaries;
4. resident navigation or partner-capacity tools;
5. an implementation and measurement plan; and
6. a learning brief separating observed results, modeled information, and open questions.

### Who it serves—and the role each retains

| Stakeholder | Value from the approach | Role that remains theirs |
|---|---|---|
| **Residents and families** | Understandable guidance and meaningful ways to shape the work | Lived experience, consent, priorities, and choice |
| **Nonprofits and community organizations** | Capacity, usable data, planning tools, and outcome learning | Trusted relationships, mission, and service delivery |
| **Agencies and policymakers** | Community-grounded evidence and implementation visibility | Public authority, policy, statutory duties, and accountability |
| **Funders and grant reviewers** | A clearer line from need to action to documented learning | Funding decisions, requirements, and stewardship |

### How TCAF works

TCAF follows a full cycle: **Research → Planning → Preparation → Execution → Continuous Assessment → Implementation → Scale Up → Scale Out.** Work starts by listening and verifying—not by assuming an intervention is proven, a partner is committed, or one community’s solution fits another.

TCAF is a 501(c)(3) public charity. Separately contracted technical or implementation work through ISS LLC remains in its own legal and procurement lane; nothing is implied without written authorization.

### Safeguards

- Participation and consequential actions are consent- and authorization-first.
- Data are separated by purpose and access; small cells are suppressed.
- Missing data are labeled, not filled with claims.
- The platform does not rank people or neighborhoods for policing, eligibility, or funding denial.
- Community insight informs decisions; it does not override agency authority or resident choice.
- A hypothesis remains a hypothesis until tested and documented.

### Geographic language to use

**Use:** “Built for use in any U.S. county—**50 states, one architecture**.”  
**Do not use:** “52 states.” The United States has 50 states. If an engagement framework intentionally includes the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico, call it a **52-jurisdiction frame**, and name that definition. Do not present architecture, source availability, or a planned partnership as current service delivery.

### Entity clarity

TCAF is the nonprofit legal entity leading charitable and civic-purpose work. ThriveUp names the platform ecosystem; it is not a separate contracting entity. ISS LLC is a separate for-profit entity and only performs independently scoped, contracted technical or implementation work.

### A responsible next step

Begin with a documented local question, the right decision owner, community participation boundaries, available evidence, and a written scope. TCAF can then help determine whether a Community Bridge approach is appropriate, what it can responsibly deliver, and how learning will be shared.
