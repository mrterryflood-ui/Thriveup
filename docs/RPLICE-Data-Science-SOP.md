## The Gentrification Displacement Pattern

**Source:** Census ACS 5-Year Timeline Data (2015, 2017, 2019, 2022)

**What happens:** When rent increases 30%+ but income in the poorest neighborhoods only increases 3-5%, existing residents get priced out. They don't get richer — they get displaced. "Revitalization" is often code for gentrification: wealthier people move in, property values rise, and existing residents are pushed to other neighborhoods or into homelessness.

**Wilmington, DE finding:** Income rose 40% in target tracts while surrounding tracts saw poverty INCREASE — displacement, not progress.

**Austin, TX finding:** 54x income gap — the richest tract and the poorest tract are 15 minutes apart. One family earns $14,800/month. The other earns $755/month. Same county government, same local news, same political representation — completely different realities.

## Key Findings Across All 12 Regions

1. **Education = #1 Protective Factor** — Across all 12 analyzed regions, higher education attainment is the strongest single predictor of reduced poverty, better health outcomes, and economic mobility.
2. **Gentrification = Displacement, Not Improvement** — Rising incomes in gentrifying tracts correlate with increased poverty in adjacent tracts. People move — problems don't disappear.
3. **County Averages Hide Everything** — In every region analyzed, tract-level data reveals 10-50x disparities invisible at the county level. Policy built on averages misses the people who need it most.
4. **Single-Parent Households = Strongest Risk Indicator** — Tracts with >30% single-parent households consistently show 3-5x higher poverty rates, lower educational attainment, and reduced workforce participation.

## SOP Summary

**Every time we tell the data story, we follow this chain:**

1. Pull live Census tract data (not county averages)
2. Identify the poverty tracts (>25% and >30% thresholds)
3. Cross-reference access barriers (broadband, vehicle, language)
4. Calculate benefit enrollment gaps (eligible vs. enrolled)
5. Map health and food insecurity downstream effects
6. Expose social isolation and system distrust patterns
7. Connect to crime data — show the downstream outcome
8. Apply Three Realities (Research vs. Political vs. Ground Truth)
9. Map interventions to each chain link
10. Show which TCAF platforms address which links

**This is the standard. No shortcuts. No county averages presented without tract-level truth underneath them.**
