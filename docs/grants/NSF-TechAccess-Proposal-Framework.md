## SECTION 1: VISION AND GOALS (2 pages)

### 1.1 The AI Readiness Crisis in Texas

Texas is the second-largest state by population (30M+) and economy ($2.1T GDP). Yet AI readiness is concentrated in a handful of urban tech corridors — Austin, Dallas, Houston — leaving the vast majority of Texans without access to AI knowledge, tools, or workforce skills.

**The populations left behind:**
- 4.3 million Texans below the poverty line
- 1.5 million veterans, many transitioning to civilian careers without AI skills
- 500,000+ returning citizens seeking workforce reentry in an AI-transformed economy
- 2.8 million small businesses that need AI tools to compete but lack training
- 6.4 million rural Texans with limited broadband and zero AI infrastructure
- Millions of Black, Latino, and immigrant community members excluded from tech sector pathways

**Current efforts are insufficient because they:**
- Center in research universities — reaching enrolled students, not community members
- Focus on theory — not practical AI deployment for real-world use
- Serve those already connected — not historically marginalized populations
- Operate in isolation — no coordinated statewide framework

### 1.2 The Texas Coordination Hub Vision

TCAF proposes a fundamentally different model: a practitioner-led Coordination Hub that deploys AI readiness through existing community infrastructure — libraries, community centers, bank branches, campuses, churches, workforce centers, and health clinics — reaching populations that never walk into a university computer lab.

**Hub Goals:**
1. Expand AI literacy across Texas — from awareness through implementation leadership
2. Build AI workforce skills through experiential learning — internships, apprenticeships, project-based work
3. Enable AI adoption by small businesses, government agencies, nonprofits, and community organizations
4. Create a sustainable statewide coordination network that persists beyond NSF funding
5. Demonstrate that community-based AI deployment is replicable nationally

### 2.2 Collaborative AI Engine

TCAF's AI infrastructure is uniquely positioned for a Coordination Hub because it does not depend on a single AI provider:

- **Four AI models run in parallel** on every interaction: Google Gemini 2.0 Flash, Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT-4o-mini, and DeepSeek R1
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** injects verified knowledge into every AI response
- **Implementation science frameworks** (CFIR 2.0, RE-AIM) are embedded in the AI pipeline
- **Consensus synthesis** merges all model outputs into validated, reliable responses
- **This matters because:** Community AI applications (benefits screening, health navigation, workforce guidance) require accuracy. Single-model hallucinations create real harm. Multi-model consensus dramatically reduces error rates.

### 2.3 Data Infrastructure

- **Census ACS 5-Year data** integrated at tract level — currently covering 501 tracts across 5 Central Texas counties for the St. David's Foundation initiative, with architecture designed for nationwide tract-level scaling
- **Benefits enrollment gap analysis** — 192,029-person gap identified with barrier index computation (Central Texas pilot); scalable to any state/county
- **Public evidence chain** at thrivingcommunitiesforall.com/sdoh-explorer — accessible nationwide
- **GIS integration** — CDC PLACES, SVI, FBI Crime Data, USDA Food Access, HUD, SAMHSA, BLS — all national datasets
- **Real-time monitoring** — platform health, enrollment tracking, outcome dashboards across all 24 platforms serving users nationwide

### 5.2 RPLICE Evaluation Framework

TCAF's RPLICE toolkit provides the evaluation infrastructure:

**CFIR 2.0 (Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research):**
- Assesses readiness of each deployment site before launch
- Monitors implementation fidelity across all coalition partner sites
- Identifies adaptation needs — what works in Pflugerville may need modification in rural Bastrop
- Tracks barriers and facilitators to AI readiness adoption

**RE-AIM Outcome Measurement:**
| Dimension | What We Measure | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | Who participates in AI readiness training | Enrollment demographics, geographic coverage, underserved population % |
| **Effectiveness** | Did AI skills improve? | Pre/post assessment scores, workforce placement rates, business AI adoption |
| **Adoption** | Do partner sites sustain the program? | Partner retention rate, site-level training volume |
| **Implementation** | Is the program delivered as designed? | Fidelity scores, curriculum completion rates, delivery consistency |
| **Maintenance** | Does AI readiness persist long-term? | 6/12-month follow-up, continued AI use, career advancement |

### 5.3 Independent Evaluation

- University-based external evaluator (to be confirmed with university partner)
- Annual evaluation reports submitted to NSF and published publicly
- Randomized control trial for workforce outcomes where feasible
- Qualitative data collection (participant interviews, focus groups, partner feedback)

### 5.4 Data Management Plan

- All evaluation data stored in TCAF's secure PostgreSQL database
- De-identified data publicly available through SDOH Explorer
- Compliance with NSF data sharing policies
- IRB approval through university partner for human subjects research

## SECTION 6: BROADER IMPACTS AND INTELLECTUAL MERIT (2 pages)

### 6.1 Intellectual Merit

This proposal advances knowledge in three domains:

1. **Community-Based AI Deployment:** No existing research documents how to deploy AI readiness training through community infrastructure (libraries, churches, banks) rather than educational institutions. This Hub generates the first systematic evidence base.

2. **Multi-Model Collaborative AI for Community Applications:** TCAF's four-model consensus AI engine is a novel approach to reducing AI hallucination risk in high-stakes community applications. This research contribution is directly applicable to any community AI deployment.

3. **Implementation Science for AI Readiness:** Applying CFIR 2.0 and RE-AIM frameworks to AI readiness is unprecedented. This Hub creates the first implementation science evidence for what works, for whom, and in what contexts when deploying AI readiness in historically marginalized communities.

### 6.2 Broader Impacts

**Broadening Participation:**
- Veteran-founded, Black-led organization
- Ecosystem designed from inception for historically marginalized communities
- Populations served: returning citizens, veterans, single parents, immigrant families, rural communities, Black and Latino youth, neurodiverse learners
- Bilingual (English/Spanish) delivery
- WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility compliance

**Economic Impact:**
- Direct workforce pipeline: AI-ready graduates connected to Texas employers
- Small business competitiveness: 1,000+ businesses equipped with AI tools by Year 3
- Economic mobility: AI skills as pathway out of poverty for historically marginalized populations

**National Model:**
- Documented, replicable deployment methodology
- Collaboration with National Coordination Lead for cross-state knowledge sharing
- Publication of best practices for other state Hubs
- Open-source curriculum and training materials

**Sustainability:**
- Workforce board integration ensures long-term funding beyond NSF
- Employer partnerships create self-sustaining training pipeline
- Technology platform persists at minimal marginal cost
- Coalition model grows — each new partner adds capacity

### 6.3 Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion

TCAF does not add DEI as an afterthought. The entire organization exists to serve historically marginalized communities. Every platform, every curriculum module, every partnership is designed around the question: "Does this reach the people current systems miss?"

- 24 platforms purpose-built for under-resourced communities

- Mission Transition — veteran-specific AI workforce pathway
