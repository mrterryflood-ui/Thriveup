## RPLICE — what it is and why it matters for the cancer prong

**RPLICE** = **Research-to-Practice Lifecycle Implementation & Community Evidence**. It is TCAF's implementation-science engine, instrumented against the 39 constructs of the **Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR)** and the RE-AIM evaluation framework, and operationalized in production with our **MAP-GAP** continuous quality improvement loop. The bridge is ~1,705 lines of executed code, not slides.

For the cancer-research prong specifically, RPLICE provides what a pediatric oncology lab at MD Anderson, Texas Children's, Dell Children's, UT Health San Antonio, or BCM-Texas Children's Cancer Center typically **cannot build in-house**:

1. A **community delivery channel** for evidence-based pediatric cancer survivorship programs — psychosocial follow-up, neurocognitive late-effects screening using the same behavioral instruments TCAF runs at clinical grade (PHQ-9, GAD-7, C-SSRS, PCL-5, ACEs via SafeReport with HITL clinician review and zero PHI egress).
2. An **SDOH stabilization layer** for treatment-disrupted families (LifeBridge handles SNAP, housing, transportation, utility assistance — the floor that has to be in place before any survivorship intervention takes root).
3. A **language-access layer** (Talk Your Talk: 89 spoken + 18 sign + 6 learning surfaces, dialect-aware including AAVE and Spanglish, RTL for Arabic) that pediatric oncology programs serving immigrant and LEP families consistently lack.
4. A **vocational rehabilitation pathway** for adolescent and young adult cancer survivors with neurocognitive late effects (Trade Sims across 6 trades with software engineering as the 7th in build, with Perfectly Different layered for cognitive-accommodation support).
5. **CFIR-instrumented evaluation** with publishable implementation outcomes, not just survivor counts.

This is the lane where TCAF earns its keep on a cancer grant: not by claiming to discover anything new about cancer, but by giving Texas cancer researchers a community implementation partner that the field is structurally short of.

## Proposed project (one paragraph)

**Texas Children with Disabilities & Pediatric Cancer Survivorship Implementation Hub — Year 1.** Over 12 months, TCAF will (A) deliver direct support to **150 Texas children with disabilities and their families** through an integrated service stack — Perfectly Different for IEP/504 navigation and neurodiversity-affirming learning support, Talk Your Talk for communication-access (including sign language coverage), ThriveUp Academy for accessible STEM/AI/financial literacy/FAFSA pathways, LifeBridge for Medicaid waiver and SSI navigation, and Whole-Person Health for family caregiver behavioral support — and (B) stand up a **pediatric cancer survivorship implementation partnership** offering RPLICE translation services to one or two Texas pediatric oncology research teams who need a community delivery and evaluation arm for survivorship work. Prong B is structured as a **named partnership with research-team confirmation, not a unilateral claim**; if no Texas pediatric oncology partner commits during the project period, we will report that honestly and redirect Prong B funds back into Prong A direct service.

## Evaluation and reporting

**Prong A — Children with disabilities (Reach / Effectiveness / Adoption / Implementation / Maintenance):**

- **Effectiveness:** IEP/504 outcomes (goal attainment scaling, parent-reported satisfaction), Perfectly Different engagement metrics, family caregiver behavioral health score change at 6 months, benefits-navigation approval rate.
