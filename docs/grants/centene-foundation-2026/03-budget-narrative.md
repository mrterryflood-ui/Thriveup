# Budget Narrative — Centene Foundation Behavioral Health Community Grant (Spring 2026)

**Applicant:** The Collaborative Advocate Foundation Inc. (DBA TCAF) · EIN 41-3618503
**Project:** Compliance-Grade Behavioral Health for the Multilingual Medicaid Cohort
**Period:** 12 months · **Total Request:** $150,000

---

## 1. Personnel ($75,000 — 50% of total)

**1a. Program Lead — Terry D. Flood, Sr., President, TCAF (0.15 FTE, $15,000).** Mr. Flood is a US Army Retiree and the President of The Collaborative Advocate Foundation. He carries institutional signature authority for all proposals and serves as the principal point of contact with Centene Foundation. His 0.15 FTE allocation reflects: program oversight (~3 hours/week), quarterly Centene Foundation reporting, partner relationship management with Sankofa CHWs / M2C peer navigators / faith partners, and direct sign-off on every CFIR/RE-AIM evaluation milestone. Rate of $100,000 base reflects nonprofit-executive median for organizations under $1M operating budget.

**1b. BH Program Coordinator (0.50 FTE, $40,000).** A 20-hour-per-week role responsible for day-to-day coordination of the BH stack: scheduling clinical decision-support reviews, monitoring crisis-routing handoffs from Talk Your Talk into Whole-Person Health, coordinating Sankofa CHW deployment, and managing the SDOH-referral inflow from LifeBridge. Position requires a clinical or paraprofessional background (LMSW, LPC-associate, or community-health-worker certification). Rate of $80,000 base reflects Travis County market for a half-time clinical-coordinator role.

**1c. Evaluation Lead — RPLICE / CFIR-RE-AIM (0.20 FTE, $18,000).** Eight hours per week dedicated to implementation-science evaluation. Responsibilities: data-infrastructure design, fidelity tracking against CFIR domains, quarterly RE-AIM dimension scoring (Reach / Effectiveness / Adoption / Implementation / Maintenance), preparation of the four quarterly outcome reports, and mid-year review presentation. Rate of $90,000 base reflects market for an MPH-credentialed evaluator with implementation-science experience.

**1d. Fringe benefits ($2,000).** Calculated at 5.3% on personnel. This is below standard fringe rates because TCAF has not yet finalized its employee-benefits package; the budget reflects expected actuals (payroll taxes only) rather than aspirational benefits. Future budgets will request standard 28–32% fringe once the benefits package is in place.

## 2. Subaward / Community Partner Stipends ($30,000 — 20% of total)

**2a. Sankofa Network CHW stipends ($20,000).** Four Community Health Workers at $5,000 each for the 12-month period, delivering culturally-responsive outreach into Black and Brown communities in Travis County. CHWs are not TCAF employees; they are community members affiliated with Sankofa Network platforms (Black Maternal Health Hub, Black Men's Health Hub, HerHealth Network) who carry trusted relationships into populations a clinical team alone cannot reach. Each CHW is expected to deliver ~175 outreach contacts over 12 months ($28 per contact). The CHW model is evidence-aligned with current health-equity literature and matches Centene Foundation's published priorities.

**2b. M2C Veteran Peer Navigator stipends ($8,000).** Two peer navigators (veterans themselves) at $4,000 each, focused on the first-12-months post-separation crisis window. Peer navigators handle outreach into VFW posts, American Legion halls, and recently-separated cohorts in Travis County, delivering Mission Transition platform engagement and routing crisis screenings into Whole-Person Health.

**2c. Faith-community delivery partner stipends ($2,000).** Two Travis County congregations at $1,000 each. These partners host monthly community-screening events, provide trusted referral pathways for residents who would not engage with a clinical setting first, and offer space for SDOH navigation conversations. Stipends compensate for space use, volunteer coordination, and printed materials in congregational languages.

## 3. Platform Operations ($20,000 — 13.3% of total)

**3a. SafeReport HITL clinician review hours ($15,000).** SafeReport's Human-In-The-Loop default-on policy requires that every AI clinical-decision-support recommendation be reviewed by a credentialed clinician before it reaches a patient. At 200 review hours per year × $75/hour (Travis County market rate for licensed clinical review), this line funds the human safety layer that makes SafeReport's responsible-AI posture real rather than rhetorical. This is the line that distinguishes our work from any "AI-only" BH platform that cuts the clinician out of the loop.

**3b. FHIR + CDS Hooks interop maintenance ($3,000).** Annual cost of maintaining the healthcare-IT interoperability test harness, including FHIR resource validation, CDS Hooks endpoint monitoring, and PHI-safe egress verification. This investment positions us for direct EHR integration with Superior HealthPlan provider networks if/when that pathway opens.

**3c. Screening-instrument licensing and administration ($2,000).** PHQ-9 and GAD-7 are public-domain. C-SSRS (Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale) and PCL-5 (PTSD Checklist) require institutional registration and training-fidelity documentation. This line covers registration, training renewal, and quality-assurance audits.

## 4. Evaluation & Implementation Science ($15,000 — 10% of total)

**4a. RPLICE data infrastructure ($8,000).** One-time build cost for the CFIR/RE-AIM data infrastructure: schema design for cross-platform outcome tracking, dashboard build for Centene Foundation program-officer read-only access, automated quarterly-report data pipelines.

**4b. Quarterly outcome reports ($6,000).** Four reports at $1,500 each. Each report includes: RE-AIM dimension scoring, CFIR fidelity narrative, unique-residents-reached count by demographic, crisis-routing handoff timing, screening-to-treatment-engagement rate, and lessons learned for cycle-over-cycle improvement. Distributed to Centene Foundation, TCAF Board, and (with permission) the broader implementation-science research community via the RPLICE platform.

**4c. Mid-year evaluation review session ($1,000).** One in-person or virtual session with Centene Foundation program officers at the six-month mark to review interim findings, surface any course-corrections needed, and confirm alignment with Centene's evolving priorities. Covers session prep, materials, and travel-if-needed.

## 5. Direct Participant Support ($5,000 — 3.3% of total)

**5a. Transportation assistance ($3,750).** Approximately 250 round-trips × $15 each (Capital Metro pass or rideshare reimbursement) for residents who otherwise could not attend screening or referral appointments. Transportation is the #2 SDOH driver of missed BH appointments after work-schedule conflicts.

**5b. Interpretation reserve fund ($750).** Talk Your Talk covers 89 spoken + 18 sign languages in-platform; this small reserve handles edge cases (rare dialects, sustained multi-hour live-interpreter sessions for complex BH intakes) that exceed in-platform capacity.

**5c. Printed materials ($500).** For residents who lack consistent device access — non-digital BH screening intake forms, crisis-line cards, and SDOH-resource guides in languages including Spanish, Vietnamese, Burmese, Tigrinya, and ASL-friendly visual formats.

## 6. Indirect Costs ($5,000 — 3.3% of total)

TCAF charges a **modest 10% indirect rate** on the indirect base of $50,000 (excluding subaward, direct participant support, and equipment), yielding $5,000. This rate is well below the 25–35% indirect rates common at established research nonprofits. We elected this rate because:

1. TCAF is a newly-determined 501(c)(3) (eff. 01/14/2026) and has not yet negotiated a federal Indirect Cost Rate Agreement (we have no prior federal awards on USASpending.gov as of submission date).
2. Centene Foundation's resources reach further when more goes to direct service.
3. Future foundation and federal grants will fund the back-office infrastructure that a sustainable indirect rate would otherwise require.

This indirect charge covers shared accounting, audit preparation, IT infrastructure beyond the platforms themselves, board governance support, and standard nonprofit insurance.

---

## Cost-Effectiveness Statement

At $150,000 for ~1,500 unique residents served, the **per-resident cost is $100**. For comparison, a single Medicaid-funded emergency-department behavioral-health visit costs $1,200–$2,400 in Texas, and a single inpatient psychiatric-admission day costs an average of $7,100. If this grant prevents even **12 ED visits or 2 inpatient admissions** across the served cohort over the funding period, the grant pays for itself in Centene's downstream cost arithmetic. We expect to prevent meaningfully more than that.

## Stewardship & Reporting Commitments

TCAF commits to:

1. **No reallocation between major categories** without prior written notice to Centene Foundation.
2. **Quarterly financial reports** alongside quarterly outcome reports — all four lined up with the funding period to support program-officer review on a single calendar.
3. **Annual Form 990 filed on time** per IRS requirement for 501(c)(3) organizations under 170(b)(1)(A)(vi).
4. **Audit cooperation** — TCAF will engage an independent CPA review of program finances within 90 days of grant period close.
5. **Open data dashboard** — Centene Foundation program officers receive read-only access for the duration of the grant period and 12 months thereafter.

We respect that Centene Foundation's stewardship of donor dollars is its own form of trust-building. We treat this grant the same way.
