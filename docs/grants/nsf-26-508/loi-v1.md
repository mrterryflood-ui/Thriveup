## Synopsis (1-page limit per solicitation §V.A — describes work in sufficient detail to permit selection of potential reviewers)

The Texas Coordination Hub will accelerate AI readiness and adoption across the second-largest state economy in the United States by activating an existing production-grade coordination platform — already shipping to real users across workforce, education, healthcare, justice, and rural-services lanes — and convening a Texas-anchored partner network spanning academic, workforce, industry, government, and Cooperative Extension stakeholders.

TCAF is uniquely positioned to execute as the neutral statewide convener the solicitation describes. The Hub will be built on TCAF's existing ThriveUp Academy platform — 271 Drizzle data tables, 211 production frontend pages, 84 server-route modules, 4-engine collaborative AI orchestration with provider failover (Gemini, Claude, GPT-4o-mini, DeepSeek R1), an 86-chunk RAG engine grounded in source-cited documents, dialect-aware translation across 89 spoken and 18 signed languages, and a grant-intelligence engine actively tracking 721+ funding opportunities across SAM.gov, Grants.gov, USASpending, and curated foundation sources. The platform already operationalizes 39 CFIR implementation-science constructs in production code and ships a Continuous-Quality-Improvement engine (MAP-GAP CQI, 1,705 lines) explicitly designed to surface and remediate ecosystem gaps — the precise capability the Hub needs to identify and address state-level AI-readiness shortfalls.

Hands-on AI deployment support is already operational: the platform ships 5-trade workforce credentialing infrastructure with 75+ lessons, industry-grade physics engines (Modified Nodal Analysis for electrical, Hardy-Cross Newton-Raphson for plumbing, AWS D1.1-compliant heat-input evaluation for welding, thermal-airflow modeling for HVAC), automated credential-pathway routing to apprenticeship.gov, Texas Workforce Commission, and WorkInTexas at 80% completion, and Socratic vs. ensemble-debrief AI tutoring modes. This pattern — pedagogically rigorous, credential-routed, multilingual, evidence-instrumented — will be extended to AI-literacy and AI-deployment training, in alignment with the DOL AI Literacy Framework, WIOA, and Perkins V, and integrated with Registered Apprenticeships, micro-credentials, digital badges, and competency-based assessments as the solicitation specifies.

The five Hub responsibilities map directly to existing TCAF capabilities, with statewide partnerships extending reach: (1) the AI Learning and Resource Navigator extends TCAF's grant-intelligence and platform-funder-fit engines to AI-training resources statewide; (2) the State AI Readiness Strategic Plan applies the platform's Chainweb 8-step citation-chained data pipeline (Census/CDC/SVI/FBI) and Community Voice live participatory-evidence engine to ground statewide priorities in source-verified data and community input; (3) AI Deployment Support combines TCAF's hands-on technical-assistance pattern with named partner channels — Texas SBDC for small business, Texas Workforce Commission and American Job Center operators for the public workforce system, Texas A&M AgriLife Extension and USDA-NIFA networks for rural and agricultural reach, and a Texas R1 academic anchor for technical depth — and develops a Texas AI Deployment Corps aligned with the National Coordination Lead's nationally coordinated AI Deployment Network; (4) AI Readiness Training & Capacity Building extends TCAF's Trade Sims credential-routing pattern, dialect-aware delivery, and multilingual instructional infrastructure to AI literacy across K-16 and workforce systems, with formal alignment to DOL's AI Literacy Framework and credential-recognition through state-board and industry-credential channels already integrated in production; (5) Coordination Within Priority Sectors leverages TCAF's existing Ecosystem Hub (25 platforms tracked, cross-platform event bus, RPLICE-bridge implementation-science routing) to convene Texas sector partners in energy, advanced manufacturing, healthcare delivery, and agriculture, complementing rather than duplicating existing sector coordination.

Performance metrics required by the solicitation — individuals trained by category, businesses and government entities assisted, statewide convenings held, contributions to national best-practice repositories, AI Deployment Corps participation, organizations technically assisted — will be tracked through the platform's existing outcome-reporting and behavioral-audit infrastructure (academy_choice_logs, network_member_events) and reported in formats consumable by NSF and the National Coordination Lead's national dashboards. Letters of Collaboration from all named partners will accompany the full proposal as Supplemental Material per §V.A. The Hub commits to active collaboration with the National Coordination Lead once established, including participation in Hub convenings, contribution to national dashboards and best-practice repositories, engagement in AI Deployment Network activities, and the role of round-1 cohort in shaping the National Lead engagement model as the solicitation describes.
