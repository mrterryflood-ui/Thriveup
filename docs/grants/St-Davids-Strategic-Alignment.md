## WAB 1.0 → WAB 2.0: What Changed and Why

### WAB 1.0 (2024-2026):
- 16 organizations funded
- Health insurance enrollment only (Medicaid, CHIP, Marketplace, MAP)
- 2-year duration
- 27,000 individuals enrolled in 18 months
- Context: Federal policy changes in 2025 reduced Medicaid/CHIP eligibility, Enhanced Premium Tax Credits expired for 2026 Marketplace plans, funding for navigation services dramatically reduced

### WAB 2.0: Building Economic Stability (2026-2029):
Three specific changes driven by 1.0 learnings:

**1. Expanded Scope**
- Beyond health insurance → now includes food security (WIC, SNAP) and income supports (EITC)
- "Reflecting the interconnected nature of this work"
- "Holistic approaches that increase access to food and income supports"
- "Recognizes the relationship between health and economic stability"

**TCAF Response:** Our Benefits Screener already covers 9 programs: SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, WIC, EITC, CTC, TANF, SSI, ACA Marketplace. We were built for 2.0's expanded scope from day one. The Get Help hub at thrivingcommunitiesforall.com/get-help organizes all 12 HHSC service categories.

**2. Prioritizing Expertise AND Trust**
- "Organizations that have the necessary expertise to effectively help marginalized populations understand and enroll in public benefits"
- "AS WELL AS possess the deep trust and culturally responsive practices necessary to connect with historically marginalized community members"
- Both are required — expertise alone is insufficient, trust alone is insufficient

**TCAF Response:** Our coalition model embeds bilingual CHWs at trusted community sites — the library, the community center, the campus, the bank branch. They're community members first, navigators second. Our digital platform provides the expertise layer (real-time screeners, policy updates, enrollment tracking) while our CHWs provide the trust layer. The ecosystem connects both.

**3. Longer Duration (3 Years)**
- "Recognizing that building effective organizational infrastructure that has a path to sustainability takes time"
- This signals they want organizations that will LAST, not pop-up programs

**TCAF Response:** HHSC Community Partner Program certification makes our enrollment infrastructure permanent state infrastructure. The coalition model grows new partners as it demonstrates results. The digital ecosystem doesn't expire when funding ends. 3 years gives us time to build the full 5-county network and demonstrate sustainability.

## WAB 1.0 Insights → TCAF Coalition Responses

### Insight #1: 27,000 enrolled despite policy chaos
**What happened:** Federal changes reduced eligibility, Enhanced Premium Tax Credits expired, navigation funding cut. "The role of trained benefits navigators has become both more essential and challenging."

**TCAF Coalition Response (Mission Command):**
- Decentralized execution means no single point of failure — when federal policy changes disrupt one enrollment pathway, others continue
- Each partner site operates with autonomy to adapt to local conditions
- Digital platform updates policy guidance in real time across all partner sites
- When Marketplace premium credits expired, CHWs at Frost Bank shift to EITC/CTC (income supports still available)
- When Medicaid eligibility narrows, CHWs redirect families to CHIP, ACA Marketplace, or MAP

### Insight #2: Policy expertise requires peer networks and real-time support
**What happened:** "Sustaining expertise requires ongoing effort... recent policy changes have made this work increasingly labor-intensive, stretching capacity." Organizations needed connections to policy experts, training, real-time support, and connections to state agencies.

**TCAF Coalition Response (Shared Infrastructure):**
- HHSC CPP certification provides direct connection to state agencies
- TCPP Partner Resources portal provides navigator training and HHSC tools
- Coalition meetings create the peer network WAB 1.0 grantees needed
- Digital ecosystem shares policy updates, best practices, and enrollment data across all partners simultaneously
- BetterScience Lab / RPLICE provides implementation science measurement (CFIR/RE-AIM) — the learning infrastructure St. David's values
- No partner carries the expertise burden alone — the coalition distributes it

### Insight #3: Trust beats everything — built over years, not intake forms
**What happened:** "Deep trust-building, human connection, and patience — often built over years." "Well-founded fear and mistrust of systems." "Building and maintaining this trust requires an increasingly significant amount of time and effort which must be accounted for in funding and staffing expectations."

**TCAF Coalition Response (Trust as Infrastructure):**
- Mixed-status families: screen children's eligibility without requiring parent immigration status
- CHWs are community members — neighbors, not government workers
- Enrollment at kitchen tables, laundromats, school pickup lines, food pantry distribution days
- 3-year duration aligns perfectly: Year 1 build trust + initial enrollments, Year 2 scale across counties, Year 3 demonstrate sustainability and HHSC CPP integration
- The coalition doesn't parachute in — we're already here (Pflugerville, Manor)
- "Culturally responsive practices that acknowledge fears and confusion" — our bilingual CHWs don't persuade, they inform and let families decide

## Mission Command Operating Model for Full Application

### Commander's Intent (TCAF as Backbone Organization):
Every eligible family in the 5-county region is screened, enrolled, and retained in benefits — through a coalition where every partner operates with autonomy but shared data, shared accountability, and shared reporting.

### Decentralized Execution (Coalition Partners):
| Partner | Community | Strength | Autonomy |
|---|---|---|---|
| City of Pflugerville | Williamson County | Municipal reach, library, trusted local government | Operates enrollment through library and community events |
| City of Manor | Eastern Travis County | Fastest-growing underserved community | Operates enrollment through community centers |
| Frost Bank | Cross-county | Financial literacy, existing customer relationships | Operates EITC/CTC enrollment through branch locations and financial wellness workshops |
| ACC / Prof. Laura Franco | Travis, Williamson, Hays | Campus access, student population, Latina leadership | Operates enrollment through student services and campus events |
| Faith-based partners (growing) | All 5 counties | Deep congregational trust | Operates enrollment through existing ministry and outreach |
| Food pantries/WIC offices (growing) | Bastrop, Caldwell, Hays | Existing access point for food-insecure families | Co-locates enrollment with food distribution |

### Reporting, Communication, Accountability:
- Real-time enrollment dashboards shared across all partners
- Every enrollment tracked: screening → application → confirmation → renewal
- Census tract-level gap analysis shows where coalition needs to grow
- Monthly coalition meetings for cross-learning and adaptation
- Public evidence chain at thrivingcommunitiesforall.com/sdoh-explorer
- HHSC CPP reporting feeds state-level accountability

### Auditing:
- Implementation science measurement (CFIR/RE-AIM) for fidelity
- Public, verifiable data — any stakeholder can audit the evidence chain
- Annual renewal tracking ensures retention, not just initial enrollment

### No Single Point of Failure:
- If TCAF's platform is down → CHWs continue with paper screeners
- If one partner exits → community connections transfer to remaining partners
- If federal policy changes → coalition adapts collectively, not individually
- If a CHW leaves → partner organization retains the community relationship
- HHSC CPP certification → infrastructure persists beyond any single grant
