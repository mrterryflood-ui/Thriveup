## 1. STRATEGIC POSITIONING ANALYSIS

### TCAF as the Technology Conduit

TCAF is not a direct service provider competing with established community organizations. TCAF is the **technology backbone** that connects partner nonprofits, community health workers (CHWs), and trusted community organizations to eligible but unenrolled people across the 5-county region. The platform does the heavy lifting virtually — eligibility screening, benefits matching, data coordination — and then routes people to trusted in-person support to close the enrollment.

**The TCAF Model:**

```
ELIGIBLE PERSON (unenrolled) 
    ↓
[TCAF Platform Layer]
  • AI-powered eligibility screening across all benefit types
  • GIS intelligence identifies who + where (zip-code level targeting)
  • Benefits matching engine cross-references SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, WIC, EITC, CTC, SSI, SSDI
  • Barrier index scoring identifies which barriers each person faces
    ↓
[Partner Coordination Layer]  
  • Routes to nearest trusted partner organization
  • CHW assignment based on language, culture, geography
  • Warm handoff — not a cold referral
    ↓
[Enrollment Support]
  • Partner CHW provides in-person application assistance
  • TCAF platform tracks application status and follow-up
  • Renewal alerts prevent benefit loss
    ↓
[Continuous Improvement]
  • MAP-GAP monitors enrollment outcomes by zip code
  • RPLICE validates fidelity of partner delivery
  • Data feeds back to improve targeting and outreach
```

**The Collaborative Advocate Foundation — We All Benefit 2.0 LOI**

Across Williamson, Bastrop, and Caldwell counties, thousands of families eligible for SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, and tax credits remain unenrolled — not because benefits don't exist, but because the systems designed to deliver them weren't designed for the people who need them most. In Williamson County alone, an estimated 45% of SNAP-eligible households aren't receiving benefits. In Bastrop and Caldwell counties, the gap is wider and the enrollment infrastructure thinner.

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation (TCAF), a 501(c)(3) headquartered in Pflugerville, proposes to close this participation gap by serving as the technology backbone that connects existing community organizations, community health workers, and trusted local partners to the people they are best positioned to serve.

**How it works:** TCAF's AI-powered platform screens families across all benefit types simultaneously — a family applying for SNAP is immediately assessed for Medicaid, CHIP, WIC, EITC, Child Tax Credit, SSI, and childcare subsidies. The platform identifies who is eligible, where they are located using GIS intelligence, and what barriers they face. Then it routes them — through warm referrals, not cold systems — to trusted community organizations and bilingual CHWs who provide the in-person support needed to complete enrollment.

**Client-driven:** Our model starts with how families actually seek help — through people and places they already trust. For mixed-status families wary of government systems, enrollment happens at church pantries, school resource centers, and community health fairs — with culturally responsive navigators who share their language and lived experience. Every interaction is designed around the family's priorities, fears, and timeline — not the system's.

**Holistic:** When our platform identifies a family eligible for SNAP, it doesn't stop at food security. It screens for healthcare access, tax credits, childcare subsidies, and housing assistance in a single interaction. While families wait for benefits approval, flexible support provides emergency food, transportation, and utility assistance — because hunger doesn't pause for processing times.

**Effective:** Our Benefits Intelligence System uses Census tract-level data to target outreach to the specific zip codes with the highest enrollment gaps. Implementation fidelity is monitored through RPLICE (our implementation science engine) using CFIR 2.0 and RE-AIM frameworks, and continuous quality improvement cycles through MAP-GAP ensure the model improves every 30 days — not every 12 months. We track both new enrollments AND renewals, because keeping benefits is as hard as getting them.

**Collaborative approach:** In Williamson County, TCAF builds the enrollment infrastructure directly. In Bastrop and Caldwell counties, we partner with existing food pantries, rural health clinics, churches, and school districts — providing the technology platform and data intelligence while partners provide the trusted community presence. Each partner has a specific, well-defined role based on complementary strengths.

**Sustainability:** TCAF's technology infrastructure is permanent. The GIS data gets richer each year. The partner network compounds. The enrolled population stays enrolled through automated renewal support. After three years, the counties have a functioning enrollment system that persists — not a program that disappears when the grant ends.

TCAF is ready to join the HHSC Community Partner Program, sign up for St. David's office hours, and begin building enrollment capacity where Central Texas needs it most.

## 5. HHSC COMMUNITY PARTNER PROGRAM PATHWAY

### What is the HHSC Community Partner Program?

The Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) Community Partner Program (CPP) allows community organizations to help community members complete HHSC benefits applications (SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, TANF). Participants receive training and varying levels of access to the HHSC application system.

### CPP Levels

| Level | Description | What You Can Do |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Community Partner | Help clients complete applications on Your Texas Benefits. Limited support — primarily navigation assistance |
| Level 2 | Enhanced Partner | Access to check application status. Can provide more detailed support through the process |
| Level 3 | Full Access Partner | Can submit applications on behalf of clients, track status, and follow up through completion. This is the most effective level for enrollment support |

## 6. TRUST-BASED OUTREACH MODEL

### Reaching Mixed-Status Families and System-Distrustful Populations

**The Problem:** Many eligible families — particularly mixed-status families, immigrant communities, and people with negative system experiences — avoid ALL government-connected systems, not just immigration. This means they miss SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, and tax credits their family members are legally entitled to.

**TCAF's Trust-Based Approach:**

### Principle 1: Meet People Where They Already Go

Don't create new intake points. Deploy through organizations families already trust:
- **Food pantries** — families seeking emergency food are already in crisis and already at a trusted location
- **Churches and faith communities** — long-term trust, culturally embedded, no government association
- **Schools** — universal touchpoint for families with children; school staff have earned trust
- **Health clinics** — people seeking healthcare are in a vulnerable moment and open to help
- **Community events** — health fairs, back-to-school events, cultural celebrations

### Principle 2: Culturally Responsive Navigators

- Hire CHWs FROM the communities they serve — shared language, culture, and lived experience
- Bilingual (English/Spanish at minimum) with additional languages based on community demographics
- Training on immigration-sensitive benefits counseling — know which benefits are safe for mixed-status families, which affect public charge determinations, and how to communicate this accurately
- Navigators are employees of trusted partner organizations, not TCAF staff — the trust stays local

### Principle 3: Accurate, Unbiased Information

- Mixed-status families need to know: eligible U.S. citizen children can receive SNAP, Medicaid, and CHIP without affecting parents' immigration status
- WIC is available regardless of immigration status
- EITC and CTC are available to eligible tax filers
- TCAF's platform provides decision support for navigators — accurate, current guidance on eligibility for every household configuration

### Principle 4: No Wrong Door

- A family walks into a food pantry for emergency groceries → the pantry staff, using TCAF's platform, screens them for SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, WIC, EITC, CTC, childcare subsidies, and utility assistance
- The family decides which benefits to pursue based on THEIR priorities and comfort level
- The navigator supports only what the family wants — no pressure, no judgment, no timeline pressure
- If they want to think about it, the platform saves their screening results and a navigator follows up (if the family consents) in the way they prefer

### Principle 5: Safety and Privacy

- TCAF platform does not collect or store immigration status information
- Screening focuses on eligibility for specific benefits, not on documentation status
- Data sharing with government agencies is limited to what is required for the specific benefit application the family chooses to pursue
- Partner organizations maintain their own privacy standards — TCAF's technology layer adds, does not replace

## 7. ENROLLMENT + RENEWAL MODEL

### The Full Enrollment Lifecycle

St. David's was explicit: **renewals are valued equally to new enrollments.** Keeping benefits is as hard as getting them.

### New Enrollment Pipeline

```
IDENTIFICATION → SCREENING → MATCHING → APPLICATION → FOLLOW-UP → ENROLLMENT
```

1. **Identification:** GIS intelligence + partner touchpoints identify eligible individuals by zip code and demographic indicators
2. **Screening:** TCAF platform runs multi-benefit eligibility screening in a single interaction
3. **Matching:** Platform identifies all benefits the individual/family qualifies for and prioritizes based on urgency and family preference
4. **Application:** Navigator assists with application — at partner location, in the person's language, with documentation support
5. **Follow-Up:** Platform tracks application status, sends reminders, escalates stalled applications
6. **Enrollment:** Confirmed enrollment is recorded, baseline economic impact is calculated, renewal timeline is set

### Renewal Support System

```
ENROLLMENT → MONITORING → ALERT → RENEWAL SUPPORT → CONFIRMED RENEWAL
```

1. **Monitoring:** Platform tracks benefit renewal dates for every enrolled individual
2. **Alert (60 days before):** Automated notification to the individual (text/email/phone based on preference) and to their assigned navigator
3. **Renewal Support (30 days before):** Navigator contacts individual to offer renewal assistance — document gathering, re-certification support, application updates
4. **Escalation (14 days before):** If no renewal action has been taken, direct outreach from partner organization — home visit, phone call, community center visit
5. **Confirmed Renewal:** Renewal is processed, next renewal date is set, cycle continues

### Preventing Benefit Loss

Common reasons people lose benefits and how TCAF addresses them:

| Reason | TCAF Solution |
|---|---|
| Missed renewal deadline | Automated 60-30-14 day alert cascade |
| Changed address — didn't update | Platform prompts address verification at every interaction |
| Confusion about re-certification requirements | Navigator provides step-by-step support |
| Fear/distrust (especially mixed-status families) | Same trusted navigator from initial enrollment handles renewal |
| Life changes affecting eligibility | Platform detects eligibility changes and adjusts benefit portfolio |
