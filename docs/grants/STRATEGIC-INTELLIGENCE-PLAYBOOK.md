## 1. GRANT WRITING: Objectives Must Be Measured Against Baseline Data
**Source:** Anika Amie, Grants Consulting/Management/Federal Grants
**Date:** April 11, 2026

Objectives are NOT evaluated independently — they are assessed in direct relation to the data presented in the Need Statement.

**Key Rules:**
- Objectives must reflect measurable change from a clearly defined baseline
- When baseline data is vague or lacks specificity, objectives appear arbitrary
- Strong applications define: **population, condition, measurement method, and comparative context**
- The strength of an objective is determined long before it is written

**TCAF Application:**
- RPLICE SDOH Location Intelligence pulls live Census ACS, CDC PLACES, EPA, and SVI data by ZIP code
- This IS the baseline engine — every grant objective should anchor to specific, locally-sourced data points
- Never write an objective without citing the baseline data from RPLICE first

## 2. WHY 99% OF GOVERNMENT CONTRACTORS LOSE — RISK REDUCTION
**Source:** David Cuellar, Founder, Government Contracting Advisory
**Date:** April 11, 2026

Most contractors think they lose because of pricing or competition. **That's not it.**

Contracts are already leaning toward someone before they're posted. Agencies don't start with an RFP — they start with **RISK REDUCTION.**

**What agencies look for:**
1. Vendors they've already seen perform under pressure
2. Companies that responded fast in past situations
3. Familiar names from prior work, outreach, or visibility
4. Proof someone can deliver without issues or delays

**The real question:** Not "Who is best?" but "Who is the safest choice to award this to?"

**The 1% who win focus on:**
1. Building proof of reliability BEFORE large contracts exist
2. Winning smaller or adjacent work that mirrors future scope
3. Being associated with OUTCOMES, not just capabilities
4. Reducing perceived risk long before evaluation happens

**TCAF Advantage:**
- 24 LIVE platforms with real users = proof of reliability
- Validated screening instruments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, C-SSRS, PCL-5, AUDIT-C, DAST-10) = outcomes
- Deployed since before FOAs drop = not pitching a concept, showing a system
- VOSB status = familiar, lower-risk vendor category

## 3. COMPLIANCE GATE: Section L & Section M
**Source:** Government contracting compliance infographic (LinkedIn)
**Date:** April 11, 2026

**Section L** = Your Blueprint (what to submit)
**Section M** = Your Scorecard (how it's graded)

**The "Administrative Guillotine" is real:**
- 1.1% price impact can be classified as "Material"
- GAO reports: failure to comply with administrative requirements is FATAL
- Proposals rejected for: incorrect fonts, page limits, missing amendments, signatures

**Process:**
1. **"Own" the RFP** — Scan for "shall," "must," "will" to identify every mandatory requirement
2. **Compliance Matrix** — Map every solicitation requirement to a section and page number
3. **"Gold Team Audit"** — Final independent review of Section L instructions before submitting

**RULE:** Before drafting ANY proposal, create a spreadsheet listing every requirement in Section L. One column = which section of your proposal addresses it. Another column = status. **Do not submit until every row says COMPLETE.**
