## B.1.a — Need Estimate

Travis and Williamson counties are home to approximately **95,000–105,000 veterans** — one of the highest concentrations in Texas outside Bexar and Harris counties. Despite this density, non-clinical community-based suicide prevention services in the Pflugerville–Manor–East Austin corridor are critically scarce.

**The foundational statistic:** The VA's 2023 National Veteran Suicide Prevention Annual Report documents that **57.3% of veterans who died by suicide in 2023 were not connected to VA health care or benefits at the time of death** [1]. This is not a discovery problem. Evidence-based interventions exist. It is an implementation problem: validated tools do not reach veterans in the community environments where distress accumulates before crisis [3].

**Local unmet need indicators:**
- VA enrollment gap: Approximately 40–45% of veterans nationally are not enrolled in VA healthcare. Texas enrollment rates trail the national average, meaning an estimated **40,000–47,000 veterans in the catchment area have no active VA care relationship** and are unreachable by VA-based prevention programs.
- Transition risk concentration: The corridor is adjacent to Camp Swift, Fort Cavazos, and multiple Texas National Guard installations, creating a sustained pipeline of transitioning service members entering a documented high-risk period — the year following separation carries 2–4× elevated suicide risk relative to the general veteran population [1].
- Geographic gap: The eastern Travis County and Williamson County corridor falls between urban Austin resources and rural VA outreach infrastructure — underserved relative to its veteran density.
- 988/VCL demand: Travis County consistently shows elevated VCL utilization, indicating both acute risk and insufficient local resolution capacity. Veterans who call and do not need clinical intervention have no structured community-based follow-up — M2C is designed to fill that gap.

**Estimated unmet need:** Approximately 35–40% of at-risk veterans in the catchment area lack access to any non-clinical, community-based suicide prevention service.

**VA non-enrollment estimate:** Based on Texas-specific enrollment patterns, TCAF estimates **55–65% of M2C participants will not be receiving VA-furnished care at enrollment** — the population most dependent on community-based alternatives and most aligned with SSG Fox's target population.

## B.3.b — Nontraditional and Innovative Approaches: YES

M2C delivers the **Community-Based Behavioral Health Engagement Model (CBHEM)** — a formally developed implementation science framework (Dartmouth/Geisel DHA capstone, defense June 2026) structured as a Type-1 effectiveness-implementation hybrid randomized trial [pre-registered, Open Science Framework]. The innovation is embedding validated suicide-risk detection inside trusted community relationships — instrumenting "someone checked on me" as a designed, repeatable activity with trained peer champions. Research by Deisenhammer et al. (2009) shows 48% of near-lethal attempt survivors had deliberation windows under ten minutes [2]. CBHEM is designed to reach veterans inside that window through relational infrastructure, not clinical scheduling.

Standalone digital tools without human follow-up fail this population: Owen et al. (2020) document veteran mHealth engagement dropping within weeks without peer support [8]; Lattie et al. (2022) report 50–80% attrition for public-facing digital mental health platforms [9]. M2C pairs the platform with trained peer champions to hold engagement where pure technology fails.

## F.1.a — Measuring Effects Using Pre/Post Validated Measures

TCAF will administer the following validated instruments at intake (baseline), 3 months, and program exit (6 months):

- **SBQ-R (Suicide Behaviors Questionnaire-Revised):** Primary outcome measure for the CBHEM trial. Assesses lifetime suicidal ideation, frequency, threat of attempt, and likelihood of future attempt. Validated for community-based veteran populations.
- **C-SSRS (Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale) [13]:** Administered at every contact. Tracks ideation intensity and behavior. Validated VA/DoD standard.
- **PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9):** Depression symptom tracking pre/post.
- **PCL-5 (PTSD Checklist for DSM-5) [11]:** PTSD symptom tracking at intake and exit for eligible participants.
- **GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7):** Anxiety symptom tracking at intake and exit.

Pre/post analysis will use paired t-tests for continuous outcomes and logistic regression for binary outcomes (e.g., VA enrollment status). Effect sizes will be reported using Cohen's d. All analytic decisions are pre-specified in the CBHEM trial protocol registered on the Open Science Framework.

## G.4.a — How Linkages Enhance Program Effectiveness

The seven-partner network is not decorative — it is the delivery infrastructure. M2C's non-clinical model works precisely because it hands off to clinical partners at the right moment. Without VA SPC warm hand-off protocols, elevated-risk veterans have nowhere to go. Without VSO coordination, unenrolled veterans stay unenrolled. Without Vet Center access, MST survivors lack specialized clinical support. Without United Way, navigator training lacks scale.

The linkage architecture converts the 10-minute deliberation window [2] from a crisis point into an intervention point — because every person in the M2C network knows their role, has a named contact at the adjacent organization, and has a documented follow-up protocol.
