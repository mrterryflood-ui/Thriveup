## What SoS:DCI Funds

### Core Research Areas

1. **Scientific Discovery Mechanisms** — How does scientific discovery happen? What social and structural mechanisms drive it?

2. **Scientific Communication & Outcomes** — How does scientific knowledge reach the public? What theories, frameworks, and data improve our understanding of how science is communicated?

3. **Societal Benefits of Science** — How does scientific activity create public value? How does science advance evidence-based policymaking?

### What They Want in Proposals
- Rigorous empirical research methods (not just theory)
- Theory-building and framework development
- New models, data, indicators, and analytical tools
- Credible assessment of project's impact and policy implications
- Robust data management plans for open science
- Proposals that strengthen America's global science leadership
- Convergent research and collaboration

### Special Priorities
- Strengthening and expanding the scientific workforce
- Cultivating high-impact discovery across sectors
- Broadening participation in sciences
- Early-career researchers
- Researchers from underrepresented groups and regions

## TCAF ALIGNMENT ASSESSMENT

### Where TCAF Fits: STRONG ALIGNMENT

TCAF's 24-platform AI ecosystem is not just a community program — it is a **living laboratory for studying how AI-augmented scientific tools reach and impact underserved communities.** This is precisely what SoS:DCI wants to fund.

#### Research Question 1: How does community-based AI deployment change scientific communication and impact?

TCAF deploys AI tools (RAG systems, collaborative multi-model AI, implementation science frameworks) through community infrastructure — libraries, churches, health clinics, community centers. This is a natural experiment in scientific communication and impact that no university lab can replicate.

**Researchable questions:**
- How do communities that have historically been excluded from science engage with AI-powered scientific tools?
- Does multi-model collaborative AI (consensus-driven) produce more trusted and actionable scientific communication than single-model AI for community applications?
- How does implementation science (CFIR/RE-AIM) improve the deployment of AI tools in under-resourced communities?
- What are the measurable societal benefits when communities gain access to AI-powered health, workforce, and education tools?

#### Research Question 2: How does a 24-platform AI ecosystem function as a scientific discovery mechanism?

TCAF's ecosystem is not just a service delivery system — it is a **complex adaptive system** where 24 platforms interact, share data, and generate emergent insights. This is a SoS:DCI research gold mine:

**Researchable questions:**
- How do interconnected AI platforms generate scientific insights that no single platform could produce alone? (ecosystem emergence)
- What communication patterns between AI-augmented platforms lead to faster discovery and better outcomes? (network science)
- How does the "Team of Teams" organizational model (7 triads + 1 hub) affect scientific discovery and innovation across platforms? (organizational science)
- Can multi-model AI consensus (4 engines synthesizing) serve as a new mechanism for scientific validation? (science of AI-augmented science)

#### Research Question 3: What are the societal benefits and policy implications of community-first AI deployment?

This maps directly to SoS:DCI's emphasis on "societal benefits of scientific activity" and "evidence-based policy making":

**Researchable questions:**
- What are the measurable economic, health, and education outcomes when under-resourced communities gain access to AI-powered scientific tools?
- How does AI-powered benefits enrollment screening (the Benefits Intelligence System) change public value creation?
- What policy frameworks are needed to support community-based AI infrastructure as public goods?
- How does veteran-founded, Black-led organizational leadership change who benefits from scientific innovation?

## GAP ASSESSMENT

### GAP 1: Research Design — CRITICAL
**Issue:** SoS:DCI is a RESEARCH program. It funds the STUDY of how science works, not the DELIVERY of science programs. TCAF's core strength is delivery. The proposal must frame TCAF as a research site/natural experiment, not just a program.
- **Mitigation:** Partner with a university researcher who can design the study. TCAF provides the phenomenon (24-platform ecosystem, community deployment data). The university provides the research methodology.
- **Status: NEEDS UNIVERSITY RESEARCH PARTNER**

### GAP 2: PI with Social Science Research Credentials — LARGELY RESOLVED
**Issue:** SoS:DCI sits in NSF's Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences Directorate (SBE). The PI needs social science research credentials.
- **Dr. Flood IS a social scientist.** His MS in Industrial-Organizational Psychology (Walden University) is a core SBE discipline — organizational behavior, workforce systems, measurement, and applied research methods are exactly what SoS:DCI funds.
- **Additional credentials:** DHA, DBA, MS Human Resource Management, MS Criminal Justice (Public Policy), MS Implementation Science (in progress, Dartmouth), Graduate Certificate Business Analytics (Texas A&M), BS Healthcare Management
- **Federal role:** Public Health Social Scientist at VA + DoD (2017–present) — this is literally a social scientist job title in the federal system
- **Military:** U.S. Army Warrant Officer (Retired), CR2I Advisor (DoD 2021-2023)
- **Remaining gap:** NSF reviewers look for publication track record in peer-reviewed journals. Dr. Flood's strength is practitioner expertise and federal implementation, not academic publishing. A university Co-PI with a publication record in science-of-science or I/O journals would complement perfectly.
- **Status: PI CREDENTIALS STRONG — Co-PI recommended for publication record, not for credential type**

### GAP 3: Theoretical Framework — PARTIALLY RESOLVED
**Issue:** SoS:DCI explicitly requires proposals to "draw from and advance theory, knowledge and frameworks on the science of science." TCAF uses implementation science (CFIR/RE-AIM) — and Dr. Flood's I/O Psychology training provides additional theoretical grounding in organizational systems, change management, and sociotechnical systems.
- **I/O Psychology frameworks directly applicable:**
  - Sociotechnical Systems Theory — how technology and human systems interact (the 24-platform ecosystem IS a sociotechnical system)
  - Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) / UTAUT — how communities adopt AI tools
  - Organizational Learning Theory — how the ecosystem generates and spreads knowledge
  - Job Demands-Resources Model — how AI tools change workforce capacity
- **Additional SoS frameworks to integrate:**
  - Diffusion of Innovation Theory (Rogers) — how AI tools diffuse through community networks
  - Knowledge Translation Framework — how scientific AI tools are adopted by non-scientific communities
  - Responsible Research and Innovation (RRI) — inclusive, anticipatory approach to AI
  - Network Science — how the 24-platform ecosystem generates emergent discovery
- **Status: STRONG THEORETICAL FOUNDATION — needs formal SoS framing in proposal writing**

### GAP 4: Publication Track Record
**Issue:** SoS:DCI reviewers expect PIs with published research in science-of-science journals (e.g., Science, Research Policy, Scientometrics, Science Communication, Minerva).
- **Mitigation:** Co-PI from university provides this. TCAF provides the research site and data access.
- **Status: NEEDS CO-PI TO RESOLVE**

### GAP 5: Data Management and Open Science Plan
**Issue:** SoS:DCI places high priority on open science. TCAF has data infrastructure (Census ACS, SDOH Explorer, platform data) but needs a formal NSF-compliant Data Management Plan.
- TCAF's public evidence chain at thrivingcommunitiesforall.com/sdoh-explorer is a strength
- Need formal plan for data archiving, sharing, and public access
- **Status: MEDIUM — can be addressed in proposal writing**

## RECOMMENDED APPROACH

### Framing: "Studying the Community AI Ecosystem as a Scientific Innovation System"

**Proposed Title:**
"Community-Based AI Ecosystems as Scientific Innovation Infrastructure: Discovery, Communication, and Impact in Historically Marginalized Communities"

**Key Argument:**
TCAF's 24-platform AI ecosystem, deployed through community infrastructure across the United States, represents a new form of scientific innovation system — one built BY and FOR historically marginalized communities. This research studies how this system functions as a mechanism of scientific discovery (how community-level data generates new health, workforce, and education insights), scientific communication (how AI-powered tools translate scientific knowledge for non-scientific audiences), and societal impact (how community-first AI deployment creates measurable public value).

### Research Design Sketch

**Study Type:** Mixed-methods longitudinal study

**Phase 1 (Year 1): Mapping the Ecosystem as a Scientific System**
- Network analysis of the 24-platform ecosystem: information flows, data exchanges, emergent patterns
- CFIR 2.0 assessment of each platform's role in scientific discovery/communication
- Baseline measures of community AI engagement across deployment sites
- Qualitative interviews with community members using AI tools

**Phase 2 (Year 2): Measuring Communication and Impact**
- Pre/post assessment of scientific literacy in communities using TCAF AI tools
- Tracking how AI-generated insights (benefits screening, health navigation) translate into community action
- Comparing outcomes in TCAF-served communities vs. matched comparison communities
- Network evolution analysis — how does the ecosystem change over time?

**Phase 3 (Year 3): Policy Implications and Replication**
- Policy analysis: what governance structures support community AI ecosystems?
- Cost-effectiveness analysis: public value per dollar invested
- Replication assessment: what elements are transferable to other communities/states?
- Publication of research findings and open datasets
