## Strategic frame

TCAF is not applying to reproduce The Black Fund of Central Texas's grantmaking or capacity-building role. The Fund moves capital into Black communities. TCAF supplies a different layer: the shared implementation, intelligence, navigation, training, and accountability infrastructure that helps community organizations, residents, schools, healthcare systems, veteran-serving programs, and other institutions turn resources into coordinated action.

The $15,000 request is not intended to build TCAF's platform from scratch. TCAF has already built the infrastructure. This request would activate and localize that infrastructure across five Central Texas counties so Black-led and Black-serving organizations can connect their work instead of operating through separate referrals, separate data systems, separate funding cycles, and separate definitions of success.

TCAF will activate an existing Black-led community operating infrastructure across five Central Texas counties, connecting community voice, public data, health, education, workforce, veteran, justice, benefits, referral, training, and implementation systems so local organizations can coordinate action, protect continuity of care, and demonstrate outcomes without surrendering their identity or autonomy.

## Executive summary

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation is a Black-led, veteran-founded 501(c)(3) public charity that operates a national-capable community infrastructure platform with a Central Texas implementation focus. TCAF does not function as another isolated direct-service program. It serves as connective infrastructure across the systems that shape whether a person, family, organization, or neighborhood can thrive.

TCAF's operating model brings together 15 service platforms and specialized engines spanning community navigation, behavioral and whole-person health, Black maternal and men's health, education, workforce development, skilled trades, foster-youth transition, justice and reentry, veteran transition, benefits access, civic voice, community evidence, implementation science, and funding readiness. These capabilities are coordinated through ThriveUp and TCAF's Initial Guidance and Navigation model.

This grant would support a five-county Central Texas activation of that existing infrastructure. TCAF will work with Black-led and Black-serving organizations and institutional partners to map where services and handoffs break down, connect referral and follow-up pathways, convert community and public data into actionable intelligence, train trusted local implementers, and establish a privacy-protective measurement system.

The result will not be another directory, another one-time workshop, or another report that stops at describing disparities. It will be a functioning community continuity layer: people can move from need to next step; organizations can coordinate without giving up their independence; community knowledge can shape implementation; and funders can see what was delivered, where gaps remain, and what should happen next.

The Black Fund's investment would be catalytic. It would localize infrastructure that is already built and make it usable by Black Central Texas communities at a scale larger than the grant itself.

## Organizational overview

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation helps nonprofits, residents, and communities move through the full cycle of community change:

**Research → Planning → Preparation → Execution → Continuous Assessment → Implementation → Scale Up → Scale Out**

TCAF's approach is called **Initial Guidance and Navigation (IGN)**. IGN meets people and organizations at their actual level of readiness, identifies the safest and most useful next step, supports execution, measures whether the intervention is working, and redirects when conditions change.

TCAF combines five operating lenses:

1. implementation science;
2. psychology and behavioral science;
3. data engineering and systems interoperability;
4. community health worker practice; and
5. user-centered design.

This combination allows TCAF to work across institutional boundaries without reducing a community problem to one program category. A family may encounter housing instability, school disruption, behavioral-health needs, employment barriers, benefits questions, transportation problems, or justice-system involvement at the same time. TCAF's infrastructure is designed to preserve the full context, connect the right specialist pathways, and keep a human accountable for consequential decisions.

TCAF is the nonprofit applicant. ISS LLC is a separate for-profit entity and is not the applicant or recipient under this request.

## The need: fragmentation, not a lack of community effort

Black communities in Central Texas are not short on knowledge, leadership, or effort. The structural problem is that the systems surrounding that work are fragmented.

A resident may be known separately to a school, clinic, workforce program, veteran organization, benefits office, faith community, or local nonprofit, yet no one is responsible for continuity across those contacts. A community organization may provide trusted support but lack access to regional data, interoperable referral tools, implementation measurement, or the evidence needed to influence institutional decisions. A school or healthcare system may identify a need but rely on a referral list that cannot confirm whether the person reached help.

The same fragmentation affects funding. Organizations are often required to translate complex work into isolated grant categories, use different measures for each funder, and repeatedly rebuild narratives and evidence. Grantwriting support alone does not solve that problem. The deeper need is shared infrastructure that makes services, community voice, data, implementation, and outcomes connect.

Without that connective layer:

- people repeat their story across systems;
- referrals become cold handoffs with no accountable next step;
- small organizations bear administrative burdens designed for larger institutions;
- community knowledge is collected without changing implementation;
- schools, healthcare systems, veteran programs, and nonprofits work from partial views of the same reality;
- data describes inequity without creating a response pathway; and
- funding supports individual projects without strengthening the surrounding community system.

TCAF was built to close that systems-level gap.

## Proposed use of The Black Fund investment

### Goal

Activate a Black community continuity and implementation layer across five Central Texas counties so that trusted organizations and institutions can coordinate services, use evidence, close referral gaps, and strengthen the systems that support Black residents.

### Workstream 1 — Five-county community intelligence

TCAF will configure a five-county community intelligence view that combines:

- public demographic, health, education, workforce, housing, and justice indicators;
- community-identified priorities;
- service availability and capacity;
- referral and handoff gaps;
- implementation barriers and facilitators; and
- evidence appropriate for community, organizational, institutional, and funder decisions.

This will not be a static needs assessment. Each identified gap will be connected to a possible action, responsible stakeholder, existing service pathway, or documented infrastructure need.

### Workstream 2 — Cross-system continuity pathways

TCAF will map and activate practical continuity pathways among participating:

- Black-led and Black-serving community organizations;
- school systems and youth-serving programs;
- healthcare and behavioral-health systems;
- veteran and military-family services;
- workforce, apprenticeship, and economic-mobility programs;
- justice, reentry, and diversion programs;
- benefits and wraparound-service providers; and
- trusted community navigators and informal helpers.

The work will define who owns the next step, what information may be shared, how consent is documented, how a referral is followed, and what happens when the intended destination is unavailable.

### Workstream 3 — Local implementation and training

TCAF will train participating organizations and trusted implementers to use the infrastructure for their own mission. Training will include:

- Initial Guidance and Navigation;
- warm referral and follow-up practice;
- data interpretation and responsible storytelling;
- implementation planning and fidelity;
- privacy, consent, and small-cell protection;
- community voice and non-extractive engagement;
- outcome documentation; and
- funding readiness as a downstream use of stronger operations and evidence.

TCAF is not asking organizations to become technology companies or to replace their current systems. Partners can use the components that fit their work while retaining their own identity, leadership, programs, and funder relationships.

### Workstream 4 — Community evidence and accountability

TCAF will create a shared learning and accountability structure that answers:

- Who was reached?
- Which organizations and settings adopted the pathway?
- Was the work delivered as intended?
- Did the referral or intervention reach a documented next step?
- Where did the process fail?
- Did outcomes differ by geography, population, or institutional setting?
- What must be adapted, maintained, expanded, or stopped?

The approach will use implementation-science dimensions including reach, effectiveness, adoption, implementation, and maintenance. It will distinguish observed outcomes, partner-reported information, modeled values, and future targets.

## What makes this different

### It begins where conventional capacity building ends

Traditional capacity building may provide a workshop, strategic plan, grant narrative, or software subscription. TCAF connects those elements to execution and follow-through. The organization can move from community need to evidence, from evidence to action, from action to measurement, and from measurement to funding and scale.

### It works across sectors

TCAF does not assume that health, education, employment, housing, justice, veteran transition, and family stability occur in separate lives. The infrastructure coordinates specialist pathways while preserving each partner's role.

### It strengthens—not replaces—trusted organizations

TCAF is a backbone, not a consolidator. Participating organizations retain their autonomy, identity, community relationships, programs, and existing funder relationships. The shared layer reduces duplication and gives smaller organizations access to infrastructure they would not reasonably build alone.

### It turns data into accountable action

The project does not stop at dashboards or disparity descriptions. Data must identify a decision, pathway, intervention, ownership point, or unresolved gap.

### It protects community knowledge

Community participation is consent-based. TCAF does not treat stories as free raw material. Personal information is excluded from public and funder-facing reporting; small groups are suppressed when counts could expose individuals; and community stories are not repurposed without permission.

### It is larger than this grant

The $15,000 request does not represent the cost or value of the full TCAF infrastructure. It is a catalytic local activation investment. The Fund would help five Central Texas counties use an existing national-capable system while preserving a locally governed implementation.

## Relationship to The Black Fund of Central Texas

This project complements the Fund rather than duplicating it.

The Black Fund identifies and invests in Black-led and Black-serving organizations. TCAF helps create the infrastructure through which those organizations and surrounding institutions can coordinate delivery, document implementation, connect residents to complete pathways, and convert community evidence into stronger decisions and sustainable resources.

The grant would therefore have two levels of value:

1. **Direct value:** participating organizations and trusted implementers gain usable community intelligence, continuity pathways, training, and measurement support.
2. **Multiplier value:** the infrastructure can strengthen how multiple organizations connect residents, institutions, data, and future funding beyond the initial grant period.

TCAF will not claim to represent or manage The Black Fund's grantees unless separately invited and authorized. Participation will be voluntary, consent-based, and governed by each organization's needs.

## Evaluation and learning

TCAF will establish a baseline at onboarding and review progress throughout the project using:

- **Reach:** who participated and who was not reached;
- **Effectiveness:** whether the pathway produced a documented useful next step;
- **Adoption:** which organizations, systems, and trusted implementers used the model;
- **Implementation:** whether the work was delivered as intended and where it changed;
- **Maintenance:** which practices and pathways remained in use;
- **Equity:** whether results or barriers differed across geography and stakeholder groups; and
- **Continuity:** whether referrals and actions ended with accountable follow-up rather than a cold handoff.

TCAF will use MAP-GAP continuous improvement:

**Map → Analyze → Plan → Execute → Reassess**

The project will not manufacture a success story. Unresolved gaps, failed handoffs, limited adoption, and implementation barriers will be documented because they are necessary for improvement and responsible future investment.

## Equity, dignity, privacy, and human authority

TCAF will apply the following safeguards:

- plan with communities, not for them;
- obtain clear consent before sharing or aggregating participant information;
- keep all consequential service, health, legal, education, and safety decisions human-led;
- exclude personally identifying information from public and funder-facing reports;
- suppress small cells that could expose individuals;
- label modeled values and never present them as observed outcomes;
- retain source and date information for evidence;
- avoid requiring people to repeat traumatic or sensitive information unnecessarily;
- provide language-access pathways across 107 languages;
- compensate or credential community contributors when their labor extends beyond ordinary participation; and
- preserve each participating organization's ownership of its mission, relationships, and story.

## Draft response: Why now?

Central Texas is growing, but growth does not automatically create continuity or equity. Black residents and the organizations that serve them still encounter systems that divide health from education, employment from family stability, veteran transition from behavioral health, and community evidence from institutional decisions.

The region does not need another disconnected pilot that disappears when a grant ends. It needs connective infrastructure that strengthens what trusted organizations are already doing and makes their combined work easier to navigate, improve, and sustain.

TCAF has built the underlying infrastructure. The immediate opportunity is to activate it locally, with Black-led and Black-serving organizations helping determine what connects, what remains separate, what evidence is useful, and what the next accountable action should be.

---

## Draft response: Why TCAF?

TCAF is positioned between community knowledge and institutional execution. It combines implementation science, psychology, data engineering, community health worker practice, education, and user-centered design in one operating model.

Its infrastructure can connect a resident journey, a community organization's work, public evidence, institutional partners, referral follow-up, workforce and education pathways, health and safety resources, and funder accountability without collapsing those roles into one organization.

TCAF's value is not that it offers the same service as every partner. Its value is that it helps the parts work together, identifies where continuity fails, and gives communities and institutions a shared way to act and learn.

---

## Draft response: How will Black communities shape the work?

Black community members and Black-led organizations will not be treated only as beneficiaries or sources of stories. They will help define priorities, interpret evidence, identify where formal systems miss lived reality, test whether pathways are usable, and decide what adaptations are needed.

Participation will be voluntary and consent-based. Organizations will retain their identity and data ownership. TCAF will provide the shared infrastructure and implementation support while local participants determine how that infrastructure should operate in their context.

---

## Draft response: What will success look like?

Success will mean more than producing documents or holding workshops. It will mean that participating organizations and systems can point to:

- a community priority connected to an accountable action;
- a referral pathway with ownership, follow-up, and a fallback;
- a trusted implementer who knows how to use the pathway;
- evidence that is current, sourced, understandable, and privacy-protective;
- a documented implementation barrier and adaptation;
- a resident or organization reaching the next useful step without restarting from zero; and
- stronger evidence for future funding, policy, or institutional decisions.

TCAF will report observed results separately from partner reports, estimates, and future targets.
