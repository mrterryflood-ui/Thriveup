## Proposed project title

**Black Central Texas Capacity and Navigation Lab**

## Short project description

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation will strengthen the ability of Black-led and Black-serving organizations in Central Texas to secure resources, tell their stories with trustworthy data, coordinate referrals, and work more effectively with schools, healthcare systems, veteran-serving organizations, and other community institutions. With support from The Black Fund of Central Texas, TCAF will provide practical capacity-building, grant-readiness and data-storytelling support, implementation training, and a coordinated navigation backbone through ThriveUp.

The project is not a technology purchase presented as an end in itself. ThriveUp is the operating backbone that helps TCAF turn partner information into usable plans, funder-ready evidence, warm referrals, and follow-through. The purpose is stronger Black community-serving organizations and better access to the services, training, and opportunities those organizations provide.

## Organizational overview

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation (TCAF) is a Texas 501(c)(3) public charity whose work helps nonprofits, residents, and communities move from identified needs to coordinated action. TCAF combines community health worker practice, implementation science, behavioral and social-work perspectives, education, data engineering, and community-serving technology.

TCAF's approach is Initial Guidance and Navigation (IGN): meet people and organizations where they are, understand their goals and readiness, provide practical tools and information, coordinate the next step, and assess whether the plan is working. TCAF does not treat data as an extraction exercise. Data is translated into information that community organizations can use for decisions, storytelling, accountability, and follow-up.

TCAF's nonprofit identity is separate from ISS LLC, a different for-profit entity. This application is submitted by TCAF, not ISS LLC.

## Statement of need

Black-led and Black-serving organizations often carry complex community responsibilities without the staff time, data infrastructure, grant capacity, or cross-system coordination needed to sustain that work. A community organization may know what families, veterans, young people, or neighbors need, yet still face barriers to:

- translating community knowledge into a clear, credible data story;
- identifying grants that fit its actual mission and capacity;
- preparing a complete and competitive application;
- coordinating referrals across nonprofit, school, healthcare, workforce, and veteran-serving systems;
- following up after a referral rather than losing the person between organizations; and
- showing funders what changed without exposing private information or overstating results.

These gaps are not a lack of commitment by community organizations. They are capacity and infrastructure gaps. When they remain unresolved, resources are harder to secure, services remain disconnected, and residents are asked to repeat their story to each new system.

TCAF proposes a practical response: strengthen the organizations closest to the work, improve the quality and usefulness of their stories and evidence, and create repeatable coordination practices that can continue after this grant period.

## Project goals

1. **Build organizational capacity.** Help participating organizations clarify goals, workflows, funding readiness, measurement, and next steps.
2. **Improve data storytelling.** Turn partner-approved information and community knowledge into concise, understandable stories for funders, boards, stakeholders, and residents.
3. **Increase grant readiness.** Provide grant opportunity alignment, application planning, narrative development, budget logic, and review support.
4. **Strengthen cross-system navigation.** Improve warm referrals and follow-through among community organizations, schools, healthcare systems, veteran-serving organizations, workforce resources, and wraparound services.
5. **Create a repeatable learning model.** Document what partners use, what barriers remain, and what practices can be sustained or expanded.

## Core activities

### 1. Capacity and readiness sessions

TCAF will conduct structured sessions with participating organizations to identify their immediate operating priorities, service pathways, funding readiness, data assets, and constraints. Each organization will leave with an actionable plan rather than a generic assessment.

Topics may include:

- mission-to-program alignment;
- service and referral mapping;
- grant-readiness and funder-fit review;
- basic outcomes and evidence planning;
- board and stakeholder storytelling;
- privacy-aware data practices; and
- implementation follow-through.

### 2. Data storytelling support

TCAF will help participating organizations explain their work in plain language and with evidence appropriate to the audience. Products may include a one-page impact brief, a funder-facing narrative, a community-facing story, a simple outcome view, or a presentation outline.

TCAF will distinguish observed information, partner-reported information, modeled information, and future targets. It will not turn estimates into outcomes or publish small-cell information that could identify people.

### 3. Grantwriting and funding readiness

TCAF will provide practical support for organizations seeking funding, including:

- opportunity screening and fit review;
- application work plans and compliance checklists;
- narrative and budget alignment;
- reviewer-oriented editing;
- evidence and attachment planning; and
- post-submission learning and next-cycle readiness.

The goal is not for TCAF to replace the partner's voice. TCAF will help each organization present its own mission, community knowledge, and evidence more clearly.

### 4. School, healthcare, veteran, and wraparound coordination

Where a participating organization serves residents who interact with multiple systems, TCAF will help map the handoffs among schools, healthcare systems, veteran-serving resources, workforce services, benefits programs, family supports, and other wraparound services.

The project will emphasize:

- warm referrals rather than passive lists;
- clear ownership of the next step;
- consent-aware information sharing;
- simple status and follow-up practices; and
- escalation to a human navigator when a need is urgent or outside the platform's role.

TCAF will not provide medical, legal, or emergency decisions through artificial intelligence. Technology will support human judgment, coordination, and documentation.

### 5. Training and shared learning

TCAF will offer training for participating organizations and designated staff, peer navigators, community health workers, or other trusted community helpers. Training will focus on using the tools, interpreting information, telling a responsible story, and maintaining a referral loop.

Participation will be designed around readiness and practical use. TCAF will not require residents or community helpers to surrender their stories or personal information as a condition of participation.

## Role of ThriveUp

ThriveUp is the integrated backbone for this work. It brings together funding intelligence, community context, grant-readiness tools, service navigation, referral workflows, learning resources, and outcome reporting so that TCAF can support partners through one coordinated operating model.

The grant would fund the human and organizational capacity needed to use that backbone well:

- facilitation and partner support;
- data-story development;
- grant-readiness coaching;
- training and materials;
- referral and follow-up design; and
- responsible measurement and learning.

The project will remain useful to organizations even when a partner uses only selected tools or needs primarily human coaching. The objective is stronger community capacity, not platform adoption for its own sake.

## Outcomes and measurement

TCAF will use a simple baseline-to-follow-up approach. Measures will be disaggregated only when doing so is safe and useful; small cells will be suppressed.

Possible measures, pending applicant approval:

- number of organizations completing a capacity plan;
- number of organizations completing a grant-readiness plan;
- number of funder-ready data stories or briefs produced;
- number of grant applications or letters of intent supported;
- number of referral pathways documented with a named next step;
- percentage of participating organizations reporting increased readiness;
- number of training participants completing the core sessions;
- number of referrals with documented follow-up; and
- partner-reported examples of improved coordination or funding readiness.

TCAF will report what was actually observed, what was reported by partners, and what remains a target. It will not claim that a grant caused an outcome that the project did not measure.

## Equity, dignity, and responsible data

This project is designed for and with Black communities and the organizations that serve them. TCAF will:

- center partner and resident voice rather than treating people as data points;
- use plain language and honor language access needs;
- make participation and data-sharing choices understandable;
- keep consent and information-sharing boundaries explicit;
- avoid publishing personally identifying information;
- suppress small cells and avoid turning modeled values into observed outcomes;
- keep a human decision-maker accountable for service and referral choices; and
- provide a human handoff when a situation involves urgent safety, health, legal, or crisis needs.

Community knowledge will not be treated as free labor. If community helpers contribute stories, review, or interpretation beyond ordinary participation, TCAF will define recognition, stipend, or credentialing arrangements before asking for that contribution.

## Sustainability

The project is designed to leave participating organizations with reusable skills and materials:

- a capacity and funding-readiness plan;
- a repeatable data-story template;
- a grant opportunity and application workflow;
- a mapped referral pathway with follow-up responsibility;
- training materials that can be reused with new staff; and
- a small set of safe, understandable measures for future reporting.

TCAF will use the learning from the project to improve future support for Central Texas organizations and to pursue additional funding for partner capacity-building. Any future expansion will be based on documented partner needs and results, not on assumed technology adoption.

## Draft response: Why this project matters now

Black communities and the organizations serving them should not have to choose between doing the work and building the systems needed to sustain the work. TCAF sees organizations with strong relationships and valuable community knowledge that need practical support to secure funding, explain their impact, coordinate referrals, and work across institutions.

This project responds at the level where a modest grant can create durable value: the organizations and trusted helpers closest to residents. By strengthening their capacity, improving their stories, and making cross-system follow-up more reliable, TCAF can help resources travel farther and make it easier for residents to reach the support they are already seeking.

## Draft response: Why TCAF

TCAF combines nonprofit capacity-building, implementation science, community health worker practice, education, data, and funding knowledge. Its IGN approach is practical: understand the starting point, identify the next safe and useful step, support execution, assess what happened, and adjust.

TCAF also has an integrated operating backbone in ThriveUp that can connect grant readiness, data storytelling, service navigation, training, and outcome reporting. That infrastructure allows TCAF to support partners without asking each organization to build a separate system from scratch. The platform remains a means to strengthen Black community-serving organizations; TCAF's value is the combination of human support, community-centered practice, and responsible infrastructure.
