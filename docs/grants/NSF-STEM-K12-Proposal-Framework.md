### The Ecosystem Difference

A student on ThriveUp Academy is not on a learning platform. They are on a community infrastructure platform where their education is connected to their health, their behavioral health, their family's stability, their workforce trajectory, and their community resources. ## SECTION 1: PROJECT SUMMARY (1 page)

### Title
"Community Infrastructure for STEM: How a 24-Platform Ecosystem Improves K-12 STEM Outcomes by Seeing the Whole Child, the Whole Family, and the Whole Community"

### Overview
This project investigates whether embedding K-12 STEM education within a community infrastructure ecosystem — where health, behavioral health, workforce, family services, criminal justice, and community resources are interconnected — improves STEM learning outcomes for underrepresented students compared to standalone educational technology.

ThriveUp is a 24-platform ecosystem across 6 domains, powered by a 4-engine AI intelligence layer. The education domain serves Pre-K through graduate and professional development. When a STEM student struggles, the platform doesn't just provide academic intervention — it identifies whether the root cause is academic, familial, economic, medical, psychological, or systemic, and activates the appropriate platforms automatically.

### Intellectual Merit
- Novel application of implementation science (CFIR 2.0, RE-AIM) to study community-integrated STEM education
- First study of whether cross-domain ecosystem support (health + workforce + family + community) improves K-12 STEM outcomes
- Multi-model AI architecture (4 engines, consensus-verified) applied to personalized STEM learning — no single point of failure
- Practitioner-researcher PI with both Dartmouth implementation science training and 25 years of direct community service

### Broader Impacts
- Directly serves populations underrepresented in STEM: Black, Latino, veteran families, justice-involved youth, rural, immigrant, first-generation, students with disabilities
- Community infrastructure model is replicable nationally — same architecture, any community
- Open dissemination of research findings and implementation frameworks through RPLICE
- Contributes to national priorities: AI readiness, STEM workforce development, equity in education

## SECTION 2: PROJECT DESCRIPTION (15 pages)

### 2.1 The Problem (2 pages)

**$180 billion in social services, disconnected from education.**

The United States treats STEM education as if it exists in a vacuum. A student's ability to learn chemistry is assumed to depend on the curriculum, the teacher, and the student's effort. In reality, it depends on whether:
- Her mother is healthy or in cancer treatment
- Her father has a job or just lost one
- Her family has stable housing or just got evicted
- Her sibling is home from prison or still incarcerated
- She has food, transportation, and childcare for younger siblings
- Her mental health is stable or in crisis
- Her family has access to legal aid, benefits, and community resources

These are not edge cases. For underrepresented students in underserved communities, these are the DEFAULT conditions. Yet no STEM education platform connects to any of them.

**The fragmentation statistics:**
- 1.2 million students drop out of U.S. high schools annually
- Black students are 2× more likely to be chronically absent (often due to family instability, not disengagement)
- Students in poverty score 25+ points lower on NAEP math assessments
- 67% of returning citizens' children experience academic decline during parental incarceration
- Children of veterans with PTSD show measurably higher rates of school behavioral problems
- 8+ disconnected systems serve the average family in need — none of them connected to the school

**Current approaches fail because they:**
- Treat the student as an individual divorced from family and community context
- Provide academic interventions without addressing root causes of academic struggle
- Operate in silos — the school, the health clinic, the workforce board, the reentry program never exchange information
- Use single-purpose technology that can't see beyond its own domain

### 2.2 The ThriveUp Approach (3 pages)

**A community infrastructure platform where education is connected to everything else.**

ThriveUp is not a STEM app. It is a 24-platform ecosystem across 6 domains — Criminal Justice & Safety, Health Equity, Behavioral Health, Workforce & Business, Education & Learning, and Community & Advocacy — connected by a 4-engine AI intelligence layer. Every platform sees the others. Every platform can flag gaps, escalate needs, and trigger services across domains.

**The education domain** includes ThriveUp Academy (Pre-K through graduate, TEKS-aligned, WIOA-compliant), WholeMind Learning (visual-first, accessibility-focused), Perfectly Different (special education), LexiBridge (multilingual), AI Mastery Academy (5-level AI literacy curriculum), and Better Science Lab / RPLICE (implementation science research backbone).

**The innovation is not the curriculum. The innovation is the connection.**

When a STEM student's grades decline, ThriveUp doesn't just flag the grade. The AI intelligence layer checks across all 24 platforms:
- Has a parent lost employment? (Workforce domain)
- Has a family member been diagnosed with a serious illness? (Health domain)
- Has a family member returned from incarceration? (Criminal Justice domain)
- Has the family's housing become unstable? (Community domain)
- Has the student's mental health screening changed? (Behavioral Health domain)

If the answer to any of these is yes, the education platform doesn't just provide a tutor. It activates the appropriate domains:
- ISSS wraps youth supports around the student
- Collaborative Advocate coordinates family resources
- Whole-Person Health connects the student to a counselor
- Mission Transition or Ecosystem Nexus helps the parent find work
- LifeBridge supports reentry for the returning family member

**Illustrative Case: Keisha's Daughter**

Keisha is a veteran, a mother, a breast cancer survivor who discovers she's pregnant during treatment. She has depression. She runs a small nonprofit. Her daughter's grades drop from B's to D's.

The school sees: a struggling student.
The hospital sees: a cancer patient.
The workforce board sees: an employee.
Nobody sees: the family.

ThriveUp sees the family. ISSS wraps tutoring, mentoring, counseling, and after-school support around the daughter — because the ecosystem knows WHY she's struggling. It's not a school problem. It's a family in crisis. And when Keisha's brother Marcus comes home from prison, the ecosystem already knows the family. LifeBridge connects to the existing family file. Marcus's reentry doesn't destabilize the daughter further — it's coordinated.

### 2.3 Research Design (4 pages)

**Framework: Implementation Science (CFIR 2.0 + RE-AIM)**

The research uses the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR 2.0) to study how ThriveUp's ecosystem approach is implemented across diverse K-12 settings, and RE-AIM (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance) to measure outcomes.

**RPLICE Three Realities Methodology:**
1. **Designed Reality** — What was ThriveUp designed to do? (Platform architecture, curriculum design, AI capabilities)
2. **Operational Reality** — What is actually happening? (Implementation fidelity, cross-domain activation rates, AI alert accuracy)
3. **Experienced Reality** — What do students, families, educators, and community partners experience? (Qualitative interviews, satisfaction, learning gains)

**Research Questions:**
1. Does embedding K-12 STEM education within a community infrastructure ecosystem improve STEM learning outcomes (test scores, course grades, STEM course-taking) for underrepresented students?
2. Does cross-domain ecosystem support (health, behavioral health, workforce, family, community) reduce chronic absenteeism and improve persistence for students in underserved communities?
3. What implementation factors (per CFIR 2.0) predict successful adoption of ecosystem-integrated STEM education across diverse school and community settings?
4. How does the AI intelligence layer's cross-domain risk detection compare to traditional school-based early warning systems in identifying students at risk of STEM disengagement?

**Study Design:**
- **Sites:** Central Texas (Pflugerville, Austin, Killeen/Ft. Cavazos region) — 3 school districts, each with 2-3 participating schools
- **Participants:** 500-800 students (Grades 6-12), prioritizing Black, Latino, veteran-family, justice-involved, and rural students
- **Comparison:** Pre/post within the ecosystem; comparison with matched schools using standalone STEM tools
- **Duration:** 3 years (Year 1: Implementation + baseline; Year 2: Full deployment + data collection; Year 3: Analysis + dissemination)
- **Measures:**
  - STEM outcomes: Course grades, standardized test scores, STEM course enrollment, AP/dual-credit participation
  - Persistence: Attendance, chronic absenteeism, graduation rate, STEM pathway continuation
  - Ecosystem activation: How often do cross-domain platforms activate for education-flagged students? What domains activate most frequently?
  - AI detection: Sensitivity and specificity of AI early warning vs. traditional school-based systems
  - Implementation fidelity: CFIR 2.0 constructs across all participating sites
  - Experienced reality: Student, family, educator interviews (n=60-80)

### 2.4 AI Architecture (2 pages)

**4-Engine Collaborative Intelligence**

ThriveUp uses four AI models in parallel — GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini, and DeepSeek — with a consensus verification protocol. No single model makes critical decisions alone.

**Why this matters for STEM education:**
1. **Cross-domain pattern detection** — The AI sees data across all 24 platforms. A single-domain AI can see that a student's math grade dropped. ThriveUp's AI can see that the math grade dropped AND the parent's workforce file shows a job loss AND the behavioral health screening flagged depression AND the community platform shows a housing instability flag. One data point vs. four.
2. **Hallucination prevention** — Four models cross-check each other. A recommendation to hold a student back must be validated by at least 3 of 4 engines. This prevents any single AI from making consequential errors about a child's education.
3. **Adaptive personalization** — The AI adjusts STEM content not just based on quiz performance, but on the learner's full context: reading level (LexiBridge), cognitive status (SafeCogniCare), emotional state (WholeMind Learning), and family stability (ecosystem-wide).

### 2.5 Evaluation Plan (2 pages)

**RPLICE as the Evaluation Backbone**

Better Science Lab / RPLICE provides the evaluation infrastructure:
- **CFIR 2.0 Assessment** — Systematic evaluation of implementation across all CFIR domains: Innovation, Outer Setting, Inner Setting, Individuals, Implementation Process
- **RE-AIM Framework** — Reach (who participates), Effectiveness (does it work), Adoption (do schools take it up), Implementation (fidelity), Maintenance (sustainability)
- **MAP-GAP Continuous Quality Improvement** — Ongoing cycle of observation, gap identification, prioritized action, and validation throughout the project period
- **SDOH Impact Chain Analysis** — Census-tract level analysis connecting social determinants to STEM outcomes (not county averages — tract-level precision)
