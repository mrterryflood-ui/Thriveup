## 1. Describe the services your firm will provide that meet the Scope of Service and Performance Requirements

ThriveUp Academy is the K–12 + adult-learner program division of The Collaborative Advocate Foundation (TCAF), a Texas-headquartered 501(c)(3) operating a national community-infrastructure platform with a Travis County implementation footprint. We propose to serve Austin ISD across three of the four Component Activity areas (Enrichment, Family Engagement, College & Career Readiness), drawing on a production-deployed program platform that already supports:

- **A 90-lesson trade-skill curriculum** across six trades — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, welding, automotive, and software engineering — each delivered as a 15-lesson sequence with formative practice banks, an AI tutor for guided hints (Socratic mode), apprenticeship-pathway routing, and a 6-certification ladder gated at 80% lesson completion. The Software Engineering track is the basis of the sample unit plan included with this response.
- **AI Literacy and Computer Science enrichment modules** designed for grades 3–12, age-banded, with TEKS-alignment crosswalks for Technology Applications strands.
- **A multilingual family-engagement layer** ("Talk Your Talk") covering **89 spoken languages + 18 signed languages = 107 total surfaces**, with dialect-aware system prompts (including AAVE and Spanglish) and right-to-left rendering for Arabic. This addresses AISD's 28.1% ELL student population and their families directly.
- **FAFSA navigation and apprenticeship-pathway counseling** for high-school College & Career Readiness, with built-in eligibility logic for foster-youth FAFSA independent status, McKinney-Vento housing transitions, Chafee ETV, and registered-apprenticeship intake.
- **Family-Engagement programming aligned to the National PTA Standards** for parental involvement: two-way home-school communication, parenting-skills support, parents-as-partners in student learning, parents-as-school-decision partners, and community-resource outreach.
- **An evidence-based research foundation** drawing on the U.S. Department of Education's "What Works" Clearinghouse OST guidance, the Harvard Family Research Project on family engagement, the Weikart Center Youth Program Quality framework, and 39 implementation-science constructs (Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research) operationalized in our continuous-quality-improvement layer.

Specific service deliverables per campus engagement include (but are not limited to):

- **Enrichment clubs and afterschool blocks** (before-school, afterschool 3:00–6:30 PM, weekend, intersession, summer): AI Literacy Lab, Intro to Software Engineering, Computer Science Club, STEM Maker Workshop, Trade-Skills Exploration (electrical/plumbing/HVAC/welding/automotive concept exposure — NOT hands-on tool work on AISD property).
- **Family Engagement workshops**: Multilingual Family Tech Night, Adult Digital Literacy series, FAFSA-for-Families session, Parent-Child AI Co-Learning, ESL-supported parenting-skills circles.
- **College & Career Readiness programming**: FAFSA / financial-aid navigation, post-secondary trade-and-apprenticeship exposure, career-clubs aligned to Texas growth occupations, mock-interview clinics, resume workshops.

All programming is supplied with curriculum, unit plans, lesson plans, instructor staffing, materials, and supplies as required by RFP Section V.B.7.

## 3. Activity Types — selected with content-area proficiency narrative

### ✅ Enrichment

**How activities increase proficiency in related content areas:**
- *AI Literacy Lab* (grades 5–12) builds critical-thinking, prompt-construction, source evaluation, and digital-citizenship competencies aligned with TEKS Technology Applications §126.7 (grades 6–8) and §126.32–§126.39 (HS Computer Science / Fundamentals of Computer Science).
- *Intro to Software Engineering* (grades 6–12) introduces algorithmic thinking, debugging, version control concepts, and the systems-thinking required to "make vibecoding work" — a phrase we use directly with high-school learners. Aligns with TEKS Computer Science I (§126.33) and Fundamentals of Computer Science (§126.32).
- *STEM Maker Workshops* (grades 3–8) build hands-on engineering-design-process competency (ask, imagine, plan, create, improve), reinforcing TEKS Science process strands and Math problem-solving expectations.
- *Trade-Skills Exposure* (grades 8–12) introduces career-cluster awareness across electrical, plumbing, HVAC, welding, automotive, and software-engineering pathways — aligned with TEKS Career Development §127 and Texas Industry-Based Certifications framework.

### ✅ Family Engagement

**How activities increase proficiency / family involvement:**
- *Multilingual Family Tech Night* uses our 89+18 language surface to remove the language barrier between home and campus. Direct translation of family-engagement materials into a family's preferred language (or dialect-aware variant) — including ASL and 17 other signed-language renderings — drives the National PTA Standard #1 (regular, two-way, meaningful home-school communication).
- *Adult Digital Literacy* (parents and guardians) builds device fluency, internet-safety awareness, school-portal navigation, and AI-tool literacy — enabling parents to monitor and participate in student academic life regardless of prior tech exposure.
- *Parent-Child AI Co-Learning* sessions place a parent and student side-by-side on the same AI-literacy task, satisfying National PTA Standard #3 (active parent participation in student learning).
- *ESL-Supported Parenting Skills Circles* address parenting practices alongside language access, aligned with PTA Standard #2 (parenting skills support).

### ✅ College & Career Readiness

**How activities increase proficiency:**
- *FAFSA / Financial-Aid Navigation* — one-on-one and group sessions for seniors and juniors, with built-in eligibility logic for special-circumstance populations (foster-youth FAFSA independent status, McKinney-Vento, ETV, dependency overrides). Aligned with Texas House Bill 3 and the AISD post-secondary planning emphasis.
- *Apprenticeship Pathway Exposure* — connects high-school learners with the 6-trade Trade Sims credential ladder and registered-apprenticeship-intermediary structures (DOL Apprenticeship Building America framework). Aligned with TEKS Career Development §127 and Texas Pathways in Technology Early College High Schools (T-STEM / P-TECH) frameworks.
- *Career Clubs* covering business environment, career counseling, mock interviews, resume preparation, post-secondary application support, dual-credit awareness.

## 4. How are your organization's activities aligned with the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS)?

Each course in our enrichment, family, and college-and-career portfolios maintains a TEKS-alignment crosswalk maintained by our curriculum lead. Direct alignments include:

- **Technology Applications**: TEKS §126.6–126.10 (grades 3–8), §126.32–126.39 (HS Computer Science strand) — direct alignment for AI Literacy Lab, Intro to Software Engineering, Computer Science Club.
- **Career Development**: TEKS §127.1–127.7 — direct alignment for Trade-Skills Exposure, Career Clubs, Apprenticeship Pathway Exposure.
- **Science process strands**: TEKS §112.x (grade-banded) — alignment for STEM Maker Workshops engineering-design-process activities.
- **English Language Arts and Reading — Speaking and Listening / Inquiry**: alignment for AI Literacy critical-thinking and source-evaluation activities.
- **Mathematics process standards**: §111.x problem-solving and modeling — alignment for Software Engineering and STEM blocks.
- **AISD Curriculum Roadmap**: we will reconcile each unit to the corresponding Roadmap grade-band and content-area pacing during the campus-level Service Agreement scoping conversation.

Sample TEKS-alignment crosswalk for one unit is included in `02-Sample-Unit-Plan-and-Lessons`.

## 5. Capacity to serve Limited English Proficiency (LEP / ELL) Students

This is a primary differentiator of our submission. AISD reports **28.1% of its student population is classified as ELL**.

Our platform provides:
- **89 spoken-language surfaces** with dialect-aware system prompts (including but not limited to: Spanish — multiple regional variants, Vietnamese, Mandarin, Arabic, Korean, French, Tagalog, Hindi, Burmese, AAVE, Spanglish).
- **18 signed-language surfaces** including American Sign Language (ASL), making this one of the few enrichment offerings in AISD that can serve Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing students and family members directly.
- **Right-to-left rendering for Arabic** and other RTL scripts.
- **Six dedicated language-learning surfaces** for active acquisition support.
- **Real-time translation for parent-teacher / parent-program communications**, including written and spoken modalities.

All instructional staff receive a "language-access first" orientation: before any English-only content is delivered, the staff member confirms language preference with the learner or family member.

## 9. How will your organization evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed activities?

We use a **mixed-methods evaluation framework** combining attendance/dosage tracking, validated screening instruments, qualitative learner feedback, and YPQ-based fidelity scoring:

**Quantitative (per session and per program-cycle):**
- Attendance — daily, with auditable rosters tied to student IDs as authorized.
- Participation depth — task-completion rates, AI-tutor hint usage, formative-practice-bank scores.
- Pre- / post-knowledge checks at the unit level (not used for AISD grading; for our program improvement only).
- Self-reported confidence and interest (validated short-form scales).

**Qualitative:**
- Student exit-ticket reflections (closed and open prompts).
- Family-engagement feedback collected in family's preferred language.
- Instructor field notes against unit fidelity criteria.

**Program-quality (fidelity to our own model):**
- YPQ instrument cycle as offered by AISD.
- CFIR 39-construct quarterly self-assessment for our continuous-quality-improvement loop.

**Critical Success Factors reported** (per RFP Section V.B.4, Attachment B):
- Academic achievement signals (where data-sharing agreements permit).
- School-day attendance correlation reporting.
- Student-behavior indicators as flagged by campus partners.
- Graduation-rate and promotion-rate contribution narratives for cohorts followed multi-year.

All evaluation outputs are summarized in a per-campus quarterly report shared with the requesting campus point-of-contact, plus an annual program-level summary for AISD Student Support Services.

## 11. Process for screening employees and volunteers

- **Fingerprinting via TX DPS FACT (Fingerprint-based Applicant Clearinghouse of Texas)** for every individual who will have direct or indirect contact with AISD students, prior to any campus access.
- **National criminal-history record** obtained through the same clearinghouse and filed with AISD per District Policy CJA REGULATION/LEGAL.
- **Sex offender registry check** at hire and recurrence-checked annually.
- **Reference checks** (minimum 2 professional) for paid staff.
- **Mandated reporter status** confirmed in writing for every individual at hire and annually.
- **SAM endorsement and child-safety policy training** completed before campus access; awareness of grooming behaviors, two-deep leadership rules, and incident-reporting flow.
- **Annual recertification** of all clearances.
- **Volunteers**: same fingerprinting + criminal-history clearance + mandated-reporter training as paid staff. No exceptions for "one-time" volunteers on campus during programming.

The Contractor is directly responsible for obtaining and maintaining all background-check documentation per AISD District Policy CJA and Texas Education Code §22.0833.
