Across Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop, and Caldwell counties, an estimated 192,029 people qualify for public benefits they are not receiving — a 60% enrollment gap. Over $192 million in annual SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, EITC, and WIC benefits go unclaimed because families face language barriers, transportation gaps, no broadband, and deep system distrust.

These are not strangers to need. They are families in Pflugerville who cross county lines for work but fall through enrollment cracks. They are parents in Manor whose citizen children qualify for CHIP but who avoid government offices out of fear. They are grandparents in Bastrop raising grandchildren on fixed incomes who do not know they qualify for SNAP.

**How We Will Do This Work**

Our initiative operates through three lines of effort across all five counties:

**Line 1: Embedded Community Enrollment.** We place bilingual Community Health Workers at trusted community sites — the Pflugerville Public Library, Manor community centers, ACC campuses, Frost Bank branches, and faith-based partner locations — to screen families for nine benefit programs and assist with applications on-site. We do not refer and hope. We screen, apply, and track every family through enrollment and annual renewal.

**Line 2: Digital-First Access for Hard-to-Reach Populations.** Our platform at thrivingcommunitiesforall.com provides a benefits screener, real-time eligibility checking, and direct links to YourTexasBenefits.com — accessible from any phone, without login. For families without transportation or broadband, our CHWs bring tablets to neighborhoods, completing applications at kitchen tables, laundromats, and school pickup lines. This is how we reach the 198 highest-barrier census tracts where poverty, limited English, and isolation compound.

**Line 3: Trust-Building with Mixed-Status Families.** For families where citizen children qualify but parents fear system contact, our CHWs are trusted community members — not government representatives. We screen children's eligibility without requiring parent immigration status. This requires patience, cultural fluency, and years of relationship. It cannot be done by phone banks or mailers.

**Community-Specific Operations:**
- *Williamson County (Pflugerville):* Library-based enrollment, Frost Bank EITC/CTC workshops, school-district partnerships
- *Travis County (Manor/East Austin):* Community center pop-ups, ACC campus enrollment via Professor Laura Franco, faith-based partner sites
- *Hays/Bastrop/Caldwell:* Mobile CHW teams deploying to rural census tracts, partnering with local food pantries and WIC offices as enrollment access points

**Sustainability and Expertise.** TCAF has initiated HHSC Community Partner Program certification, integrating our enrollment infrastructure into the state's official benefits system. Once certified, our CHW network operates as permanent state infrastructure — not dependent on any single grant. Our data methodology, Census tract analysis, and evidence chain are publicly verifiable at thrivingcommunitiesforall.com/sdoh-explorer. We measure every enrollment, every renewal, and every dollar of benefits claimed — in real time.

We are asking for the resources to be the coordinated, cross-county effort that meets people where they are and walks them through the door.
