Across Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop, and Caldwell counties, an estimated 192,029 people qualify for public benefits they are not receiving. That is a 60% enrollment gap — families eligible for SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, EITC, and WIC who remain uncovered due to language barriers, lack of transportation, no broadband access, and system distrust. Over $192 million in annual benefits go unclaimed in this five-county region.

These are not strangers to need. They are families in Pflugerville who cross from Williamson into Travis County for work but fall through enrollment cracks at the county line. They are parents in Manor whose citizen children qualify for CHIP but who avoid government offices out of fear. They are grandparents in Bastrop raising grandchildren on fixed incomes who do not know they qualify for SNAP.

County boundaries are administrative lines. Poverty is not.

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation proposes to deploy a community-based benefits enrollment initiative across all five counties, placing trained Community Health Workers in the 198 highest-barrier census tracts — neighborhoods where poverty, limited English proficiency, lack of broadband, and lack of transportation compound to create enrollment deserts.

Our approach is direct: screen families for nine federal and state benefit programs, assist with applications on-site, and follow each family through enrollment and annual renewal. We do not refer and hope. We enroll and track. Our initiative follows an implementation science framework with real-time measurement. Our data methodology, Census tract analysis, and evidence chain are publicly available and verifiable at thriveupacademy.replit.app/sdoh-explorer.

We are building this initiative with partners already embedded in these communities. The Cities of Pflugerville and Manor provide municipal touchpoints for enrollment in Williamson and Travis counties. Frost Bank brings community financial presence and connection to EITC and Child Tax Credit enrollment. Austin Community College, through Professor Laura Franco, provides campus-based enrollment access reaching students and families — many near-poverty and benefits-eligible — across Travis, Williamson, and Hays counties.

For mixed-status families — where citizen children qualify but parents fear system contact — our model uses bilingual Community Health Workers who are trusted community members, not government representatives. We do not require immigration status to screen for children's eligibility.

Sustainability is built into the model. TCAF is initiating HHSC Community Partner Program certification, which integrates our enrollment work into the state's official benefits infrastructure. Once certified, our CHW network operates as part of the Texas enrollment system — not dependent on any single grant. Every family enrolled generates measurable savings in emergency care, crisis intervention, and safety-net costs that justify continued public investment.

The enrollment gap is not a mystery. The data is public. The families are real. What has been missing is a coordinated, cross-county effort to meet people where they are and walk them through the door.

We are asking for the resources to be that effort.
