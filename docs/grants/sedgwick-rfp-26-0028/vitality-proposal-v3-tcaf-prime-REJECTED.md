# Section 2 — Executive Summary

## 2.1 The three-sentence thesis

Sedgwick County is already spending approximately $5.15M per year on unmanaged GLP-1 medications for 373 employees and dependents, with a ~47% twelve-month non-continuation rate — consistent with published unmanaged-GLP-1 patterns of 30–50% — that demonstrates prior authorization alone is not working. We build with, not against, UnitedHealthcare, OptumRx, and Rally; we displace no existing vendor; and we are the only respondent purpose-built for the four-partner program design model the County asked for.

## 2.2 What Vitality is

Vitality is a comprehensive, virtual-only weight management program designed for self-insured public-sector employers. The program combines:

- **Three evidence-based clinical pathways** (Lifestyle, GLP-1-Managed, and Surgical-Coordinated), each anchored in published evidence (NIH/DPP, STEP Trials, LOOK AHEAD, RAND).
- **A 5-phase member journey** (onboarding, baseline, intensive intervention, maintenance, graduation) overseen by Dr. Chela Love, DNP and her clinical team at Love Clinic MedSpa.
- **A behavioral coaching engine** led by Dr. J. Michelle Vann, with weekly check-ins, lifestyle accountability, and family/youth program extensions.
- **A digital Progressive Web App platform** (no app-store install required) built and operated by Dr. Terry D. Flood's team at The COLLABORATIVE Advocate Foundation, with FHIR/HL7 integration, persistent-memory AI coaching, and HIPAA-grade security.
- **Prime contractor administration** by Hargrave Innovative Solutions, providing government contract management, compliance oversight, reporting coordination, and single-point accountability.

## 2.3 Why Vitality wins on the County's three named KPIs

The County named three KPIs in Addendum #2, in this order:

1. **Member engagement** — Vitality's VITA AI persistent-memory advisor remembers every prior conversation with each member and resumes context across sessions. Combined with Dr. Vann's weekly behavioral cadence, the program is designed to sustain engagement over 12–24 months, not just at signup.
2. **Proper use of GLP-1s** — Dr. Love's structured titration protocol, OptumRx fill-history reconciliation, and side-effect management directly address the non-continuation pattern visible in the County's 705 → 373 cohort data.

## 4.2 The GLP-1 utilization picture

The single most operationally significant disclosure in Addendum #2 is the GLP-1 utilization data. We interpret it as follows:

**(a) Current GLP-1 spend is substantial and unmanaged.** At the conservative RAND 2023 figure of $13,800 per unmanaged GLP-1 patient per year, the 373 current GLP-1 members represent approximately $5.15 million in annual pharmacy cost to the County's medical plan. This spend is occurring with no clinical oversight beyond standard PBM prior authorization.

**(b) The ~47% twelve-month non-continuation rate is the diagnostic signal.** 705 members used a GLP-1 in the past twelve months, but only 373 are on therapy today — a 332-member differential, or approximately 47% non-continuation. We use "non-continuation" rather than pure "discontinuation" because the County figure may include planned course completions, approved-but-never-filled prior authorizations, within-class therapy switches, and members recently started who are still in titration. Even with those qualifications, the figure sits squarely within the published unmanaged-GLP-1 range of 30–50% twelve-month non-continuation (Gleason et al., JAMA Network Open 2024; AHIP real-world adherence analysis 2023), and the underlying drivers are well-established: side effects (nausea, fatigue) without titration support, absence of behavioral coaching, and no clinical follow-up.

**(c) The capture opportunity is concrete and addressable.** RAND's 2023 conservative figure for the cost differential between unmanaged and managed GLP-1 therapy is $5,600 per patient per year ($13,800 − $8,200). Applied to the 373 current GLP-1 members, the modeled Year-1 capture opportunity is approximately **$2.09 million** (373 × $5,600). Migrating the cohort to a managed protocol does not require a single new prescription or any change to OptumRx's formulary — it requires only that the existing 373 members receive structured clinical oversight, titration support, side-effect management, and behavioral coaching. We label this a "capture opportunity" rather than a "guaranteed saving" because realization depends on member uptake; we convert it into a contractual commitment through the at-risk pricing structure described in Section 8.5.

## 4.3 Current gap analysis

The Addendum disclosed: *"Current cost containment is tied to prior authorization program with PBM."* The County has identified this as insufficient and has stated its preference for *"a broader support-focused program centered around outcomes, health coaching participation, and sustainable lifestyle changes."*

We share the County's diagnosis. Prior authorization gates approval at the moment of prescription but does not address:

Vitality is designed to fill exactly this gap, while preserving OptumRx's PBM role and UnitedHealthcare's TPA role.

# Section 5 — Program Design: Vitality Comprehensive Weight Management

## 5.1 Program philosophy

Vitality is built on five principles:

1. **Member-centric, not vendor-centric.** Every design decision starts from the member's job-to-be-done.
2. **Evidence-based.** Every clinical recommendation cites the underlying study (DPP, STEP, LOOK AHEAD, RAND, NIH).
3. **Integration-first.** We coexist with the County's existing UHC, OptumRx, and Rally relationships. We do not replace them.
4. **Support-focused.** We meet the County's stated preference: coaching and clinical wraparound, not compliance enforcement.
5. **Outcomes over activity.** Every dashboard leads with the three KPIs the County named.

## 5.2 Member journey — the 5-phase clinical protocol

Every Vitality member moves through five phases. Each phase has defined entry and exit criteria, a clinical owner, and a measurable outcome.

| Phase | Duration | Owner | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Onboarding | 1–2 weeks | Vitality coaching | Identity verified, employer eligibility confirmed, consent collected, baseline survey complete |
| 2. Clinical baseline | 2–4 weeks | Dr. Love (Clinical) | Medical baseline, BMI, A1C, blood pressure, comorbidity inventory, pathway assignment |
| 3. Intensive intervention | 12–26 weeks | Dr. Love + Dr. Vann | Pathway-specific intervention (lifestyle, GLP-1, surgical-coordinated); weekly coaching cadence; biweekly clinical check |
| 4. Maintenance | 26–52 weeks | Dr. Vann (Behavioral) | Reduced cadence (biweekly to monthly); habit consolidation; outcome stabilization |
| 5. Graduation | Ongoing | Self-directed with quarterly check-in | Member graduates to alumni program with quarterly touchpoints; clinical and engagement data continue to feed reporting for the savings carryover model |

## 5.3 Three clinical pathways

Members are assigned to one of three pathways during Phase 2 (Clinical baseline), based on Dr. Love's clinical assessment, member preference, and clinical guideline match.

**Pathway A — Lifestyle Intervention** (estimated 72% of enrolled members)
- Evidence base: NIH/DPP Trial (58% reduction in type 2 diabetes progression at 5–10% weight loss)
- Components: structured nutrition coaching, activity tracking, behavioral group sessions led by Dr. Vann, weekly check-ins
- Clinical cadence: quarterly Dr. Love review
- Cost component: lifestyle baseline ($3,200/member/year all-in)

**Pathway B — GLP-1 Managed Care** (estimated 20% of enrolled members)
- Evidence base: STEP-1, STEP-2, STEP-3 trials (15–17% body weight reduction at 68 weeks)
- Components: GLP-1 prescription, structured titration, side-effect management, monthly clinical visits, weekly coaching, OptumRx fill-history reconciliation
- Clinical cadence: monthly Dr. Love visit during titration; quarterly during maintenance
- Member can elect: in-program prescribing (Dr. Love) OR coaching-only support with member's existing prescriber (per Addendum #2 confirmation that either model is acceptable)

**Pathway C — Surgical-Coordinated** (estimated 8% of enrolled members)
- Evidence base: LOOK AHEAD Trial (sustained 7–8% loss at 4 years with intensive lifestyle); bariatric surgery outcomes literature
- Components: pre-surgical clinical evaluation, post-surgical lifestyle support, coordination with member's chosen surgical center, long-term behavioral coaching
- Clinical cadence: aligned with surgical center protocol; Vitality provides wrap-around support

## 5.4 Behavioral engagement model

Dr. Vann's behavioral engagement model is the engine that sustains member engagement — the County's #1 KPI.

- **Weekly check-in cadence** during Phase 3 (intensive intervention), via the Vitality PWA, with structured prompts and a coaching response within 24 hours.
- **Biweekly group sessions** during Phase 3 and Phase 4, virtual cohort format, 8–12 members per cohort, facilitated by Dr. Vann or a licensed coach under her supervision.
- **Family and youth programming extensions** for members with dependents — addressing the household environment that drives sustainable weight management.
- **Automated escalation** for any member who does not check in for 14 days, routed to a human coach for outreach.
- **Engagement scoring** for the dashboard, surfaced to the County in quarterly reports.

## 5.5 VITA AI persistent-memory advisor

VITA is Vitality's AI coaching assistant, embedded in the member portal and the employer dashboard. VITA is differentiated from generic AI chatbots by three properties:

1. **Persistent memory.** Every member conversation is stored. VITA resumes context on every new session — there is no "starting over." A returning member sees a "Resuming — X prior exchanges" badge on their portal.
2. **Live clinical context.** VITA receives the member's current weight, BMI, GLP-1 status, active care plan phase, recent weight trend, and achieved milestones with every message. Responses are anchored to the member's real situation, not generic content.
3. **Evidence-cited responses.** VITA's clinical answers cite specific studies (DPP, STEP, LOOK AHEAD, RAND). This is a deliberate design choice for an RFP-evaluator audience as well as for clinical defensibility.

VITA operates in two modes: a warm, Dr. Love–voiced member mode, and an analytical, ROI-focused employer mode for the County dashboard. Both modes share the same persistent-memory architecture.

## 5.6 The GLP-1 managed-care protocol — the headline differentiator

This is the operational answer to the $2.09M Year-1 modeled capture opportunity. The protocol has six components:

1. **Initial clinical evaluation by Dr. Love** for every member entering Pathway B. Establishes baseline metrics, comorbidities, contraindications, and titration plan.
2. **Structured titration over 12–16 weeks** with proactive side-effect management. Members are contacted on day 3, day 7, day 14, and weekly thereafter during titration to surface and address side effects before they cause discontinuation. This is the single most important intervention against the GLP-1 non-continuation pattern visible in the County's 705-to-373 cohort transition.
3. **OptumRx fill-history reconciliation.** Vitality receives weekly fill-history feeds from OptumRx and reconciles them against expected fills. Members who miss a fill receive coaching outreach within 48 hours.
4. **Behavioral coaching from Dr. Vann's team** throughout titration and maintenance. Members on GLP-1 receive structured nutrition support, activity coaching, and sleep/stress modules — the lifestyle scaffolding that makes the medication work.
5. **Coordination with member's existing prescriber** (if the member chooses to remain with their PCP rather than have Dr. Love prescribe). Vitality acts as the supporting coaching and reporting layer; the member's PCP retains prescribing authority. Per Addendum #2, the County has confirmed this model is acceptable.
6. **Monthly clinical visits during titration; quarterly thereafter.** Dr. Love (or a credentialed clinician on her team) maintains direct medical oversight throughout the member's GLP-1 course.

The protocol directly targets the non-continuation pattern visible in the County's 705-to-373 cohort transition. Vitality commits to a measurable performance threshold tied to this protocol: **≥30% of the County's current 373-member GLP-1 cohort retained in active managed-protocol participation at month 12** (a conservative target relative to published managed-GLP-1 retention rates of 55–70%). At-risk pricing tied to this threshold is described in Section 8.5, converting the modeled capture opportunity into a contractual commitment.

# Section 6 — Network Access: Integration with the County Medical Plan Ecosystem

*In response to Criterion II's requirement that the program demonstrate the ability to integrate and coordinate with the County's existing medical plan ecosystem (UnitedHealthcare TPA, OptumRx PBM, Rally member-engagement platform, and County HRIS / identity systems), as clarified in Addendum #2.*

## 6.1 PWA architecture

The Vitality member experience is delivered as a Progressive Web Application (PWA). This means:

- **No app-store install required.** Members access the program from any modern web browser on any device — phone, tablet, laptop, desktop. The PWA supports add-to-home-screen on iOS and Android for native-app-style access without the friction of an app-store flow.
- **Offline capability.** Members can log weight, complete daily check-ins, and review coaching materials offline; data syncs when connectivity returns.
- **Push notifications** on Android and modern iOS, for coaching prompts, medication reminders, and weekly check-in nudges.
- **Accessibility:** Design conformance to WCAG 2.1 AA and Section 508; screen-reader support, keyboard navigation, and high-contrast mode in the current member portal. A third-party WCAG 2.1 AA conformance audit will be commissioned during Weeks 4–8 of the 90-day implementation and the audit report delivered to County IT prior to County-wide go-live.

The platform stack is React 19 + TypeScript on the front end, Express 5 on the API server, PostgreSQL with Drizzle ORM for the database, and a Claude AI (Anthropic) integration for the VITA assistant. All software is built and maintained by Dr. Flood's team at The COLLABORATIVE Advocate Foundation.

## 6.2 Integration with UnitedHealthcare (TPA)

UnitedHealthcare is Sedgwick County's TPA on the self-insured plan (Group #902769). Vitality is designed to integrate with UHC across three surfaces during the 90-day implementation; none of these integrations are live in the demonstration platform today. The three planned surfaces are:

- **Eligibility feed:** SFTP or API-based daily roster sync from UHC's eligibility files. Will establish the eligible adult population (4,213 per Addendum #2) and support automated enrollment validation.
- **Claims data feed:** Monthly de-identified claims extract under BAA, to be used for outcome measurement (claims-cost trend, hospital and ER utilization for the at-risk cohort).
- **FHIR R4 read access** (if available from UHC's API gateway): will support real-time member clinical context for VITA and Dr. Love's clinical team.

Integration timeline: stand-up within 30–45 days of contract execution, dependent on UHC IT scheduling. See Section 7.4 for the full demo-vs-implementation maturity matrix.

## 6.3 Integration with OptumRx (PBM)

OptumRx is Sedgwick County's PBM. Vitality is designed to integrate across two surfaces during the 90-day implementation; neither integration is live in the demonstration platform today. The two planned surfaces are:

- **GLP-1 authorization status feed:** read-only access to OptumRx prior-authorization decisions for members enrolled in Vitality. Will allow Dr. Love's clinical team to know which members have current GLP-1 authorization without manually contacting members.
- **Fill-history feed:** weekly extract of GLP-1 fill events for enrolled members. Will drive the fill-reconciliation process described in Section 5.6.

We will not request prescribing authority within OptumRx, will not modify the formulary, and will not change any prior-authorization rules. Vitality's role is observational and coordinative.

# Section 8 — Pricing Model and Return on Investment

## 8.1 PEPM (per-employee-per-month) pricing structure

Vitality offers a blended PEPM pricing model that scales with the County's enrolled member count and the actual mix of clinical pathways used. The blended PEPM range is **$476–$525 per enrolled member per month**, depending on the GLP-1 mix.

The pricing is constructed bottoms-up from operational cost components:

| Component | $/member/month |
|---|---|
| Coaching (Dr. Vann's team + VITA AI) | $55 |
| Platform & analytics (CAF technology infrastructure) | $45 |
| Administration, compliance, and BAA management (HIS) | $30 |
| Clinical oversight (Dr. Love, blended across pathways) | $45 lifestyle / $130 GLP-1 / $75 surgical |
| GLP-1 medication (at negotiated specialty rate, when applicable) | $930 for the 20% on the GLP-1 pathway |
| Operator margin (20%) | Variable |

At a representative 20% GLP-1 mix, the blended all-in PEPM is approximately **$476**. At a 30% GLP-1 mix, it rises to approximately **$525**.

## 8.2 Lifestyle pathway baseline cost

For members on Pathway A (Lifestyle), the all-in annual cost is **$3,200 per member per year** ($267/month). This includes coaching, platform, administration, and clinical oversight, but excludes medication (none required on this pathway).

## 8.3 Year-1 program cost at target enrollment

At our target enrollment of **534 members** (35% of the 1,525 modeled at-risk adult population), the Year-1 program cost at the lifestyle baseline is **$1,708,800**.

This figure is conservative — it assumes all enrollees are on the lifestyle pathway. At the realistic ~20% GLP-1 mix, the actual Year-1 cost is approximately **$2.55M–$2.80M** (the higher figure reflects the GLP-1 medication cost that is otherwise being absorbed by the pharmacy plan today).

## 8.4 Return on Investment — the headline economic case

### 8.4.1 The GLP-1 capture

The single largest ROI driver is the migration of the County's existing 373 GLP-1 members from unmanaged to managed therapy.

- Current spend: 373 × $13,800 = **$5,147,400 per year** (RAND 2023 unmanaged figure)
- Managed spend: 373 × $8,200 = **$3,058,600 per year** (RAND 2023 managed figure)
- **Modeled Year-1 capture opportunity: $2,088,800**

This is not a hypothetical population; it is the County's actual current cohort, disclosed in Addendum #2.

### 8.4.2 Lifestyle and clinical savings

Modeling the broader at-risk population at 35% enrollment (534 members), 68% clinical threshold achievement (NIH/DPP), and per-success savings of $2,639 ($1,861 medical from CDC/CMS 2023 + $778 productivity from RAND, 2.73 days × $285/day):

- Successful members: 534 × 68% = 363
- Annual medical savings: 363 × $1,861 = **$675,543**
- Annual productivity savings: 363 × $778 = **$282,414**
- **Subtotal: $957,957 per year**

### 8.4.3 Total Year-1 economic opportunity

- GLP-1 capture: $2,088,800
- Lifestyle + clinical: $957,957
- **Total: $3,046,757 per year**

Against a Year-1 program cost of $1,708,800 (lifestyle baseline) or up to ~$2.80M (with realistic GLP-1 mix), the County's net Year-1 benefit is **$240K–$1.34M** in Year 1, and grows through Years 2–5 with graduate-savings carryover.

### 8.4.4 Multi-year ROI projection

Using a conservative graduate-savings carryover model (85% YoY retention of savings from members who hit clinical threshold, capped at a 3-year carry tail per DPP follow-up data):

| Year | Cumulative ROI ratio |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | 0.56x |
| Year 3 | 1.01x (break-even) |
| Year 5 | 1.32x |

The headline industry benchmark (Harvard meta-analysis 2010: $3.27 return per $1 invested in workplace wellness) is the upper bound — we deliberately under-promise relative to that benchmark.

## 8.5 Base price and optional at-risk performance overlay (separated for Criterion V scoring)

**The scored base price for Criterion V is the blended PEPM in Section 8.1: $476–$525 per enrolled member per month, depending on GLP-1 mix (lifestyle baseline $267 PEPM).** That figure is the number reflected on the Response Form (Appendix F) and is the figure the County's cost-evaluation formula will compare against other bidders' base pricing.

**The at-risk performance overlay described below is a separate, optional contract structure offered above and beyond the base price. It is not part of the base PEPM and does not change the price the County is asked to score under Criterion V.** The County may elect, decline, or modify this overlay at contract negotiation; the base price stands regardless. We separate it deliberately so a competitor with a lower headline number cannot leverage our at-risk commitment against us on cost scoring.

**Optional at-risk performance overlay (offered above base price, negotiable at contract):**

- **15% of total billed PEPM held at risk** in a quarterly performance withhold under a separate at-risk addendum to the contract. Released on attainment of all three County-named KPIs:
  1. **Engagement threshold:** ≥75% of enrolled members maintain weekly check-in activity at the end of each quarter
  2. **GLP-1 utilization threshold:** **≥30% of the County's current 373-member GLP-1 cohort retained in active Vitality managed protocol at month 12** (conservative against the 55–70% published managed-GLP-1 retention range)
  3. **Cost savings threshold:** Year-1 medical-cost trend for the enrolled cohort outperforms a matched no-program baseline (carrier-validated by UHC at Year 1 reconciliation)

- **Performance bonus (above base PEPM) for outperformance:** if Vitality retains ≥50% of the 373-cohort in managed protocol at month 12 (representing ~$2.50M+ in captured spend differential), the County pays an outperformance bonus of 5% of base PEPM.

- **Failure-to-perform clause:** if Vitality misses any one threshold by more than 25% of target in two consecutive quarters, the County may terminate without cause and the 15% withhold is permanently forfeited.

This overlay converts the $2.09M modeled capture opportunity into a contractual commitment, aligns vendor incentives with the County's three named KPIs, and protects the County's downside — without altering the base price scored under Criterion V. The specific percentages, thresholds, and reconciliation methodology are negotiable at contract.

## Appendix A — Strategic Analysis of RFP #26-0028 Addendum #2

This appendix documents the strategic conclusions the Vitality proposal team drew from Addendum #2, issued May 22, 2026 by Tammy Culley, Purchasing Agent. It is preserved here as a discrete artifact for the evaluation committee's reference.

### A.1 The GLP-1 number is the entire story

Addendum #2 disclosed three numbers that, together, reframe the entire proposal:

- 373 members are currently on GLP-1 medications through the County's medical plan
- 705 members used a GLP-1 in the past 12 months
- The County's only current GLP-1 cost-containment tool is OptumRx prior authorization

Three observations follow:

**(a) The County is already spending heavily on GLP-1 medications.** At the conservative unmanaged rate of $13,800/patient/year (RAND 2023), 373 current members represent approximately $5.15M per year in GLP-1 pharmacy spend with zero clinical wraparound.

**(b) The ~47% twelve-month non-continuation rate is the diagnostic signal.** 705 members used a GLP-1 in the past twelve months but only 373 are on therapy today — a ~47% non-continuation rate that sits within the published unmanaged-GLP-1 range of 30–50% (Gleason et al., JAMA Network Open 2024; AHIP real-world adherence analysis 2023). We label this conservatively as "non-continuation" rather than pure discontinuation because the County figure may include planned course completions, approved-but-never-filled PAs, within-class switches, and recent starters still in titration.

**(c) The modeled Year-1 capture opportunity is approximately $2.09M.** At RAND's conservative $5,600-per-patient differential between unmanaged and managed GLP-1 therapy, migrating the 373 current users represents approximately $2.09M in addressable annual spend — nearly funding the entire $1.71M Year-1 program cost. Vitality converts this model into a commitment via the at-risk pricing structure in Section 8.5.

### A.2 The County has named its own incumbent strategy as insufficient

The Addendum states verbatim: "Current cost containment is tied to prior authorization program with PBM." The ~47% twelve-month non-continuation rate demonstrates that prior authorization alone is not converting pharmacy spend into sustained outcomes. The Addendum further clarifies the County's preference: "A broader support-focused program centered around outcomes, health coaching participation, and sustainable lifestyle changes." The County explicitly rejected a compliance-focused program.

### A.3 The County is buying program design, not administration

The Addendum confirms: "Recommend and design a comprehensive solution." The Addendum also confirms that either clinical model is acceptable: prescribing physicians on the vendor's staff OR NBHWC-credentialed coaches supporting members' own prescribers. Vitality offers both within a single integrated program — Dr. Love as the in-program prescribing lead, Dr. Vann's coaching model for members who stay with their existing PCP.
