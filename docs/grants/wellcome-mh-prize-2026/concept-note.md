## The intervention

We propose adopting **Collaborative Care** — the most-replicated depression-care model in psychiatry (Unützer et al. 2012 IMPACT; AIMS Center extensions 2022–2025; >90 RCTs across populations [TO BIND: see `evidence-pulls.md` §Gate 1]) — and adapting it for **perinatal depression and anxiety delivered in community-based organizations** rather than primary-care clinics.

The content payload (caseload registry, measurement-based stepped care, psychiatric consultation, behavioral activation / problem-solving therapy) is unchanged. What changes is:

1. **Delivery context** — community organizations (faith communities, Head Start centers, CHW-anchored neighborhood hubs) rather than primary-care clinics.
2. **Content extension** — perinatal-specific content from Tandon group's *Mothers and Babies* lineage (in-window RCTs of group, individual, mobile, and CHW-delivered variants).
3. **Measurement architecture** — multi-vantage Adaptable Treatment Fidelity, prospective Mechanism-Preservation Registry, and Sustainment Capability scored at month 12 (in-grant) AND month 24 (post-grant) as a **funded deliverable**.

This is exactly the "**new or novel intervention at an early stage of implementation**" Wellcome's call describes: **established content, novel delivery context, novel measurement architecture, novel sustainment as a scored gate** — not an incremental advance.

## Why this clears Gate E.5 (RCT-grade evidence, last 5 years) — honest framing

We are **not claiming a new RCT**. We cite the intersection of **two converging in-window RCT lineages whose intersection is currently under-deployed**:

- **Collaborative Care core:** Unützer 2012 IMPACT + continuous AIMS Center replication portfolio through 2025; multiple in-window pragmatic trials and meta-analyses. [TO BIND: 3 in-window primary citations from RPLICE pull]
- **Perinatal extension content:** Tandon group *Mothers and Babies* — in-window RCTs of group, individual, mobile, and CHW-delivered variants; public-domain curriculum. [TO BIND: 3 in-window primary citations from RPLICE pull]
- **Endpoints already validated:** PHQ-9 (Kroenke 2001), GAD-7 (Spitzer 2006), EPDS (Cox 1987), WHO-5 (Topp 2015) — all on Wellcome's clinical-relevance criterion (symptoms + functioning + QoL).

## What is novel — the TCAF implementation stack

The novelty Wellcome is scoring lives in the **implementation architecture**, not in the content payload. These components exist as **scaffolded pages on the RPLICE platform today; the prize funds their instrument validation, field deployment, and evidence generation** — not their initial build.

1. **8-Dimensional Adaptable Treatment Fidelity** (RPLICE — `client/src/pages/measurement-studio.tsx`, 256 lines, scaffolded). Multi-vantage fidelity scoring — observer + recipient + community + system + mechanism-preservation under adaptation. Addresses the field's monocular-fidelity problem.
2. **Mechanism-Preservation Registry** (RPLICE — `client/src/pages/mechanism-preservation-registry.tsx`, 337 lines, scaffolded). Adaptations logged against a prospectively declared mechanism map. Tells reviewers exactly which Collaborative Care core elements travel into the community-organization perinatal context and which do not.
3. **Sustainment Capability Tracker** (RPLICE — `client/src/pages/sustainment-capability-tracker.tsx`, 258 lines, scaffolded). The measurement capability becomes a **funded deliverable at T0** — the host community organization owns the instruments by month 12 regardless of program continuation. This operationalizes the field's chronic blind spot named in Birken 2020 as a **scored gate**, for what we believe is the first time.
4. **Lived-experience by design.** Cortney Jones, MSW (Change 1 / Grit Growth Global) is a **co-investigator with design authority** over instrument selection, content adaptation, and fidelity vantages — not an advisor consulted after design locks. Directly addresses Wellcome's lived-experience criterion (Wellcome scoring criterion S.4).
5. **Community-organization siting.** Closes the access gap IMPACT has historically depended on integrated health systems to bridge.
