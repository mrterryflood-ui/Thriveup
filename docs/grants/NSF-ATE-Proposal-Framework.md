## WHAT THIS PROPOSAL IS

A partnership between Austin Community College and ThriveUp (TCAF's 24-platform community infrastructure ecosystem) to train community college students in **AI-assisted implementation science** — teaching technicians how to use AI tools to move evidence-based practices into real-world community settings.

This is NOT a traditional "AI curriculum" proposal. This is:
- Teaching students to USE AI tools for community health and social service implementation
- Training the next generation of implementation science technicians who can bridge the gap between research evidence and community practice
- Using RPLICE (Rapid Participatory Leadership Implementation Composite Evaluation) as both the training tool AND the research methodology

## PROJECT SUMMARY (1 page)

### Overview
This project creates a new ATE program at Austin Community College (ACC) that trains community and technical college students to become **AI-assisted implementation science technicians** — professionals who use artificial intelligence tools to evaluate whether community programs work, why they work, and how to improve them. The program leverages ThriveUp, a 24-platform community infrastructure ecosystem developed by The Collaborative Advocate Foundation (TCAF), as both the training environment and the real-world application platform.

### Intellectual Merit
The project advances knowledge in three ways: (1) It establishes a new technician education pathway in implementation science — a field that currently trains only at the doctoral level — making it accessible to associate's degree students; (2) It generates evidence about how AI-augmented tools (specifically the RPLICE evaluation framework) can accelerate novice practitioners' ability to conduct valid implementation assessments; (3) It produces a replicable curriculum model that other community colleges can adopt to train students in AI-assisted program evaluation.

### Broader Impacts
The project directly serves populations historically excluded from STEM technician careers: veterans transitioning to civilian employment, returning citizens, single parents, and first-generation college students. ACC's student population is 44% Hispanic/Latino, 7% Black/African American, and 66% first-generation. By training students to evaluate programs that serve their own communities — maternal health, reentry support, workforce development, behavioral health — the project creates a self-reinforcing cycle where diverse technicians improve services for diverse populations. The stackable credential model (certificate → Associate's → workforce placement) ensures immediate economic mobility.

### Keywords
Advanced Technological Education, implementation science, artificial intelligence, community college, technician education, health informatics, program evaluation, CFIR, RE-AIM, workforce development

## PROJECT DESCRIPTION (15 pages max)

### Section 1: Introduction and Rationale (2 pages)

#### 1.1 The Evidence-to-Practice Gap

The United States spends over $60 billion annually on community health and social service programs. Yet research consistently shows that only 14% of evidence-based interventions are implemented with fidelity in community settings (Balas & Boren, 2000). The result: programs that worked in controlled trials fail when deployed in real communities — not because the evidence is wrong, but because implementation is wrong.

Implementation science — the study of methods to promote the adoption, adaptation, and sustained delivery of evidence-based practices — has emerged as the solution to this gap. But implementation science currently trains practitioners at the doctoral level. Community-based organizations, local health departments, and social service agencies — the organizations that NEED implementation science skills most — cannot recruit PhD-level implementation scientists.

#### 1.2 The AI Acceleration Opportunity

Artificial intelligence creates an unprecedented opportunity to democratize implementation science. AI-powered tools can:
- Automatically assess organizational readiness using validated frameworks (CFIR 2.0)
- Analyze implementation outcomes across RE-AIM dimensions in real-time
- Identify patterns across multiple program sites that human evaluators would miss
- Generate actionable recommendations based on implementation data

But these tools require trained operators — technicians who understand both the AI technology and the implementation science methodology. This program creates those technicians.

#### 1.3 Why Austin Community College

ACC serves over 74,000 students across 11 campuses in Central Texas. Its student body is:
- 44% Hispanic/Latino
- 7% Black/African American
- 66% first-generation college students
- 38% Pell Grant eligible
- Significant veteran population (proximity to Fort Cavazos, Camp Mabry)

ACC already offers programs in Health Information Technology, Data Analytics, and Public Health — providing natural feeders for an AI implementation science technician track.

#### 1.4 Why ThriveUp / TCAF

ThriveUp is not a theoretical training environment. It is a live, operational community infrastructure platform serving real populations in Central Texas. Students trained on ThriveUp are not practicing on simulated data — they are conducting real implementation assessments on real programs serving real families.

The platform's 24 interconnected services create a rich, multidomain training ground:
- A student evaluating a workforce reentry program can see how participants' housing stability, mental health, and family dynamics affect employment outcomes
- A student assessing maternal health outreach can trace how language barriers, transportation access, and insurance status interact with care engagement
- A student analyzing STEM education effectiveness can connect academic outcomes to family crisis events, food insecurity, and neighborhood safety

No single-purpose platform can provide this training depth.

### Section 3: Program Design and Curriculum (3 pages)

#### 3.1 Certificate Program Structure (12 Credit Hours)

**Course 1: Foundations of Implementation Science (3 credits)**
- Introduction to the evidence-to-practice gap
- CFIR 2.0 framework: 5 domains, 39 constructs
- RE-AIM framework: Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance
- History and evolution of implementation science
- Ethics of community-based evaluation

**Course 2: AI Tools for Program Evaluation (3 credits)**
- Introduction to AI/ML concepts for non-computer scientists
- RPLICE platform orientation and certification
- AI-assisted data collection and analysis
- Pattern recognition in implementation data
- Automated reporting and visualization
- Hands-on: Conduct a complete RPLICE assessment on a live ThriveUp program

**Course 3: Applied Implementation Assessment (3 credits)**
- Field practicum: Students conduct real implementation assessments
- Multi-domain analysis: How health, education, workforce, and justice outcomes interact
- Stakeholder communication: Translating AI findings for community audiences
- Fidelity measurement and continuous quality improvement
- Cultural competency in community-based evaluation

**Course 4: Capstone: Implementation Science in Action (3 credits)**
- Team-based capstone project: Full implementation assessment of a community program
- Integration of CFIR, RE-AIM, and RPLICE findings
- Presentation to community stakeholders
- Portfolio development for employment
- Career pathway planning and employer matchmaking

#### 3.2 Pedagogical Approach

The curriculum follows a **progressive complexity model**:
1. **Learn** (Course 1): Theory, frameworks, foundational concepts
2. **Practice** (Course 2): Hands-on with AI tools in controlled settings
3. **Apply** (Course 3): Field work with real community programs
4. **Synthesize** (Course 4): Independent assessment and professional presentation

Each course integrates:
- **Culturally responsive teaching** — community examples reflect student populations
- **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** — multiple means of engagement, representation, and action
- **Work-based learning** — every course includes employer-connected activities
- **AI ethics** — every module addresses responsible AI use, bias detection, and community consent

#### 3.3 RPLICE as the Training Platform

RPLICE (Rapid Participatory Leadership Implementation Composite Evaluation) serves dual roles:

**As a Training Tool:**
- Students learn implementation science by DOING implementation science
- AI-assisted scaffolding reduces cognitive load for novice evaluators
- Built-in rubrics align with CFIR 2.0 and RE-AIM competency domains
- Real-time feedback on assessment quality

**As a Research Instrument:**
- RPLICE captures granular data on student assessment processes
- Pre/post competency measurement using validated instruments
- Process tracing: How do students use AI assistance differently as they gain expertise?
- Comparison: AI-assisted vs. traditional assessment accuracy

### Section 4: Research Design (3 pages)

#### 4.1 Research Questions

1. **RQ1:** Does AI-assisted implementation science training (using RPLICE) produce more accurate implementation assessments among community college students compared to traditional training methods?
2. **RQ2:** How does the progression of AI tool use change as students move from novice to competent implementation science technicians?
3. **RQ3:** What barriers and facilitators affect the adoption of AI implementation science training in community college settings?
4. **RQ4:** Do graduates of the program demonstrate competencies valued by employers in health, social services, and workforce development?

#### 4.2 Methodology

**Design:** Mixed-methods quasi-experimental with embedded process evaluation

**Quantitative Strand:**
- Pre/post competency assessments using validated implementation science measures
- RPLICE-generated accuracy scores on implementation assessments
- Learning analytics from the ThriveUp platform (time-on-task, help-seeking behavior, accuracy trajectories)
- Employment outcomes at 6 and 12 months post-completion

**Qualitative Strand:**
- Semi-structured interviews with students (n=20) at program entry, midpoint, and completion
- Focus groups with employer partners (n=3 groups) at 6 and 12 months
- Observation field notes from Course 3 and Course 4 field placements
- Document analysis of student capstone portfolios

**Implementation Evaluation (using CFIR 2.0 + RE-AIM on the PROGRAM ITSELF):**
- The meta-design: We use implementation science to evaluate the implementation of an implementation science training program
- CFIR 2.0 constructs assessed: Innovation characteristics, Inner Setting (ACC institutional factors), Outer Setting (employer market, community needs), Individuals (student characteristics, instructor readiness), Process (curriculum delivery fidelity)
- RE-AIM dimensions: Reach (who enrolls), Effectiveness (learning outcomes), Adoption (ACC institutional commitment), Implementation (delivery fidelity), Maintenance (sustainability after grant period)

#### 4.3 Data Analysis Plan

| Data Source | Analysis Method | Timeline |
|------------|----------------|----------|
| Pre/post competency scores | Paired t-tests, effect sizes | Each semester |
| RPLICE accuracy scores | Growth curve modeling | Continuous |
| Learning analytics | Sequential pattern mining | Continuous |
| Student interviews | Thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke) | Semesters 2, 3, 4 |
| Employer focus groups | Framework analysis (CFIR-mapped) | Months 12, 24 |
| Employment outcomes | Descriptive statistics, logistic regression | Months 18, 30 |
| Capstone portfolios | Rubric-based scoring, inter-rater reliability | Each cohort |

#### 4.4 Human Subjects

All research activities will be submitted for IRB approval through ACC and any required TCAF institutional agreements. Student participation in research components is voluntary and separate from course enrollment. Community program data accessed through ThriveUp will be de-identified.

### Section 5: Broader Impacts and Broadening Participation (2 pages)

#### 5.1 Who This Serves

The program specifically targets populations underrepresented in STEM technician careers:

**Veterans:** ACC serves a significant veteran population. ThriveUp's M2C (Military-to-Civilian) platform provides culturally competent transition support. Veterans bring leadership, mission focus, and operational discipline to implementation science — skills that translate directly to program evaluation.

**Returning Citizens:** Through TCAF's criminal justice platforms, students from formerly incarcerated backgrounds gain access to a career path that leverages their community knowledge. No other ATE program in the country offers this pathway.

**First-Generation Students:** 66% of ACC students are first-generation. The stackable credential model (certificate → Associate's → employment) provides multiple on-ramps and off-ramps, reducing the all-or-nothing pressure of traditional degree programs.

**Hispanic/Latino Students:** ACC's 44% Hispanic/Latino enrollment, combined with ThriveUp's Speech Bridge dialect recognition and bilingual capabilities, ensures culturally and linguistically responsive training.

#### 5.2 Economic Impact

AI implementation science technicians fill a critical workforce gap:
- Local health departments need evaluators but can't afford PhD-level staff
- Community-based organizations are increasingly required to demonstrate evidence-based outcomes for funding
- The growing emphasis on evidence-based policy (ESSA, WIOA, Maternal and Child Health Block Grants) creates demand for implementation-literate technicians

**Salary projections:** $45,000-$65,000 for entry-level positions in program evaluation, health informatics, quality improvement, and data analytics roles.

#### 5.3 Replication and Scale

The curriculum is designed for portability:
- All courses use OER materials
- RPLICE platform access will be available to partner institutions
- The CFIR 2.0 and RE-AIM frameworks are publicly available
- Curriculum mapping documents will be published for community colleges to adapt
- Two additional community college sites will be recruited for Year 3 adoption testing

### Why ATE and Not Another NSF Program:
- ATE is the ONLY NSF program that requires a two-year institution as lead — and you HAVE that partner
- ATE values industry partnerships and workforce placement — your ecosystem IS the industry
- ATE funds up to $600K for Track 1 (new program development) — significant investment

1. **The community infrastructure angle** — No other ATE proposal can claim their training platform is connected to 24 real-world service delivery platforms

3. **The meta-evaluation design** — Using implementation science to evaluate an implementation science program is elegant and will appeal to reviewers
4. **RPLICE as both tool and research instrument** — Dual-use efficiency that maximizes impact per dollar

Every section of this proposal is written through implementation science frameworks:
- **CFIR 2.0** structures the research evaluation (5 domains, 39 constructs)
- **RE-AIM** structures the program evaluation (5 dimensions)
- **RPLICE** is the composite instrument that operationalizes both
- The proposal models what it teaches: if you can't implement your own implementation science training with fidelity, you have no business teaching it
