## 2. FY26 program focus (verbatim from official page)

> "**This year the program will support community-based efforts that strengthen economic mobility and expand opportunities for immigrants and refugees to thrive.**"

FY26 focus narrows to a single priority: **immigrant and refugee inclusion**. (Prior FY25 had three: immigrant inclusion, solidarity with unhoused people, anti-displacement.) This is *unusually focused* — every reviewer is reading proposals through the immigrant/refugee lens only.

## 3. Reviewer scoring posture (from official program page + FY25 Austin Monitor coverage)

Three direct funder quotes that act as the de-facto rubric:

1. *"The ideal applicant plans to or currently works to improve disparities and incorporates **lived experiences of people most impacted at the center of every phase of the work**."*
2. *"Strong applicants include organizations and **grassroots groups working together on the same issues**."*
3. *"If a project is working to **build coalitions in partnership with other organizations** addressing the same issue, it would be an **even stronger candidate** for a mini grant award."*

**Implication:** Coalition framing is not a tiebreaker, it is *explicitly bonus-scoring*. A proposal naming and committing to a coalition of partner organizations beats a solo proposal of equal merit.

## 4. Eligibility & priority signals

- **Special priority** to grassroots efforts, often volunteer-run, with annual operating budget **≤ $500K**. Larger orgs *will* still be considered (no hard ceiling).
- **501(c)(3) status not required** (fiscal sponsor option allowed). Vendor registration with City of Austin required before disbursement.
- One submission per applicant org.
- Service area: **Austin–Travis County and the City's ETJ**.
- "Immigrant" defined inclusively: refugee, asylee, undocumented, parolee, etc.

## 5. Post-award commitments (worth disclosing in narrative)

Awardees commit to:
1. Equity Office sponsored trainings
2. Hosting a site visit + participation in **Equity Action Team meetings**
3. Final report (written or video) covering results, reflections, impact, challenges, and stories

## 6. Prior winners — what is and is not public

The City does **not** publish a single indexed list of past Equity Mini Grant recipients. The "Past Equity Mini Grant Initiatives & Mini Grant Fund Recipients" section on the official grant programs page lists project highlights, not a comprehensive awardee table. To get the full FY24 / FY25 awardee list:

- Email equity@austintexas.gov requesting the FY24 + FY25 awardee list (open-records-friendly request)
- Cross-reference Austin City Council budget transparency reports on the open data portal: `data.austintexas.gov`

## 7. Inferred winning patterns (from program page narrative + FY focus history)

| Pattern | Evidence | Implication for TCAF |
|---|---|---|
| Grassroots org / volunteer-led / ≤$500K budget | "Special priority" language, repeated 3x on program page | Lead with the coalition framing — TCAF anchors the platform; partner CBOs supply the grassroots |
| Co-creation with directly impacted communities | "Co-create their proposals with the directly impacted communities they seek to serve" | Show co-design (Voices of Austin recordings, Community Advisory Group, paid translators, paid Deaf testers) |
| Lived experience at the center | Repeated 2x | Quote in narrative §11 verbatim |
| Coalition of multiple orgs on same issue | "Even stronger candidate" — explicit bonus | Name all 11 partner CBOs (already done in §6) |
| Holistic, not single-issue | "Address disparities holistically … not single-issue projects" | Frame as language + legal + housing + behavioral health + economic mobility, not just translation |
| Past supported areas | COVID-19 recovery, public safety, homelessness, immigrant inclusion | Immigrant inclusion is a returning category — there is institutional muscle memory at the Equity Office for this |
