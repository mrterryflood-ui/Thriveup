## LOI NARRATIVE (1–2 Pages)

### Organizational Background and Capacity

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation (TCAF) is a veteran-founded, Black-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit headquartered in Pflugerville, Texas. TCAF operates a 24-platform technology ecosystem — ThriveUp Academy — designed to strengthen communities through workforce development, health equity, and social determinants of health navigation. Our leadership reflects the communities we serve: Dr. Terry Flood, a U.S. Army veteran (Bronze Star x2) and Doctor of Healthcare Administration, founded TCAF to build permanent infrastructure for the populations most systems overlook — youth of color, immigrant families, veterans in transition, and formerly incarcerated individuals rebuilding stability.

TCAF's workforce development capacity is anchored by RPLICE (Reach, Program, Legitimacy, Implementation, Community, Evaluation) — a proprietary implementation science engine built on CFIR 2.0 and RE-AIM frameworks that measures whether workforce programs are reaching the right people, delivered with fidelity, and producing equitable outcomes. RPLICE doesn't just evaluate — it drives 30-day continuous improvement cycles through MAP-GAP (Measure, Analyze, Plan — Gap, Action, Progress), ensuring programs improve in real time rather than waiting for annual reports. Our staff expertise spans workforce pipeline design, culturally responsive curriculum development, community health worker training, and data-driven program evaluation.

### Nonclinical Mental Health Provider Experience

TCAF's ecosystem directly supports nonclinical mental health provider roles across multiple platforms. Our Sankofa Health Network includes culturally responsive platforms for maternal health, men's health, cognitive safety, and medication adherence — all designed to be delivered by community health workers, peer support specialists, and health navigators rather than licensed clinicians. Our workforce readiness curriculum, currently live and serving youth in Central Texas through Pflugerville ISD partnership, builds foundational professional skills (communication, teamwork, problem-solving, work ethic, professional presence) that form the baseline competencies nonclinical providers need before specializing in mental health support roles.

Through LifeBridge, our community navigation platform, we train and support individuals in benefits screening, barrier identification, and warm referral — core navigator competencies that translate directly to nonclinical mental health navigation roles. Speech Bridge provides multilingual access so providers can serve communities regardless of language barriers.

### Program Description

TCAF proposes **"Pathways to Purpose: Building Central Texas's Nonclinical Youth Mental Health Workforce"** — a 2-year program that recruits, trains, places, and retains nonclinical youth mental health providers from the communities they will serve.

**What we will do:**

**Attraction and Recruitment:** Using GIS-powered community data from 8 federal sources (CDC PLACES, Social Vulnerability Index, Census ACS, SAMHSA, and more), we identify the specific Central Texas neighborhoods with the highest youth mental health need and the lowest provider access. We recruit from WITHIN those neighborhoods — young adults with lived experience, cultural knowledge, and community trust who already serve as informal mental health supports but lack formal pathways into the field. Pflugerville ISD, Manor ISD, and community organizations serve as recruitment pipelines.

**Development and Training:** Participants complete TCAF's workforce readiness curriculum (20 lessons across 5 modules: Professional Presence, Communication, Teamwork, Problem-Solving, Work Ethic & Leadership) enhanced with a Youth Mental Health First Aid specialization track. Training is delivered through ThriveUp Academy's phone-first, culturally responsive platform featuring characters and scenarios that reflect participants' real lives. RPLICE monitors training fidelity — ensuring every cohort receives the same quality instruction regardless of facilitator.

**Placement and Entry to Practice:** Graduates are placed with youth-serving organizations across Central Texas — school-based health centers, community mental health organizations, after-school programs, and faith-based youth ministries. TCAF's partner network provides supervised entry-to-practice experiences where new providers build confidence under mentorship.

**Upskilling and Career Progression:** TCAF's platform provides continuous professional development — specialized modules in trauma-informed care, crisis de-escalation, cultural humility, and adolescent development. RPLICE tracks competency progression and identifies when providers are ready for advanced roles. Career pathway mapping shows providers exactly how their nonclinical role connects to future opportunities (CHW certification, peer specialist certification, social work education).

**Retention and Sustainability:** Provider burnout is the workforce crisis within the crisis. TCAF addresses retention through: peer cohort communities (providers supporting providers), ongoing mental healthcare access for providers themselves, living wage advocacy with employer partners, and MAP-GAP data showing providers the measurable impact of their work — because seeing that you matter is the strongest retention tool.

**Who we serve:** Young adults (18-30) from historically underserved Central Texas communities, with priority given to individuals with lived experience of mental health challenges, youth of color, bilingual individuals, and those from communities with the highest Social Vulnerability Index scores.

**Pipeline stages addressed:** All five — Attraction/Recruitment, Development/Training, Placement/Entry to Practice, Upskilling/Career Progression, Retention/Sustainability.

### Workforce Pipeline Need

Central Texas faces a severe youth mental health workforce shortage. Travis and Williamson counties are among the fastest-growing regions in the country, but licensed mental health provider-to-youth ratios have not kept pace. The result: wait times of 3-6 months for youth therapy appointments, emergency departments serving as de facto mental health intake, and school counselors carrying caseloads 3x the recommended maximum.

Nonclinical providers — peer specialists, CHWs, navigators, health educators — can fill this gap immediately, but no structured pathway exists in Central Texas to recruit, train, and retain them. Young adults from the most affected communities have the cultural knowledge and lived experience to be effective providers, but without formal training infrastructure, career pathway visibility, and retention support, they either never enter the field or burn out within 18 months.

We know this need exists because our GIS intelligence identifies the exact census tracts where youth mental health indicators (CDC PLACES) are worst and provider density is lowest — and they are consistently in communities of color.

### Youth Focus

Youth mental health is fundamentally different from adult mental health work. Young people communicate differently, build trust differently, and experience stigma differently. Our program specifically addresses this by:
- Training providers in adolescent development, social media's impact on mental health, and school-based engagement strategies
- Recruiting providers who are close in age and cultural experience to the youth they serve — because relatability is the foundation of nonclinical trust
- Integrating youth voice through LifeBridge community input — young people in our target communities define what support looks like for them, and that input directly shapes provider training

### Pipeline Connectivity

**Entry:** Participants enter through referrals from Pflugerville ISD (via Dr. Flood's School Health Advisory Council role), Manor ISD, community organizations, workforce development boards, and direct community outreach in high-need zip codes identified by our GIS intelligence.

**After:** Graduates connect to placement partners (school-based health centers, Integral Care, community organizations), with TCAF providing ongoing professional development, peer cohort support, and career advancement tracking through the ThriveUp Academy platform. The platform remains available to graduates permanently — it's not a program they age out of.

### Equity Strategy

Equity is not an add-on — it is the architecture. Our recruitment targets the specific census tracts with the highest Social Vulnerability Index scores. Our curriculum features characters and scenarios reflecting the cultural realities of Black, Latino, and immigrant youth. Training is delivered phone-first because that's how our communities access education. Speech Bridge provides multilingual access. RPLICE disaggregates every outcome by race, ethnicity, language, and zip code — so we can see and address disparities in real time, not discover them in a final report.

### Youth Voice and Lived Experience Integration

Youth voice enters the program at three points: (1) LifeBridge community input shapes training content — young people define what mental health support they want, and provider training reflects those priorities; (2) participants with lived mental health experience are prioritized in recruitment because their stories become teaching tools and trust-builders; (3) youth advisory input is embedded in curriculum review cycles through MAP-GAP — every 30 days, we ask young people: "Is this working for you?" and adjust.
