## 2. What happens without intervention (the counterfactual evidence)

**Source: *Midwest Evaluation of the Adult Functioning of Former Foster Youth* (Courtney, Dworsky et al., Chapin Hall at the University of Chicago). The gold-standard longitudinal cohort study tracking ~750 youth aging out of foster care in IL, IA, and WI from age 17 through age 26+. Multiple waves; results cited below are from Wave 5 (age 26).**

### By age 26, former foster youth (vs. national peers age 26):

| Outcome | Former foster youth | National peers (age 26) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Homelessness or couch-surfed since leaving care** | **36%** | ~4% | Midwest Study, Wave 5 |
| **Convicted of a crime** (males) | **~60%** | ~17% | Midwest Study, Wave 5 |
| **HS diploma or GED by age 19** | **~50%** | **87%** | Midwest Study, Wave 4 |
| **4-year college degree by age 26** | **~6–8%** | **~36%** | Midwest Study, Wave 5 |
| **Pregnant by age 21** (females) | **71%** | ~34% | Midwest Study, Wave 4 |
| **Unemployed at age 24** | **~52%** | ~24% | Midwest Study, Wave 4 |
| **Earnings (annual median, age 26)** | **~$8,000** | ~$22,000 | Midwest Study, Wave 5 |
| **Health insurance coverage at age 24** | ~50% | ~70% | Midwest Study, Wave 4 |

### Mental health load:
- **PTSD lifetime prevalence: ~25%** among former foster youth — *roughly twice the rate among U.S. war veterans*. Source: Pecora et al., *Improving Family Foster Care: Findings from the Northwest Foster Care Alumni Study* (Casey Family Programs, 2005). PTSD finding replicated in Midwest Study.
- **Major depression past-12-mo: ~20%** vs. ~9% in age-matched national sample. Source: Midwest Study + Pecora et al.
- **Substance use disorder lifetime: ~21%** vs. ~9%. Source: same.

### NYTD (National Youth in Transition Database) — federal outcome data:
The Children's Bureau requires every state to survey 17-, 19-, and 21-year-olds in/exiting care. Most recent published NYTD outcomes (Cohort 2 / 2017 wave at age 21):
- **Past-2-year homelessness: ~21%** (age 19 wave); **~28%** (age 21 wave)
- **Past-2-year incarceration: ~28%**
- **Currently in school: ~36%**, only **~4% with a bachelor's degree**
- **Receiving public food assistance: ~50%+**

Source: Children's Bureau, *Highlights from the NYTD Outcomes Report*, FY2017–2021 cohort tables.

### The "five years out" reality (the cost-of-inaction picture):
Aggregating Midwest + NYTD + Casey Northwest data, by 5 years post-exit, the typical aged-out youth experiences **3 or more** of: homelessness, incarceration, unemployment, untreated MH disorder, no postsecondary credential, parenting without supports. **Co-occurrence is the norm, not the exception.** This is why **single-point interventions fail** and integrated infrastructure matters.

## 3. The economic counterfactual — cost of NOT acting

| Figure | Source / methodology |
|---|---|
| **~$300,000 in lifetime social costs per youth who ages out without supports** | Jim Casey Youth Opportunities Initiative (now part of Annie E. Casey Foundation), *The Cost of Doing Nothing*, 2013 — calculates lost tax revenue + corrections + Medicaid + homelessness services + lost lifetime earnings |
| **$30,000–$50,000/year per chronically homeless young adult** | HUD CoC + USICH cost estimates; varies by metro |
| **$35,000–$60,000/year per incarcerated young adult** | Vera Institute of Justice, *Price of Prisons* (2017, updated annually) — TX state cost ~$22,000/year, federal ~$40,000/year |
| **Extended Foster Care to 21 ROI: ~$2.40 returned per $1 invested** | Chapin Hall, *Cost of Extending Foster Care to Age 21*, Courtney & Dworsky — increases in earnings + reduced corrections + reduced Medicaid utilization |
| **Each additional year a young person remains in foster care after age 18 is associated with a doubling of the odds of postsecondary enrollment** | Courtney et al., Midwest Study Wave 4 |
| **Stable housing alone reduces ED utilization by ~30% in this population** | Multiple Housing First evaluations applied to TAY (transition-age youth) cohorts |

**Implication for funders:** Every aged-out youth diverted from homelessness or incarceration in their first 5 years post-exit pays back the cost of the intervention multiple times. Chafee, ETV, FYI, and Continuum of Care youth set-asides exist precisely because this math is well-established.

## 4. The evidence base for our approach (why what TCAF builds is "evidence-based," not "well-intentioned")

### Warm handoff + peer-navigator + CHW dispatch (LifeBridge approach):
- **SOAR (SSI/SSDI Outreach, Access, and Recovery) model** — SAMHSA-validated; same warm-navigation logic for benefits access. National outcomes: 65%+ approval rate on SSI applications vs. 10–15% baseline. Source: SAMHSA SOAR TA Center.
- **Chafee-funded peer mentor evaluations** — Multiple state evaluations show peer-navigator models reduce homelessness post-exit by 30–40%. Source: HHS/ACF, *Chafee Foster Care Program: Improving Outcomes for Older Youth* (multiple state evaluations).
- **Community Health Worker (CHW) evidence base** — HRSA + AHRQ evidence reviews show CHW-dispatched SDOH navigation produces meaningful reductions in ED utilization, missed appointments, and uncontrolled chronic conditions. Source: AHRQ Evidence-Based Practice Center reports on CHW interventions.

### Trauma-informed crisis routing (Whole-Person Mental Health approach):
- **SAMHSA TIP 57** — *Trauma-Informed Care in Behavioral Health Services* — establishes the evidence base for trauma-informed care as standard of practice for populations with high ACE exposure (foster youth average 4+ ACEs vs. 1.4 in general population per CDC-Kaiser ACE Study).
- **NCTSN (National Child Traumatic Stress Network)** — evidence-based protocols for foster-experienced youth, including TF-CBT (Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy) and ARC (Attachment, Regulation, Competency).
- **C-SSRS, PCL-5, PHQ-9, GAD-7, EPDS** — all validated screening instruments embedded in our crisis routing pathway.

### Multilingual access (Talk Your Talk approach):
- **ACA Section 1557** — federal civil-rights requirement for meaningful language access in federally-funded health programs. ~25% of foster youth come from non-English-primary households.
- **OMH (HHS Office of Minority Health) National CLAS Standards** — Culturally and Linguistically Appropriate Services standards establish language access as a quality-of-care issue, not an add-on.

### Independent-student FAFSA + ETV pathway (ThriveUp Academy approach):
- **Foster Care to Success** national data: foster youth who use ETV are **2x more likely** to complete a postsecondary credential than peers who don't. Source: FC2S program-evaluation data.
- **John H. Chafee Education and Training Voucher Program** — federal; provides up to $5,000/year per former foster youth for postsecondary education up to age 26 (extended from 23 by Family First Prevention Services Act).

## 6. The system gap — why this funding doesn't translate into outcomes

Every published evaluation of Chafee/ETV/FYI implementation surfaces the same operational gaps:

1. **Caseworker bandwidth** — average TX DFPS caseload exceeds recommended Child Welfare League of America (CWLA) standard. The 1:1 case-management model breaks at age-out.
2. **Warm handoff failure** — youth lose their caseworker the day they age out; >40% of NYTD respondents at age 19 cannot identify a single adult they can call in a crisis. Source: NYTD outcomes tables.
3. **Eligibility-but-not-enrollment** — most aged-out youth qualify for SNAP, Medicaid (to 26 under ACA §2004), ETV, FAFSA independent-student status, FYI vouchers, RHYA Transitional Living. **Most don't enroll.** Source: GAO-19-313, *Foster Care: Additional Actions Could Help HHS Better Support States' Use of Private Providers to Recruit and Retain Foster Families* (parallel finding on benefits utilization).
4. **Language access failure** — rising share of foster youth are from LEP households; existing transition-services materials are mostly English-only.
5. **Geographic gap** — rural youth aging out have fewer providers in driving distance; urban youth face longer waitlists. Both lose to navigation failure.
6. **The data gap** — most state ILP programs report inputs (services delivered) rather than outcomes (youth status at 18, 19, 21, 26). NYTD is the only consistent outcome lens, and it's incomplete.

**Every one of these six gaps is a feature TCAF's infrastructure addresses by design** — not as a future plan, as built capability. See `Foster-Youth-Transition-Briefing.md` §"How TCAF closes each system gap."
