## 1. How is your work addressing disability justice, tech, and inclusion from an intersectional social justice lens?

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation (TCAF) operates a 24-platform AI-powered ecosystem that directly centers disability justice within broader social justice infrastructure. Three platforms specifically serve disabled communities:

**Perfectly Different** -- A neurodiversity-affirming education and workforce platform providing IEP/504 accommodation support, sensory-friendly interfaces, neurodivergent learning pathways, and individualized transition planning. Built with and for neurodivergent youth and their families, Perfectly Different rejects deficit-based models and instead positions neurodivergent learners as capable professionals deserving of technology designed for their strengths.

**LexiBridge (Speech Bridge)** -- An assistive communication technology platform serving individuals with speech and communication disabilities. LexiBridge provides augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) tools, speech-to-text interfaces, and communication support that ensures disabled individuals can fully participate in workforce development, healthcare navigation, and community engagement.

**SafeCogniCare** -- A cognitive safety and assessment platform serving individuals with cognitive disabilities, traumatic brain injuries, and neurodegenerative conditions. SafeCogniCare provides validated cognitive assessments, safety planning, and care coordination that respects self-determination while ensuring appropriate support.

Our intersectional lens is structural, not performative. These disability-focused platforms operate within an ecosystem that simultaneously addresses racial health disparities (Sankofa Health Network serving Black communities), economic exclusion (LifeBridge reentry services), and educational inequity (ISSS youth support). A disabled Black woman navigating maternal health, a neurodivergent veteran transitioning to civilian workforce, a justice-involved person with cognitive disabilities seeking reentry support -- these are not edge cases in our system. They are the people our architecture was built to serve.

Every platform in our ecosystem is built as an offline-capable Progressive Web App (PWA), ensuring access in connectivity-limited environments that disproportionately affect disabled communities, rural residents, and low-income populations. Our phone-first design reflects the reality that many disabled individuals rely on mobile devices as their primary computing platform.

## 2. How will you use grant funds to build your organization's infrastructure and operating capacity?

Grant funds will directly strengthen three areas:

**Accessibility Infrastructure ($40,000):** Expand assistive technology capabilities across all 24 platforms -- screen reader optimization, keyboard navigation improvements, WCAG 2.1 AA compliance audits, cognitive load reduction in user interfaces, and alternative format content delivery. This investment benefits every platform user, not just those on disability-specific platforms, embedding universal design principles into the ecosystem's core.

**Disability Community Leadership ($35,000):** Fund a Disability Justice Advisory Council composed of disabled community members who will guide platform development priorities, review accessibility of new features, and ensure self-determination principles are embedded in technology design. Stipends for council members recognize that disabled expertise has economic value. This council will also inform cross-movement coalition building with TCAF's existing health equity, workforce development, and education partners.

**Digital Divide Bridging ($25,000):** Develop and deploy assistive technology lending library, provide device access support, and expand offline platform capabilities to reach disabled individuals in institutional settings (group homes, care facilities, correctional institutions) where internet access is limited or controlled.

## 4. How do you measure impact and success?

TCAF's ecosystem generates real-time outcome data. For this initiative, we will track:

**Platform Accessibility Metrics:**
- WCAG 2.1 AA compliance score across all platforms (baseline and progress)
- Assistive technology compatibility rate (screen readers, switch access, voice control)
- Time-to-task completion for users with and without disabilities (equity gap measurement)
- Offline mode utilization rates in connectivity-limited environments

**Community Engagement Metrics:**
- Disability Justice Advisory Council participation and recommendation implementation rate
- Number of disabled individuals engaged across platforms (disaggregated by disability type, race, age)
- Cross-movement coalition partnerships formed
- Assistive technology lending library utilization

**Programmatic Outcomes:**
- Neurodivergent learners completing workforce readiness modules (Perfectly Different)
- LexiBridge communication session completions and user-reported satisfaction
- SafeCogniCare cognitive assessment completions with care plan follow-through
- IEP/504 accommodation compliance rate through ISSS integration
- Employment or education placement rates for disabled program participants

**Systemic Change Indicators:**
- Open-source accessibility patterns published and adopted by other organizations
- Policy recommendations developed by Advisory Council
- Community report reach and citations
