## The Problem We're Solving Together

**192,029 people** in our 5-county region qualify for public benefits they are not receiving.

That's a **60% enrollment gap** — and it represents **$192 million** in annual SNAP, Medicaid, CHIP, EITC, and WIC benefits going unclaimed every year.

**Why the gap persists:**
- 20+ page HHSC application in English only
- Mixed-status families avoid ALL government systems
- No broadband in rural Bastrop and Caldwell
- No local enrollment infrastructure in growing communities like Pflugerville, Hutto, Manor
- System distrust — earned over decades, not overcome by websites

**No single organization can close this gap alone.**

## What St. David's Learned from We All Benefit 1.0

St. David's funded 16 organizations in WAB 1.0. After 18 months, their insights (published March 26, 2026) directly shaped WAB 2.0:

**Insight 1: 27,000 enrolled despite policy chaos**
Federal changes reduced Medicaid/CHIP eligibility. Premium Tax Credits expired. Navigation funding was cut. Yet trained navigators still got 27,000 people enrolled. The work is "more essential and challenging" than ever.

**Insight 2: No organization can maintain expertise alone**
Policy changes are too fast, too complex. Organizations need peer networks, real-time support, and connections to state agencies. The burden must be shared.

**Insight 3: Trust beats everything**
"Deep trust-building, human connection, and patience — often built over years." Culturally responsive. Community members making informed decisions for their families. And St. David's acknowledged: this trust work "requires an increasingly significant amount of time and effort which must be accounted for in funding and staffing expectations."

**What WAB 2.0 added based on these insights:**
- Expanded scope (SNAP, WIC, EITC — not just health insurance)
- Expertise AND Trust required together
- 3-year duration ("building effective organizational infrastructure takes time")

## How It Works in Practice

**Scenario 1: Pflugerville Library**
A mother brings her children to story time at the Pflugerville Library. A bilingual CHW at the library asks if she'd like a free benefits check. In 3 minutes, the screener identifies the family as eligible for SNAP ($3,024/year), Medicaid for the children ($7,200/year), and EITC ($3,584/year). The CHW helps complete the SNAP application on site. Total new benefits: $13,808/year for one family.

**Scenario 2: ACC Campus**
A student tells Professor Franco she's skipping meals to afford textbooks. Franco connects her to the TCAF screener through student services. The student qualifies for SNAP and a childcare subsidy — keeping her enrolled in her nursing program instead of dropping out.

**Scenario 3: Frost Bank Branch**
Frost Bank hosts a financial wellness workshop. A TCAF CHW screens attendees for EITC and Child Tax Credit. Twelve families discover they're eligible for an average of $3,584 in EITC they've never claimed. Six are also eligible for SNAP.

**Scenario 4: Manor Community Center**
A mixed-status family attends a community health fair. They're afraid of government systems. A CHW — a neighbor they know — explains that their U.S. citizen children qualify for Medicaid and CHIP without affecting the parents' immigration status. The family decides to enroll the children. Trust, not pressure.

**Scenario 5: Dads Care 2 Fatherhood Program**
A father in Eric Hargrave's Dads Care 2 program is rebuilding after incarceration. He's focused on employment and co-parenting — he doesn't know his children qualify for Medicaid and CHIP, or that he's eligible for SNAP and EITC. During a Dads Care 2 session, a TCAF screener identifies $9,608/year in benefits for his family. The CHW helps him apply on site. Stronger father, more stable family.

## Sustainability: What Happens After Year 3

This is NOT a program that disappears when funding ends.

1. **HHSC CPP certification** makes our enrollment infrastructure part of Texas's permanent benefits system
2. **The technology platform persists** — AI screening, enrollment tracking, renewal alerts continue functioning at minimal cost
3. **The data compounds** — 3 years of enrollment data creates deep knowledge of who, where, and what works
4. **The coalition grows** — new partners join as results prove the model, each bringing their own resources
5. **Partner organizations keep their community relationships** — the trust stays local
