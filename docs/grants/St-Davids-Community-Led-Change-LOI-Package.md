## 1. UNDERSTANDING THE OPPORTUNITY

### What St. David's Wants

From the funding page: *"Investments to support communities with the greatest health needs to define their own health priorities and influence the practices, policies, and systems that support them."*

**Goal:** Community-Driven Change
**Funding Priorities:** Community Voice + Decision Making, Civic Health

This is NOT a traditional health services grant. This is about **community power** — putting tools, data, and decision-making authority in the hands of the people most affected by health inequities.

### What Won in 2024

- **23 grants awarded, $9.1 million total**
- **91% of awardees were led by people of color**
- **300+ LOIs submitted → 114 invited to full application → 23 funded**
- Grants were **unrestricted, one-time**
- Eligibility required: 501(c)(3), annual budget ≥$250,000, evidence of community engagement in decision-making
- Collaboratives could receive **up to $1,000,000** vs $250,000 for individual orgs

### Why TCAF Wins This

TCAF doesn't deliver services TO communities — TCAF builds technology that puts communities IN CHARGE of their own health priorities. The 24-platform ecosystem is the infrastructure that makes community-led change measurable, sustainable, and scalable.

Most organizations serve communities. TCAF **equips** communities:

**The Collaborative Advocate Foundation — Catalyzing Community-Led Change LOI**

In Pflugerville, a neighborhood with rising diabetes rates has no say in which health programs come to their zip code. In Del Valle, residents who could reshape local health policy don't have access to the data that proves what they already know — that their community is underserved. In Bastrop, a community health worker with decades of trust has no technology to track whether her referrals actually result in better outcomes.

Across Central Texas, communities with the greatest health needs have the least power to define their own health priorities. The data exists. The community knowledge exists. What's missing is the infrastructure that connects them.

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation (TCAF), a Black-led, veteran-founded 501(c)(3) headquartered in Pflugerville, proposes to build that infrastructure — a community-owned health intelligence system that puts neighborhood-level data, decision-making tools, and outcome tracking directly in the hands of the people and organizations closest to the problems.

**Community voice, not outside expertise:** TCAF's LifeBridge platform captures what community members say matters most — not what funders or researchers assume matters. Residents in each zip code define their health priorities. Those priorities drive which platforms activate, which data gets tracked, and which outcomes get measured. The community leads. The technology follows.

**Data that belongs to the neighborhood:** Through Neighborhood Intelligence, TCAF transforms 8 federal data sources — CDC PLACES, Social Vulnerability Index, Census ACS, USDA Food Atlas, and more — into compassionate, understandable community profiles at the census tract level. For the first time, a neighborhood association or church health ministry can see their own community's health story told in data they can use, not academic reports they can't access.

**Health tools designed by and for communities of color:** The Sankofa Health Network — 7 culturally responsive platforms covering maternal health, men's health, chronic disease, medication adherence, and cognitive safety — was built with the communities it serves. Speech Bridge ensures language is never a barrier. Every tool is mobile-first, because that's how these communities actually access health information.

**Community accountability, not annual reports:** RPLICE (TCAF's implementation science engine) monitors whether programs are reaching who they're supposed to reach and producing the outcomes the community defined. MAP-GAP drives 30-day improvement cycles — the community sees what's working and what isn't in real time, not 12 months later in a grant report.

**Collaborative structure:** TCAF serves as the technology backbone for a collaborative of POC-led community organizations across Williamson, Travis, and Bastrop counties. Each partner brings trusted community relationships and cultural authority. TCAF provides the data infrastructure, health platforms, and evaluation tools that make their community-led work visible, measurable, and sustainable. Each partner has a specific, complementary role — this is a connected team, not a list.

**Sustainability:** Technology infrastructure is permanent. Community data gets richer each cycle. Partner capacity compounds. When the grant period ends, the communities have tools and data that belong to them — not a program that disappears.

TCAF is ready to attend office hours, demonstrate the platform, and build alongside Central Texas's most powerful asset: the communities themselves.
