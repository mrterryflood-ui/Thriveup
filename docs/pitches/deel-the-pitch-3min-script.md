## THE SCRIPT

*(Walk to center stage. Pause. Make eye contact.)*

I spent 15 years in the U.S. Army. After that, I spent another decade in criminal justice, behavioral health, and workforce development — working directly with people the system forgot. Returning citizens. Single mothers. Veterans. Youth aging out of foster care.

And every single time, I watched the same thing happen.

A young man comes home from prison. He needs a job. He needs housing. He needs mental health support. He needs to reconnect with his kids. And we hand him eight different apps, four different case managers, and a stack of paperwork — and then we wonder why he ends up back inside.

*(Pause.)*

The United States spends **180 billion dollars a year** on social services. And most of it is lost — not to fraud, not to waste — but to **fragmentation**. Every community organization, every school district, every workforce board builds its own silo. Nobody talks to each other. The people who need help the most fall through the cracks between systems that were never designed to connect.

I didn't wait for someone to fix this. I built the fix.

**ThriveUp** is a 24-platform AI-powered ecosystem. Workforce readiness. Behavioral health screening. Crisis intervention. K-12 and adult education. Business development. Emergency management. Community resource navigation. All of it — connected through one integrated architecture.

Not 24 separate products duct-taped together. One ecosystem. Built on implementation science. Bilingual — English and Spanish. Fully accessible. And every platform is live right now. Not a prototype. Not a roadmap. Deployed and serving real people in Central Texas today.

*(Slight lean forward.)*

Here's the business: **SaaS licensing.** Community organizations, school districts, workforce development boards, and government agencies pay monthly or annual licenses to deploy ThriveUp. One customer gets workforce readiness. Another gets the full ecosystem. They pay based on what they need.

The market is enormous. There are **40,000 community action agencies** in the United States. **13,000 school districts.** **550 workforce development boards.** Every single one of them is using disconnected tools right now. We replace that fragmentation with one platform.

Our first customer is already live — a 501(c)(3) deploying the full ecosystem across Central Texas. We have an active grant pipeline — **$1.6 million** with the Texas Workforce Commission, plus applications to Google.org, the Gates Foundation, and NSF.

*(Stand straight. Direct.)*

I'm asking for **one million dollars** to take ThriveUp from Central Texas to five additional markets in twelve months. Hire a sales and customer success team. Reach **two million in annual recurring revenue.**

I have a DHA, a DBA, five master's degrees, Implementation Science training from Dartmouth, and 25 years of direct service. I didn't build ThriveUp because I thought it would be a good business. I built it because the communities I serve were dying without it.

*(Final pause. Eye contact.)*

The infrastructure to transform how America serves its most vulnerable communities — it exists. It's live. It works. And now it needs to scale.

I'm Dr. Terry Flood. This is ThriveUp. And we're ready.

*(Nod. Hold.)*

## COACHING NOTES FOR DELIVERY

1. **Pace yourself.** You have 2:50 written — that gives you 10 seconds of breathing room. Don't rush.
2. **The prison story is your hook.** Don't skip it. Don't shorten it. That's what makes the judges lean in.
3. **"I didn't wait for someone to fix this. I built the fix."** — This is your transition line. Hit it hard.
4. **Numbers land better when you slow down.** When you say "180 billion" or "40,000 agencies" — pause slightly before the number.
5. **The close is about YOU, not the product.** "I built it because the communities I serve were dying without it" — that's what separates you from every other pitch they hear that day.
6. **Don't over-explain the tech.** Judges don't need to know the architecture. They need to know it works and it's live.
7. **"It exists. It's live. It works. And now it needs to scale."** — Three short sentences. Let each one land.

## Q&A PREP (Common VC/Judge Questions)

**Q: Why hasn't someone built this before?**
A: They have — in pieces. Salesforce built CRM. Workday built HR. Canvas built LMS. But nobody built the connective tissue for community services. That's because the people who build software don't serve these communities, and the people who serve these communities don't build software. I do both.

**Q: What's your competitive moat?**
A: Integration depth. Anyone can build one platform. We built 24 that talk to each other. A competitor would need to replicate workforce, behavioral health, education, crisis intervention, business development, emergency management, and community navigation — and make them all interoperable. That's years of work.

**Q: How do you acquire customers?**
A: Two channels. First, grants — workforce boards and school districts use federal and state funding to purchase technology. We align to those funding streams (WIOA, ESSA, SAMHSA). Second, direct sales — community organizations that need to consolidate their tech stack.

**Q: What's your burn rate / runway?**
A: We're lean. The platform is built. Core team is in place. This investment extends runway to 18+ months and funds the go-to-market expansion.

**Q: Can this work outside the US?**
A: The architecture is geography-agnostic. Bilingual already (English/Spanish). Community services fragmentation is a global problem — every country with social safety net programs faces the same disconnect.
