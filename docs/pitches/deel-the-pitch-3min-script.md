## THE 2-MINUTE SCRIPT

**ESTIMATED DELIVERY TIME: 1 minute 55 seconds at natural speaking pace**

*(Walk to center stage. Pause. Make eye contact.)*

**[SLIDE 1 — TITLE visible behind you]**

I'm Dr. Terry Flood. Army veteran. Five master's degrees. A doctorate in healthcare administration and a doctorate in business. The United States spends **180 billion dollars a year** on social services. And most of it is lost — not to fraud — but to **fragmentation**. A veteran leaving the military needs job placement, mental health support, benefits navigation, and family services — and we hand them four different agencies with four disconnected systems. A pregnant Black woman in a maternal health desert needs one connected care pathway — instead she gets referral loops and dropped calls.

**[SLIDE 3 — THE SOLUTION]**

I didn't wait for someone to fix this. I built the fix.

**ThriveUp** is a 24-platform AI-powered ecosystem — and every platform is live right now.

**[SLIDE 4 — PLATFORM HIGHLIGHTS]**

Let me show you three.

The business is simple: **SaaS licensing.** Community organizations, school districts, workforce boards, and government agencies pay monthly or annual licenses. One customer deploys workforce readiness. Another deploys the full ecosystem.

**[SLIDE 7 — TRACTION]**

Our first customer is live — a 501(c)(3) deploying across Central Texas. Active pipeline: **$1.6 million** with the Texas Workforce Commission. Applications in with Google.org, the Gates Foundation, and NSF.

**[SLIDE 8 — THE ASK]**

I'm asking for **one million dollars** to scale from Central Texas to five markets in twelve months. Hire sales and customer success. Reach **two million in ARR.**

**[SLIDE 9 — FOUNDER / WHY ME]**

*(Direct eye contact.)*

I didn't build ThriveUp because I thought it would be a good business. I built it because the communities I serve were dying without it.

It exists. It's live. It works. Now it needs to scale.

I'm Dr. Terry Flood. This is ThriveUp. We're ready.

## WHY THESE 3 PLATFORMS

The three spotlights were chosen strategically:

1. **Mission Transition (Veterans)** — Deel's judges include J.P. Morgan and major VCs. Veteran-founded companies resonate strongly with institutional investors. This also directly connects to YOUR story as the founder.

2. **Black Maternal Health Network** — This is a national crisis with massive media attention. Black maternal mortality is 3x the rate. When you say this on stage, the room goes quiet. It humanizes the technology immediately.

3. **Sankofa Health Network (Breast Cancer/Health Equity)** — Breast cancer screening navigation for underserved women is one of the most underfunded, most impactful applications of health technology. It shows the ecosystem's breadth — you're not just workforce, you're the whole person.

Together, these three platforms prove: this isn't a single-use tool. It's an ecosystem that serves veterans, mothers, cancer patients, returning citizens, students — the full spectrum of human need.

## COACHING NOTES FOR 2-MINUTE FORMAT

1. **You have ZERO room for filler.** Every word in this script earns its place. Practice until you can deliver it without looking at notes.
2. **Let the slides do visual work.** Don't describe what's on screen — narrate what it MEANS. The screenshots speak for themselves.
3. **Three platform highlights = three emotional beats.** Veterans (duty). Mothers (urgency). Cancer patients (compassion). Each hits differently.
4. **"I didn't wait for someone to fix this. I built the fix."** — This is still your pivot line. Hit it and pause ONE beat before continuing.
5. **The close is 10 seconds.** Don't rush it. The last thing they hear is: "We're ready." Let that sit.
6. **Practice with a timer.** If you're at 1:50 in rehearsal, you're perfect. If you're at 2:05, cut one adjective from each section.

## Q&A PREP (Post-Pitch Judge Questions)

**Q: Why hasn't someone built this before?**
A: They have — in pieces. Salesforce built CRM. Workday built HR. Canvas built LMS. Nobody built the connective tissue for community services. That's because the people who build software don't serve these communities, and the people who serve these communities don't build software. I do both.

**Q: What's your competitive moat?**
A: Integration depth. Anyone can build one platform. We built 24 that talk to each other. A competitor would need years to replicate the interoperability across workforce, health, education, crisis, and business development.

**Q: How do you acquire customers?**
A: Two channels. Grants — workforce boards and school districts use federal funding (WIOA, ESSA, SAMHSA) to purchase technology. Direct sales — community organizations consolidating their tech stack.

**Q: Show me a platform demo?**
A: *(Pull up the live site on your phone or have a backup laptop ready.)* Every platform is live at its own domain right now. I can show you any of the 24.

**Q: What's your burn rate?**
A: We're lean. Platform is built. Core team is in place. This investment extends runway 18+ months and funds go-to-market.

**Q: Can this work internationally?**
A: The architecture is geography-agnostic. Already bilingual. Community services fragmentation is a global problem — every country with social safety nets faces the same disconnect.

**Q: Why should we invest in a social impact company?**
A: Because this isn't charity — it's infrastructure. Every school district in America buys software. Every workforce board buys software. We're selling to the same buyers Salesforce sells to, solving a problem nobody else has addressed. The social impact is the market advantage, not a limitation.
