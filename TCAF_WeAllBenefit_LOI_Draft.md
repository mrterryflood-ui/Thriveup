# TCAF — We All Benefit 2.0 Letter of Intent
## Draft for St. David's Foundation · Due April 27, 2026

---

Every day, eligible families in Central Texas leave money on the table. A single mother in Williamson County qualifies for $287 monthly in SNAP benefits but doesn't know it. A retired couple in Hays County could reduce their Medicaid costs by $4,800 annually but never completed the application. Across the five-county region, 862,612 people meet the criteria for at least one public benefit program yet remain unenrolled. That represents $4.1 billion in unclaimed assistance annually—money that could pay for food, medicine, rent, and stability for families already stretched thin.

The Collaborative Advocate Foundation exists to bridge this gap. We are a veteran-founded, Black-led nonprofit born from the conviction that access to benefits should not depend on luck, geography, or navigational privilege. We have spent two years building what most communities lack: a shared technology infrastructure that identifies eligible families, screens them quickly and accurately, and connects them to trusted local organizations that complete enrollment and manage renewals.

Our approach solves a real coordination problem. Today, community organizations—churches, food banks, workforce centers, health clinics—meet eligible families every single day. But without shared screening tools and data visibility, many referrals fall through. A navigator in Austin may duplicate a screening already done in Pflugerville. A family may miss a renewal deadline because no system tracks it across multiple programs. We eliminate that friction through a centralized Benefits Intelligence System that equips local partners with the tools they need while respecting confidentiality and trust.

The system works like this: Our census tract analysis identifies neighborhoods with the highest concentration of eligible, unenrolled residents. A three-minute offline screener—available on tablets, phones, or paper—determines which programs a family qualifies for. We then execute a warm referral to a trusted community navigator: the woman who runs the food pantry, the caseworker at the clinic, the pastor who knows the family. That navigator completes the SNAP, Medicaid, or LIHEAP application with the family present, in person, in their language. Critically, we embed automated renewal alerts so families don't lose benefits when paperwork deadlines arrive.

This work is deeply local. In Williamson County alone, 143,877 eligible people remain unenrolled—our first target. We will deepen partnerships with existing community organizations, train navigators on complex applications, and deploy mobile enrollment into rural areas where transportation to central offices is a genuine barrier. We will prioritize mixed-status families, whose immigration anxiety often silences them from applying even when they qualify. We will invest equally in renewal support, recognizing that retention is enrollment.

We are requesting a three-year partnership with St. David's Foundation to scale this model across the five-county region. We bring the data infrastructure, screening technology, and coalition backbone. Our partner organizations bring trust, geography, and direct access to families. Together, we will move 862,612 people from the gap into enrollment—not through coercion, but through coordination, cultural competency, and partnership with the institutions families already know.

The need is clear. The solution is ready. The families are waiting.
